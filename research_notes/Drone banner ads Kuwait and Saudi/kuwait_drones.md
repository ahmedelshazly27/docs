Headline for the report writer: **civilian drone flying of any kind, aerial filming included, has been banned nationwide by the Ministry of Interior since 3 March 2026 "until further notice". The ban was reaffirmed on 15 September 2026, and I found no report that it has been lifted.** Retail sale of drones has been banned by MOCI since April 2026. A drone banner or a branded drone show by a private company is therefore **not currently possible in Kuwait** (this is a conclusion drawn from the sources below, not a direct statement in any of them).

## 1. DGCA / statutory drone regime (registration, licences, categories, altitude, no-fly zones, crowds, night, payload, insurance)

### Takeaway
Since September 2026 the governing text is **Decree-Law 89 of 2026** on drones, light sport aircraft and air sports. It requires DGCA registration, an airworthiness certificate, a DGCA operating licence and **Ministry of Interior approval before any flight**, plus permits to import or modify a drone. Penalties reach 3 years' prison and KD 5,000. The operational detail (categories, altitude, distances, crowd and night rules, payloads, insurance, fees) is left to executive regulations due by 31 Dec 2026, which I could not find. Before the decree, the regime was a DGCA circular (2019‑34, announced around Jan 2021). That circular's technical numbers could not be verified.

- Arts. 11, 12, 25 and 27 (the top penalty tier) probably cover operating without a licence or MOI approval and flying in prohibited areas. That is a guess from the pattern of reporting, since the article contents were not extracted.

## 2. Ministry of Interior / security approvals, current bans, imports, penalties

### Takeaway
On top of the statutory MOI approval requirement, the **MOI imposed a total nationwide ban on drone operation and aerial photography on 3 March 2026**, citing the security situation in the region; the 2026 Iranian strikes on Kuwait included drone strikes on Kuwait International Airport. **The ban was reaffirmed on 15 Sep 2026 "until further notice", and I found no lifting since.** MOCI separately banned the sale and trading of drones in shops (Ministerial Decision 31/2026, April 2026). Before the ban, the practical permit was obtained from the MOI in Subhan.

## 4. Municipality ad licence / Ministry of Information for aerial ads

### Takeaway
The Municipality's Advertising Regulation (599/2023 as amended by 223/2024) covers **flying balloon ads (البالونات الطائرة), which require approval from the General Fire Force and the DGCA**. That is the closest existing "aerial advertising" hook. I found no specific clause on drones, blimps or banner-towing aircraft. Because the regulation's KD 3,000–5,000 fine applies to a commercial ad "by any means" without a licence, a drone ad would most likely also need a Municipality ad licence. From around April 2027 the Media Law's Art. 34 licence (Decree-Law 102/2026) will apply at company level; see the earlier report.

### Cited Findings
- The Advertising Regulation requires approval from the General Fire Force (الإدارة العامة للإطفاء) and the DGCA for flying-balloon advertisements — [mesferlaw (text of 599/2023 + 223/2024)](https://mesferlaw.com/%D8%A8%D9%84%D8%AF%D9%8A%D8%A9-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%83%D9%88%D9%8A%D8%AA-%D9%82%D8%B1%D8%A7%D8%B1-%D8%B1%D9%82%D9%85-599-%D9%84%D8%B3%D9%86%D8%A9-2023-%D8%A8%D8%B4%D8%A7%D9%86-%D9%84%D8%A7%D8%A6%D8%AD/); [lawskw](https://lawskw.com/section/%D8%A8%D9%84%D8%AF%D9%8A%D8%A9-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%83%D9%88%D9%8A%D8%AA-%D9%82%D8%B1%D8%A7%D8%B1-%D8%B1%D9%82%D9%85-599-%D9%84%D8%B3%D9%86%D8%A9-2023-%D8%A8%D8%B4%D8%A7%D9%86-%D9%84%D8%A7%D8%A6%D8%AD%D8%A9-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A7%D8%B9%D9%84%D8%A7%D9%86%D8%A7%D8%AA%D9%88%D8%B2%D8%A7%D8%B1%D8%A9-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AF%D9%88%D9%84%D8%A9-%D9%84%D8%B4%D8%A6%D9%88%D9%86-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A8%D9%84%D8%AF%D9%8A%D8%A9-%D9%82%D8%B1%D8%A7%D8%B1-%D8%B1%D9%82%D9%85-223-%D9%84%D8%B3%D9%86%D8%A9-2024-%D8%A8%D8%B4%D8%A7%D9%86-%D8%AA%D8%B9%D8%AF%D9%8A%D9%84-%D9%84%D8%A7%D8%A6%D8%AD%D8%A9-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A7%D8%B9%D9%84%D8%A7%D9%86%D8%A7%D8%AA) (search extract)

- The earlier report (Mobile screen ads Kuwait and Saudi) covers Municipality licensing, fines, Decree-Law 102/2026 Art. 34 and the timeline. It is not repeated here.

### Inferences
- A drone banner or screen over a street, beach or mall is most plausibly licensed by analogy to the "flying balloon" category: Municipality ad licence plus Fire Force plus DGCA. The DGCA side now means Decree-Law 89 licences and MOI approval. A drone light show forming a logo is still a commercial ad "by any means", so a Municipality licence is likely needed too. Both points are inferences, untested.
- Site owners (malls, beach and park managers, e.g. the Touristic Enterprises Company for Green Island and Shaheed Park) would be an extra consent layer. This is an inference.

### Gaps
- Exact article number and fee for flying-balloon ads not extracted.
- No rule on banner-towing aircraft or blimps found.
- Ministry of Information: no current role found for aerial ads, beyond the 2027 Media Law licence.

## 5. Practical: timelines, per-event permits, 2024–2026 changes

### Takeaway
No reliable data on approval timelines. All evidence points to **per-event, per-flight approvals** (DGCA operating licence plus MOI approval for each use). The 2024–2026 timeline is: Feb 2025 national-day drone shows → 3 Mar 2026 MOI total ban → Apr 2026 MOCI sales ban → Sep 2026 Decree-Law 89/2026 → 15 Sep 2026 ban reaffirmed → executive regulations due 31 Dec 2026 → 1-year owner regularisation window to about Sep 2027.

### Cited Findings
- The decree requires MOI approval before use, which reads as a per-use approval — [Al-Seyassah](https://alseyassah.com/article/471682/)
- Executive regulations due by 31 Dec 2026, and a 1-year regularisation period — [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/world/gulf/kuwait/kuwait-brings-drones-under-new-law-with-jail-terms-and-fines-up-to-kd5000-1.500672815)
- The ban was reaffirmed 15 Sep 2026 — [Kuwait Times](https://kuwaittimes.com/article/49809/kuwait/moi-reiterates-nationwide-ban-on-drones-and-aerial-photography/)

### Inferences
- The realistic earliest window for a private drone-ad pilot is after (a) the MOI lifts the March 2026 ban and (b) the executive regulations issue, and probably 2027 at the earliest. The company should treat drones as a watch item rather than a 2026–27 product line.
- Given the 2022 government-only practice and the security posture, the most plausible first route is as a subcontractor for state or major-festival shows (Ya Hala, national celebrations), not standalone brand flights over streets or crowds.
- Flights over the Gulf Road, palaces (Bayan, Seif and Dasman are near the Gulf Road), oil facilities and the airport are very likely to fall in "sensitive" or restricted areas. No source lists these zones explicitly.

### Gaps
- No timeline, fee or lead-time data for drone permits in Kuwait.
- No confirmation whether approvals will be per-event or per-season under the new regulations.
- No Oct–Nov 2026 news found on the executive regulations or on lifting the ban. The company should check directly with PACA/DGCA and the MOI.
