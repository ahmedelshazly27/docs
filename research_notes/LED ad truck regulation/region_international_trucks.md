# Regulation of mobile LED billboard trucks: UAE, KSA, Qatar, Bahrain, Oman, Jordan, plus an international risk lens

Research date: 16 Nov 2026. Method: web search (about 17 tool calls). This brief builds on `../LED delivery ad platform regulation/rest_of_region.md` and does not repeat its general vehicle-ad and delivery-box findings. It cross-references them where the truck question depends on them.

**Blocked hosts.** Every direct page fetch failed with a DNS or allowlist block: khaleejtimes.com, intro.nyc, gov.om, visitdubai.com (the RTA OOH manual PDF), www.gov.uk, www.dubizzle.com, www.publications.qld.gov.au and services.amana-md.gov.sa. ## 1. Per-country legality, licensing authority, permit category and fees for dedicated LED ad trucks

### Takeaway
Dubai is the only market found with an explicit permit category for third-party "mobile advertising vehicles". The RTA charges advertising companies a reported AED 50,000 a year for mobile vehicles, trailers and semi-trailers, and applicants need a Dubai advertising trade licence plus Vehicle Licensing approval. Oman has an explicit service for an e-screen on a commercial vehicle, but the screen must be off while moving (reported fee OMR 2,500). Saudi Arabia's MOMAH sign rules include a "boards on/inside mobile vehicles" category and require both municipal and traffic approval. However, no official text was found that authorises a third-party LED truck, and earlier research found wording that ties vehicle signs to the vehicle owner's own business. Qatar, Bahrain and Jordan license vehicle ads generically and have no truck-specific category found.

**Qatar**
- Law No. 1 of 2012 on advertisements, as amended by **Law No. 5 of 2025**, requires a licence from the competent municipality before display. Fines reach QAR 30,000 plus removal costs. — [Mondaq on Law 5/2025](https://www.mondaq.com/real-estate/1649916/qatar-enacts-law-no-5-of-2025-major-reforms-to-the-regulation-of-advertisements-statues-and-memorials); [Al Tamimi](https://turtl.tamimi.com/story/law-update-issue-378-real-estate-and-construction-and-hotels-and-leisure/page/21)

**Oman**
- Gov.om service "**Add a Mobile Advertisement Electronic Screen on a Commercial Vehicle**": the screen **must not operate while the vehicle is moving**, the vehicle must not park near highways or main roads, and the reported fee is **OMR 2,500**. - A general gov.om "advertising on commercial vehicles" licence requires vehicle ownership papers, an active vehicle operating card and the ad images. **Ads on private vehicles are prohibited.** — [Gov.om advertising licence](https://gov.om/w/get-advertising-license-4)

**Jordan**
- The Greater Amman Municipality (GAM) advertising licensing regulation lists "advertising signs on vehicles and buses" as a licensable type (from earlier research). No LED-truck category or fee was found. — [JordanianLaw](https://jordanianlaw.com/?p=2780)

### Inferences
- Dubai's AED 50,000/yr "advertising company – mobile vehicles/trailers" line, if current, is a per-company fee rather than per vehicle. The source wording is ambiguous, so verify with the RTA. It is a clear legal pathway for a third-party LED truck fleet, gated on holding a Dubai advertising-activity trade licence.
- In KSA, the likely path is municipal (Amanah) licensing under the "mobile vehicle" sign category plus traffic/TGA approval. Whether third-party content is allowed is unresolved. A partner holding a transport or advertising licence (as with MediaGo and JTC) is the de-risked route.
- Oman effectively permits only **stationary** LED trucks, which function as a parked mobile billboard.

### Gaps
- The Dubai OOH manual text (any brightness, video or route rules for mobile LED vehicles) could not be read, so the current AED 50,000 figure is unconfirmed.
- No Abu Dhabi or Sharjah official permit for LED ad trucks was found.
- The KSA MOMAH mobile-vehicle sign conditions were not read in full: screen size, brightness, and whether third-party ads are allowed remain open.
- No fees were found for LED trucks in Qatar, Jordan or Bahrain beyond the generic per-m² vehicle-ad fee.

## 2. Video while driving, parking/stopping limits, truck movement bans, sound

### Takeaway
Only **Oman** has an explicit, official "screen off while moving" rule. No Gulf source found explicitly allows or bans full-motion video on a moving truck. Elsewhere the binding constraints are probably general traffic rules (distraction, dazzling lights, parking) and truck time and zone bans. Those were not found specifically for ad trucks in this pass.

### Cited Findings
- Oman: the screen must not operate while moving, and no parking near highways or main roads. — [Gov.om](https://gov.om/en/w/add-a-mobile-advertisement-electronic-screen-on-a-commercial-vehicle)
- Dubai precedent for screens in motion: Talabat/Flyby LED delivery boxes show ads **only while bikes are moving** (from earlier research). This shows Dubai tolerates moving digital ads at small scale. — [Logistics Middle East](https://www.logisticsmiddleeast.com/news/talabat-rolls-out-smart-delivery-ads-in-dubai)
- KSA: MediaGo claims to be "the first Saudi company **licensed and regulated** to enable digital advertising screens on moving vehicles" (Jeddah, with JTC). This is a self-claim. — [LinkedIn](https://sa.linkedin.com/in/sami-wali-75268830); [Campaign ME](https://campaignme.com/mediago-mobile-dooh-platform-jeddah/)
- A vendor blog claims mobile LED ads in Madinah are barred within 2 km of the Prophet's Mosque and must keep a minimum speed of 20 km/h. **This is unverified and has no official citation.** — [media.co.uk](https://media.co.uk/blogs/blog/sacred-city-led-regulations-madinah-scroller-permit)

### Inferences
- Sound: no Gulf source on audio from ad trucks was found. Assume general noise and public-nuisance rules apply and that audio would need explicit permit coverage.
- Truck time bans: Gulf cities commonly restrict heavy-truck movement at peak hours. Choosing a light-vehicle chassis (van or pickup under the heavy-vehicle threshold) likely avoids these, but this was not verified in this pass.

### Gaps
- No official Dubai, KSA, Qatar, Bahrain or Jordan rule was found on video versus static content, brightness or dwell time for vehicle screens.
- Truck-ban hours (Dubai and Riyadh heavy-vehicle curfews) and how they apply to LED trucks were not researched within budget.

## 5. International comparison: common regulatory concerns (risk lens)

### Takeaway
Major US cities treat vehicles whose **primary purpose is advertising** as a nuisance and ban or limit them, mainly for congestion, parking-space occupation, distraction and visual clutter. Courts have upheld these bans as content-neutral (Ninth Circuit). The UK treats parked ad vehicles and trailers as "advertisements" needing express consent, while genuinely moving vehicles are exempt. Australia handles it through vehicle standards and road rules on dazzling or distracting lights, and at least one operator says it cannot show video within drivers' sight.

### Cited Findings
- **New York City:** mobile billboard trucks are already illegal under traffic rule **34 RCNY §4-12(j)**. Council bills Int. 75-2022 (to codify it) and Int. 541-2024 (Brannan, in the Transportation committee, introduced 7 Mar 2024) followed. Courts upheld summonses against "flat" trucks and cargoless "moving billboard" trucks. — [Int. 75](https://intro.nyc/0075-2022); [Int. 541](https://intro.nyc/0541-2024+); [FindLaw case](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/ny-supreme-court-appellate-division/1132850.html)
- New York State also banned digital and flashing billboards on vessels in navigable waters (2019). — [NY Senate](https://nysenate.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2019/brad-hoylman-sigal/governor-signs-hoylman-gottfried-legislation-ban)
- NYC TLC banned rooftop advertising on rideshare vehicles. — [The Rideshare Guy](https://therideshareguy.com/new-york-tlc-ban-on-rooftop-advertising-will-cost-drivers-thousands-of-dollars/)
- **California / Los Angeles:** Vehicle Code §395.5 defines a "mobile billboard advertising display" by its **primary purpose of advertising**, and §21100 lets cities regulate them (AB 2756). LA banned parking such displays on any public street (Dec 2010). The **Ninth Circuit upheld the ordinances** in *Lone Star Security & Video v. City of LA* (2016). — [CBS LA](https://www.cbsnews.com/losangeles/news/la-city-council-approves-ban-on-mobile-billboards); [LAMC](https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/los_angeles/latest/lamc/0-0-0-214564); [Justia 9th Cir.](https://us9thcircuitcourtofappealsopinions.justia.com/2016/07/08/lone-star-sec-video-v-city-of-los-angeles); [Legal Newsline](https://legalnewsline.com/stories/510968289-ninth-circuit-upholds-ban-on-mobile-ads-in-los-angeles-other-calif-cities)
- **San Francisco:** the Transportation Code has findings on "parking large vehicles such as trucks and vans in public streets for the purpose of displaying commercial advertising". The operative text was not seen. — [SF Transportation Code](https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/san_francisco/latest/sf_transportation/0-0-0-51846)
- **Chicago:** no truck ban was confirmed. A 2014 local blog asked why ad trucks had not been banned. Chicago did ban ads on TNC (rideshare) vehicles, which was litigated in *Vugo v. City of Chicago*. — [Grid Chicago](https://gridchicago.com/?p=6919); [Justia – Vugo](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/district-courts/illinois/ilndce/1:2017cv00864/336201/47)
- **UK:** planning guidance states that ads on vehicles or trailers **parked in fields, on verges or in lay-bys need express consent**, and only vehicles normally used as moving vehicles, not mainly for display, are exempt. (Quoted via a PAS forum; gov.uk was blocked.) Councils also use Highways Act 1980 s.137 (obstruction) and s.132 (unauthorised marks). The Clean Neighbourhoods and Environment Act 2005 targets vehicles parked on roads for sale. — [PAS forum](https://khub.net:443/web/planningadvisoryservicepas/forum/-/message_boards/message/6221997); [gov.uk advertisements guidance](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/advertisements); [Dudley](https://www.dudley.gov.uk/resident/parking-roads/roads-highways-and-pavements/road-markings-and-signage/illegal-signs-/); [Slough](https://www.slough.gov.uk/parking-permits/nuisance-parking)
- **Australia:** NSW (RMS VSI No. 35, 2012) and Queensland issue vehicle-standards notes on mobile advertising units, which must meet construction and equipment rules and road rules. A NSW operator states it "cannot display video content on the truck within sight of drivers" because the road rules ban lights that dazzle or distract. Brisbane moved to ban flashing or moving digital signs. — [NSW RMS VSI 35](https://www.nsw.gov.au/sites/default/files/2021-02/RMS-12.045-Mobile-advertising-units-Vehicle-Standards-Information-No-35-September-2012.pdf); [Qld guide](https://www.publications.qld.gov.au/dataset/ec37bf52-66be-47b0-9832-0e51df00eb3f/resource/b4b7b9b0-4b8f-46c2-98f4-209a929b68cb/download/advertising-signs-and-additional-lighting-fitted-to-a-vehicle_sep21-final.pdf); [CarAdvice](https://www.Caradvice.Com.Au/caradvice/are-mobile-billboards-legal/); [B&T Brisbane](https://www.bandt.com.au/Brisbane-to-ban-flashing-and-moving-digital-signs/)

### Inferences
- Common regulatory concerns are: (1) a "primary purpose advertising" vehicle occupying kerb space, (2) driver distraction from video or bright moving content, (3) congestion from slow loops, (4) visual clutter, and (5) emissions and idling. Gulf regulators already show (1) and (2) through Oman's off-while-moving rule and KSA's owner-only vehicle-sign wording. Expect these lenses when Dubai and KSA applications are reviewed.
- Design mitigations: static or slow-transition content while moving, brightness auto-dimming, no audio, no parking on public streets unless permitted, and fixed approved routes.

### Gaps
- Chicago and San Francisco operative ban text was not confirmed. The UK Highway Code and section 147 points could not be verified (gov.uk blocked). Australian state rules were not checked state by state.
