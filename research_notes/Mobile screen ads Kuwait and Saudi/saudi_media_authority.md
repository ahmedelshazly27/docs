# Saudi Arabia: media authority rules for advertising content and advertising businesses (moving LED screens on trucks, delivery boxes, taxi/car tops)

Research date: 18 Nov 2026. Method note: in this session, direct page fetches of gamr.gov.sa, gmedia.gov.sa, tamimi.com and saudigazette.com.sa were blocked by the network (DNS failure), so **every finding below comes from search-engine snippets and summaries, not from reading the full pages.** Treat specifics (fees, fine amounts, dates) as "reported" until someone checks the primary page. Anything marked as inference is my own reasoning.

Saudi content in the earlier report (`/home/user/docs/reports/LED delivery ad platform regulation.md`) covers only the municipal and traffic layer: the Balady sign licence, the need for municipal plus traffic approval on vehicle signs, a possible "vehicle owner's own brand only" rule, TGA delivery rules, and MediaGo + Jeddah Transport Co. It does not cover the media authority. These notes fill that gap.

## Q1. Authority structure as of 2026. Which body licenses advertising activity and ad agencies?

### Takeaway
GCAM (General Commission for Audiovisual Media, est. 2012) was reorganised by Council of Ministers Decision No. 174 of 27/02/1445 AH (about September 2023). It became the General Authority for Media Regulation (GAMR, also "Gmedia"; Arabic الهيئة العامة لتنظيم الإعلام). Its remit now covers all media, publishing included. The board is chaired by the Minister of Media. GAMR licenses advertising offices and agencies through its "Iʿlam" (منصة إعلام) e-services portal. A single new Media Law that would replace the Press & Publications Law and the Audiovisual Media Law has been drafted and consulted on several times. I could not confirm that it has been enacted as of Nov 2026.

### Cited Findings
- GCAM was founded in 2012. In September 2023 the Council of Ministers reorganised it to also regulate publication media, turning it into the General Authority of Media Regulation — [Wikipedia: General Authority of Media Regulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Authority_of_Media_Regulation)
- The new statute, Council of Ministers Decision No. 174 dated 27/02/1445 AH, replaces the GCAM statute (Decision No. 332 dated 16/10/1433 AH) — [Qanoniah: Statute of the General Panel for Media Regulation](https://qanoniah.com/en/File/m9WMNVEaj5oly0zLeybYvQB6O-Statute-of-the-General-Panel-for-Media-Regulation.); [Zawya: GAMR tasked to supervise entire media sector](https://www.zawya.com/en/legal/regulations/saudi-general-authority-of-media-regulation-is-tasked-to-supervise-entire-media-sector-y215a5za); [Digital Policy Alert: Adopted Regulation of GAMR](https://digitalpolicyalert.org/event/24132-adopted-regulation-of-the-general-authority-for-media-regulation)
- Naming varies. The statute text uses "General Commission/Panel for Media Regulation", while Wikipedia, Saudipedia and the press use "General Authority of/for Media Regulation" (GAMR, Gmedia). Press reports in 2025 still sometimes say "GCAM" — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Authority_of_Media_Regulation); [Arab News PK: GCAM lists new Mawthooq rules](https://www.arabnews.pk/node/2176661)
- Saudipedia says GAMR oversees advertising activity by individuals, companies and institutions — [Saudipedia: The General Authority of Media Regulation](https://saudipedia.com/en/the-general-authority-of-media-regulation)
- The Minister of Media (Salman Al-Dosari) chairs the authority's board — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Authority_of_Media_Regulation)
- GAMR's e-services portal has a service called "Visual & audio media advertising offices, marketing offices and advertising agencies" (مكاتب الدعاية والإعلان الإعلامية المرئية والمسموعة ومكاتب التسويق ووكالات الدعاية) — [eservices.gamr.gov.sa/user/services/13](https://eservices.gamr.gov.sa/user/services/13); services list at [gmedia.gov.sa/ar/services](https://gmedia.gov.sa/ar/services)
- Draft media law history:
  - 2020: GCAM published a comprehensive draft media law covering traditional and digital media, to replace the two existing laws — [ASG/DGA analysis](https://dgagroup.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/ASG-Analysis-New-Law-Governing-Media-in-Saudi-Arabia-1.pdf); [Digital Policy Alert: Saudi Arabian Media Law](https://digitalpolicyalert.org/change/10222-saudi-arabian-media-law)
  - 2023: GAMR closed consultation on a draft Media Law on 5 Dec 2023. The draft gives broad content powers and sets up a licensing system for all media — same sources
  - Asharq Al-Awsat reported a "draft Media Law" released for public comment — [Aawsat: مشروع نظام الإعلام لمرئيات العموم](https://aawsat.com/node/4649966) (date not visible in the snippet)
- **Unverified:** a LinkedIn profile says the new Media Law "will take effect 180 days from publication, on 30 December 2026". No official or law-firm source confirmed this, and a targeted search for a 2026 Media Law royal decree found nothing — [LinkedIn (personal profile)](https://sa.linkedin.com/in/sultan-khallaf)
- In July 2025 the Minister of Media said the national media strategy was still under review — [Saudi Gazette](https://saudigazette.com.sa/article/635755) (snippet only)
- **Possible confusion:** a "draft Digital Media Regulation Law 2026" (مسودة نظام تنظيم الإعلام الرقمي), together with the line "Media authority: no targeting of freedom of opinion" (14 Apr 2026), appears on almamlakatv.com. That is a **Jordanian** outlet, and the draft cites Jordanian laws, so it is probably **not** Saudi — [Almamlaka TV](https://www.almamlakatv.com/news/189042-نص-مسودة-نظام-تنظيم-الإعلام-الرقمي)

### Inferences
- The body to deal with on media and advertising content is GAMR/Gmedia (formerly GCAM). It sits under the Ministry of Media; the Ministry sets policy and GAMR licenses and enforces.
- The current legal base is still the Audiovisual Media Law (1438/2017) and its Implementing Regulation ([Qanoniah: Implementing Regulation of the Audio-Visual Media Law](https://qanoniah.com/en/File/j3O8KWdMkPo6p4A7GBR6q5eZg-Implementing+Regulation+of+the+Audio-Visual+Media+System)), plus the Press & Publications Law. This holds unless a unified Media Law has been published, which I could not confirm.

### Gaps
- Whether the unified Media Law (نظام الإعلام) was approved or published in 2025–2026, and its effective date. Check uqn.gov.sa and laws.boe.gov.sa directly; both were unreachable here.
- The official current English name. Gmedia's own site was unreachable.

## Q2. Is a media licence needed to run digital/outdoor ad screens or an ad agency? Fees, requirements, platform

### Takeaway
GAMR runs a licence for advertising offices and agencies (مكاتب/وكالات الدعاية والإعلان) on the Iʿlam portal. Prerequisites: a commercial register (CR) that lists the media activity, a national address and valid ID. I found **no GAMR licence specific to digital or outdoor screens**. Physical screens and signs are licensed by municipalities (MOMAH/Balady), and vehicle signs also need traffic approval. The agency-licence fee was not found. The only confirmed GAMR advertising fee is SAR 15,000 for 3 years for the individual Mawthooq licence.

### Cited Findings
- The agency licence is applied for on the Iʿlam portal. Requirements: a CR with the requested media activity added, a defined national address, and the applicant's valid ID (national ID, iqama or passport) — [GAMR e-services: service 13](https://eservices.gamr.gov.sa/user/services/13) (search summary)
- In Jan 2024 GAMR described its media-consultancy agency licence. It is also applied for via Iʿlam and requires the media activity to be on the CR, which suggests the same pattern applies across its business licences — [Sabq](https://sabq.org/saudia/v8dr5yytaz)
- GAMR's FAQ includes the question "What are the activities required to obtain advertising and publicity licenses?", but the answer text was not retrievable — [GAMR FAQs](https://gamr.gov.sa/en/faqs)
- Mawthooq (individual social-media advertiser licence) fee: SAR 15,000, valid 3 years. This figure appears in a consultation draft and secondary sites — [NCC Istitlaa draft (GCAM)](https://istitlaa.ncc.gov.sa/ar/Media/GCAM/AdvertisingContent/Pages/default.aspx); [mz-mz.net](https://mz-mz.net/1667916/)
- Non-Saudi non-GCC individuals who want to advertise must either contract with a licensed local advertising agency or hold an investment (MISA) licence — [Arab News PK](https://www.arabnews.pk/node/2099346/media); [Okaz](https://www.okaz.com.sa/news/local/2212992)
- One case study says an advertising agency needs a MISA licence, then a CR, then an "initial permit" from GAMR (secondary source) — [Wamda: Tawaref series, Saudi specialised licences (Sep 2024)](https://www.wamda.com/en/2024/09/tawaref-series-need-know-saudi-specialised-licences)
- Physical signs are licensed separately by municipalities through the Balady operational licence for advertising/promotional signs, and vehicle signs need municipal plus traffic approval — [Balady service](https://www.balady.gov.sa/en/services/issuing-operational-license-advertising-or-promotional-sign); [Balady FAQ](https://balady.gov.sa/en/faq/it-required-attach-building-permit-when-issuing-operational-license-advertising-or-promotional) (from the earlier report)
- MOMAH issued updated requirements for permanent and temporary ad boards (2025) and directed advertisers to Balady — [Moving Walls](https://www.movingwalls.com/locations/saudi-arabia); [Okaz](https://www.okaz.com.sa/news/local/2208664)

### Inferences
- A company selling ad time on its own screens to brands looks like an advertising business ("advertising agency/office"). It would most likely need (a) a CR with the advertising activity, (b) the GAMR advertising-office/agency licence, (c) a MISA licence if it is foreign-owned, and (d) municipal and traffic permits per vehicle or screen.
- The GAMR licence is likely needed because GAMR licenses the advertising activity itself, not the screen. That is an inference from the e-service scope; I found no explicit GAMR text that names vehicle or DOOH screens.
- Mawthooq is for individuals advertising on their own social-media accounts. It is **not** the licence for a screen network. It matters only if the startup pays influencers or riders to promote campaigns on social media.

### Gaps
- GAMR agency-licence fee, validity, processing time, and whether DOOH or mobile screens are a separate activity code. The GAMR portal was unreachable.
- Whether Saudi ISIC activity codes for "advertising agency" or "outdoor advertising" trigger the GAMR licence automatically.

## Q3. Content rules: standards, Arabic, prohibited categories, sector approvals, Mawthooq, penalties

### Takeaway
GAMR enforces media content rules: no content against national identity, religion or social values, no exploitation of children, no bullying, and limits on vulgarity and showing off wealth. It also enforces a total ban on promoting tobacco. Alcohol and gambling are illegal in KSA, so ads for them are not allowed (inference; no specific ad text found). Sector regulators add their own pre-approval: SFDA runs an "Iʿlan" licensing system for food ads and an approval process for medical-device and drug ads, and SAMA requires Arabic as the primary language plus APR and VAT-inclusive pricing for financial ads. Reported penalties go up to SAR 500k for ad violations and up to SAR 5m plus prison for cover-up (tasattur) or unlicensed foreign advertisers. Repeat offences double the fine. GAMR is running a fine-instalment programme until 30 Dec 2026.

### Cited Findings
- GAMR's new media content rules ban content that conflicts with national identity or social values, blackmail, threats, exploitation of children and bullying, and treat vulgar language or flaunting money and cars as a violation — [Khaleej Times](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/world/gulf/no-bullying-misleading-content-saudi-authority-issues-new-media-regulations?amp=1); [Annahar](https://www.annahar.com/arab-world/arabian-gulf/245513/)
- The tobacco promotion ban covers all channels and platforms, commercial ads included, and violators face fines and legal action — [Okaz](https://www.okaz.com.sa/specialized-corners/na/2213015) (search summary)
- Mawthooq licensees must follow the "individual advertising content controls" and advertise only through the account registered with GAMR. GAMR found 460 violating ads by 335 individuals after the licence came into force — same Okaz source; [Arab News PK: new Mawthooq rules](https://www.arabnews.pk/node/2176661)
- The Mawthooq requirement for every content creator earning ad revenue applied from early October 2025 (announced August 2025). Licensees must stop any ad on GAMR's request — [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/world/gulf/saudi/saudi-arabia-tightens-rules-on-influencer-ads-with-mandatory-licensing-1.1733131320843); [Saudipedia: Mawthooq](https://saudipedia.com/en/what-is-the-mawthooq-license)
- REGA's real estate ad rules also refer to a "General Guide to Advertising Content Controls" (الدليل العام لضوابط المحتوى الإعلاني). This suggests a cross-sector guide exists, but I could not find its text — [Qanoniah: ضوابط الإعلانات العقارية](https://qanoniah.com/File/QNzgY5ZPxKo1x6jndyG3q6wpX-ضوابط-الإعلانات-العقارية); REGA ad licence rules effective 1 May 2026 — [Gulf Construction Online](https://gulfconstructiononline.com/Article/464369/Understanding_REGA’s_new_marketing_rules)
- SFDA runs an electronic "Advertising licences for food" system (نظام تراخيص الدعاية والإعلان للغذاء "إعلان") — [SFDA eservice 358](https://www.sfda.gov.sa/ar/eservices/358). SFDA's food/feed/pesticide ad guide lists roadside billboards as an ad channel — [SFDA guide PDF](https://beta.sfda.gov.sa/sites/default/files/2024-03/FOODFEEDPISTCIDE.pdf)
- SFDA medical-device ads need prior approval and must be in Arabic when aimed at the public. One source says English is required, so the sources conflict — [Al Tamimi: SFDA medical device advertising](https://www.tamimi.com/news/new-sfda-guidance-on-medical-device-advertising); [SFDA news](https://www.sfda.gov.sa/en/news/sfda-compels-companies-abide-requirements-medical-devices-products-advertisement); [CMS guide](https://cms.law/en/int/expert-guides/cms-expert-guide-to-advertising-of-medicines-and-medical-devices/saudi-arabia)
- SAMA rules for financial-institution ads: Arabic must be the primary language (except ads aimed at non-Arabic speakers), APR must be disclosed for financing, and fees must be shown including VAT — [SAMA Rules for Advertising Products and Services (AR PDF)](https://www.sama.gov.sa/ar-sa/LawsRegulations/ConsumerProtectionRules/Rules_for_Advertising_Products_and_Services_Provided_by_Financial_Institutions-AR.pdf)
- Penalties:
  - Reported fines for ad violations range from SAR 100 to SAR 500,000. In cover-up (tasattur) cases they reach SAR 5m, with up to 5 years' prison, suspension of the activity and a ban on the advertiser — [Okaz](https://www.okaz.com.sa/news/local/2212992); [Ajel: SAR 5m fine for non-Saudi celebrities advertising without licence](https://ajel.sa/thehour/a-fine-of-5-million-riyals-preventing-non-saudi-celebrities-from-advertising-without-a-license)
  - One creator was fined SAR 660k in total for advertising without a licence, and repeat violations double the fine — [Okaz](https://www.okaz.com.sa/local/na/2228242) (search summary)
  - A foreign influencer was fined SAR 400k and banned from advertising to Saudi audiences — same search set ([Okaz](https://www.okaz.com.sa/news/local/2106407))
  - Snippets also mention GAMR powers of up to SAR 1m in fines, licence suspension or cancellation, and seizure of broadcast equipment. That figure comes from a search summary and is not verified — [Okaz](https://www.okaz.com.sa/news/local/2212992)
- Arabic for general ads: only a vendor blog says Arabic prominence is "generally expected". No official general rule was found — [media.co.uk](https://media.co.uk/blogs/blog/saudi-advertising-regulations-legal-compliance-guide)
- The UAE (not KSA) issued an advertising content guide in Oct 2026 requiring 50% of ad space in Arabic and banning visible tattoos. Do not confuse it with KSA — [Emarat Al Youm](https://www.emaratalyoum.com/local-section/other/2026-10-21-1.2094596)

### Inferences
- For a DOOH network, the practical content compliance stack is:
  1. GAMR content standards (values, children, tobacco)
  2. Sector pre-approvals: SFDA (food, drugs, devices, cosmetics), SAMA (banks, fintech), REGA (real estate)
  3. Municipal sign rules, which typically include Arabic text requirements; that is in the municipal regulation, see the earlier report
- Brands, not the screen operator, usually hold the SFDA or REGA ad licence. Even so, the operator should collect these as part of its creative approval process.
- Alcohol and gambling ads: no specific ad provision was found, but both activities are illegal in KSA. Treat them as prohibited (inference).

### Gaps
- The full text of the "General Guide to Advertising Content Controls" and whether it applies to outdoor screens.
- The official GAMR violations schedule (جدول المخالفات) with amounts for businesses, as opposed to individuals.
- Any general statutory Arabic-language requirement for ads outside SAMA, SFDA and municipal sign rules.

## Q4. Does GAMR cover outdoor ads and screens, or only Balady/municipality? Coordination with MOMAH?

### Takeaway
I found no explicit source showing GAMR licensing or pre-approving outdoor or vehicle screens, and no published GAMR–MOMAH coordination rule for digital screen content. Physical outdoor permits sit clearly with MOMAH/Balady, plus traffic for vehicles. GAMR's mandate covers "advertising activity" and media content generally, so its content rules plausibly apply to what the screens show.

### Cited Findings
- Billboards need municipal approval on top of content clearance, and "most advertising requires pre-approval". This is a vendor blog, not official — [media.co.uk](https://www.media.co.uk/blogs/blog/saudi-advertising-regulations-legal-compliance-guide); [media.co.uk Riyadh LED permits](https://www.media.co.uk/blogs/blog/riyadh-led-6-permits-digital-regulations)
- MOMAH updated its permanent and temporary ad board requirements in 2025 and directs applicants to Balady — [Okaz](https://www.okaz.com.sa/news/local/2208664); [Moving Walls](https://www.movingwalls.com/locations/saudi-arabia)
- Makkah municipality publishes technical requirements for ad boards — [Holy Makkah municipality PDF](https://sp.holymakkah.gov.sa/Static/Documents/RegulationsAndRequirements/Technical/الاشتراطات%20الفنية%20للوحات%20الدعائية%20والإعلانية.pdf)
- MOMAH's Furas investment platform tenders screen sites with a tender booklet (كراسة الشاشة). These are municipal concession contracts for screen sites — [furas.momah.gov.sa](https://furas.momah.gov.sa/opportunity/attachment/232548243/كراسة%20الشاشة/pdf)
- MediaGo + Jeddah Transport Co. claims to be the first licensed moving-vehicle screen network in KSA (from the earlier report) — [Campaign ME](https://campaignme.com/mediago-mobile-dooh-platform-jeddah/)
- One source says the CITC (now CST) works with GAMR on campaign approval. This is a low-quality vendor blog — [istizada.com](https://istizada.com/blog/saudi-arabia-advertising/)

### Inferences
- The likely split: MOMAH/Balady and traffic (Muroor) permit the physical screen or vehicle sign; GAMR licenses the advertising business and enforces content standards; sector regulators approve regulated-product ads.
- Ask MediaGo or JTC which licences they actually hold. That is the quickest real-world check.

### Gaps
- No primary source on GAMR jurisdiction over DOOH or vehicle screens, and none on GAMR–MOMAH coordination. Needs a direct inquiry to GAMR (Iʿlam portal or call centre) or local counsel.

## Q5. 2024–2026 enforcement news on unlicensed advertising

### Takeaway
Enforcement reported in 2024–2026 has targeted individuals and influencers (Mawthooq) and companies that hire foreign advertisers. I found no reported action against unlicensed outdoor or screen operators.

### Cited Findings
- GAMR found 128 violating ads by 94 individuals advertising without a licence (date unclear) — search summary of [Okaz](https://www.okaz.com.sa/news/local/2212121)
- GAMR referred companies that contracted foreign-based advertisers to the competent committees — [Okaz](https://www.okaz.com.sa/news/local/2212992)
- A Snapchat celebrity was fined and had their licence suspended for repeated violations — [Sabq](https://sabq.org/article/6Ykv7Pd)
- Fine instalment initiative: GAMR accepts applications from private and non-profit media establishments to pay fines in instalments, until 30 Dec 2026 (email fi.dp@GMedia.gov.sa) — [Okaz](https://okaz.com.sa/local/na/2270478) (search summary; exact article not verified)
- Mawthooq became mandatory in October 2025, and new guidelines followed in December 2025 — [Arab News PK](https://www.arabnews.pk/node/2176661); [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/world/gulf/saudi/saudi-arabia-tightens-rules-on-influencer-ads-with-mandatory-licensing-1.1733131320843)

### Inferences
- The instalment scheme for "media establishments" shows that GAMR fines businesses as well as individuals.

### Gaps
- No outdoor or DOOH-specific enforcement cases found.

## Q6. Foreign ownership (MISA) and Saudization

### Takeaway
A Kuwaiti (GCC) company may be treated like a Saudi company for some activities. Non-GCC foreign shareholders need a MISA investment licence before the CR. Secondary sources say 100% foreign ownership is possible for most marketing and advertising services. None of this was confirmed against MISA's official negative list in this session.

### Cited Findings
- Agencies with non-GCC shareholders generally need a MISA licence before the CR; wholly Saudi or GCC-owned agencies usually do not. This is a consultancy source — [NobleCore KSA (2026)](https://noblecoreksa.com/marketing-advertising-agency-saudi-arabia/)
- 100% foreign ownership is reportedly allowed in most marketing and advertising services. This is a formation-services vendor — same; see also [Setup in Saudi: media business licence types](https://www.setupinsaudi.com/en/news/announcements/media-business-setup-in-saudi-arabia-license-types-and-process)
- MISA processing takes roughly 2–4 weeks, with fees of SAR 2,000–10,000 depending on activity. This is vendor-reported — [AstroLabs MISA guide 2025](https://insight.astrolabs.com/how-to-get-a-misa-license-in-saudi-arabia-2025-guide-for-foreign-businesses)
- Foreign advertisers can operate via a licensed local agency or a MISA investment licence — [Arab News PK](https://www.arabnews.pk/node/2099346/media)
- TGA limits freelance delivery to Saudis, and non-Saudi riders must work through licensed companies. This affects the rider-box model (from the earlier report) — [Entarabi](https://entarabi.com/en/2024/04/tga-begins-implementing-the-first-phase-of-decisions-to-regulate-the-order-delivery-sector-in-saudi-arabia/)

### Inferences
- If the Kuwaiti parent is 100% GCC-owned, it may be able to register without MISA under GCC national treatment. If it has any non-GCC investors (common for VC-backed startups), assume a MISA licence is needed.
- Nitaqat Saudization quotas apply to any Saudi entity's headcount. I found no ad-sector-specific Saudization rule.

### Gaps
- MISA's official current position on advertising activities (ISIC 7310) and any minimum capital. misa.gov.sa was not checked.
- Saudization percentage requirements for advertising or marketing occupations.
