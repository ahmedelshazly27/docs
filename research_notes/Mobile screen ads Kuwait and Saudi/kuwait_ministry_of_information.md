# Kuwait Ministry of Information (وزارة الإعلام) and advertising on moving LED screens / DOOH

Research date: 18 November 2026. Scope: Kuwait only.

**Method caveat (read first).** In this session, page fetching was blocked for almost every host (tamimi.com, glaco.com, mesferlaw.com, aljarida.com and others were refused by the network proxy). All findings therefore come from search-engine result snippets and summaries, not full page reads. No primary gazette text (Kuwait Al-Youm issue 1811) was read directly. Article numbers and quotes below should be treated as **reported**, not verified, until counsel checks the gazette. Earlier project reports already cover the Municipality advertising regulation (599/2023, 223/2024, and the Dec 2025 amendments), Traffic Department rules, and the basics of Decree-Law 102/2026. These notes add the Ministry of Information angle.

## 1. Which laws give the Ministry of Information power over advertising?

### Takeaway
Until about April 2027, the Ministry of Information's powers come from three media laws: Press & Publications 3/2006, Audiovisual Media 61/2007 and Electronic Media 8/2016. Those laws regulate advertising only inside *media outlets*: publications, TV/radio, news websites and production houses. **Decree-Law 102/2026 on Media Regulation** (قانون تنظيم الإعلام) repeals all three and creates, for the first time, a general licensing requirement for *anyone* who advertises or promotes commercially to a public in Kuwait (Art. 34). It was published on 4 Oct 2026 and takes effect about 4 Apr 2027. Whether "anyone" will be read to include DOOH screen operators is the key open question.

### Cited Findings
- Decree-Law No. 102 of 2026 issuing the Media Regulation Law was published in Kuwait Al-Youm on 4 October 2026, issue 1811. It was reportedly issued on 27 Sept 2026 and takes effect six months after publication, i.e. about 4 April 2027 — [Al-Jarida, "صدور «تنظيم الإعلام»... والتنفيذ خلال 6 أشهر"](https://www.aljarida.com/article/146388); [Al-Anba](https://www.alanba.com.kw/1379106); [Sarmad](https://sarmad.com/346775/); [salahkw.com](https://salahkw.com/blog/qanun-tanzim-alilam-alkuwaiti-102-2026)
- The issuance decree (Art. 2) requires the competent minister to issue the **Executive Regulations within six months of publication**, i.e. by about April 2027 — [Al-Seyassah](https://alseyassah.com/article/472942/); [Al-Jarida](https://www.aljarida.com/article/146388)
- The law repeals Law 3/2006 (Press & Publications), Law 61/2007 (Audiovisual Media) and Law 8/2016 (Electronic Media). Al-Jarida, reporting the draft: "نص على إلغاء القانون رقم ۳ لسنة 2006 بشأن المطبوعات والنشر، والقانون رقم 61 لسنة 2007 بشأن الإعلام المرئي والمسموع، والقانون رقم 8 لسنة 2016 بتنظيم الإعلام الإلكتروني" — [Al-Jarida, "«تنظيم الإعلام»... 3 قوانين في تشريع واحد"](https://www.aljarida.com/article/143317); [Al-Seyassah](https://alseyassah.com/article/472942/)
- **Art. 34 (as reported):** "تخضع لأحكام القانون كل من يباشر الدعاية أو الإعلان التجاري أو الترويج متى استهدف محتواه جمهوراً داخل الكويت، مواطناً كان أو مقيماً أو زائراً. ولا يجوز له ذلك، بمقابل أو بدون مقابل، إلا بترخيص من الوزارة المختصة." (Anyone who carries out commercial advertising or promotion aimed at a public in Kuwait needs a licence from the competent ministry, paid or unpaid.) — [kwt.one](https://kwt.one/media-law-advertisers/); [Al-Hadath](https://www.alhadath.net/2026/09/02/الكويت-تقر-قانونا-جديدا-لتنظيم-المؤثرين-والاعلانات-الرقمية)
- Chapter 8 (Arts. 34–37) covers "advertisers and influencers". Art. 36 reportedly lets the Ministry set rules for contests, gifts and prizes — [ASAR](https://www.asarlegal.com/kuwaits-new-media-regulation-law-what-businesses-need-to-know/); [Mondaq/ASAR](https://www.mondaq.com/social-media/1853030/kuwaits-new-media-regulation-law-what-businesses-need-to-know)
- Scope as summarised by law firms: newspapers and publications, audiovisual media, technical broadcasting services, electronic news outlets, news agencies, foreign correspondents, artistic production and cinema, plus digital activity including social-media advertising and commercial promotion. ASAR also says the law "introduces broader requirements for licensing, advertising, promotional activity and digital content" — [ASAR](https://www.asarlegal.com/kuwaits-new-media-regulation-law-what-businesses-need-to-know/); [Wefaq Law](https://www.wefaqlaw.com/en/news/kuwait-issues-new-media-regulation-law-with-licences-for-websites-influencers-and-advertisers)
- The law is administered by the **General Administration for Media Regulation (الإدارة العامة لتنظيم الإعلام)** at the Ministry of Information — [Wefaq Law](https://www.wefaqlaw.com/en/blog/kuwait-media-law-2026-what-it-means-for-influencers-companies-and-media-businesses)
- Explanatory memorandum: the law organises new concepts such as electronic media, the electronic media outlet and its owner/user, and social-media platforms. The search summary found no definition of "إعلان" in the reported extracts — [Al-Qabas](https://www.alqabas.com/article/5970538/); [Sarmad](https://sarmad.com/346812/)
- **No source found mentions outdoor screens, billboards, LED screens, vehicles or "الشاشات الإعلانية" in Decree-Law 102/2026** (searched in English and Arabic) — see the searches summarised from [ASAR](https://www.asarlegal.com/kuwaits-new-media-regulation-law-what-businesses-need-to-know/), [Kuwait Times](https://kuwaittimes.com/article/50892/kuwait/new-media-law-brings-social-media-ads-under-regulation/), [Lexis Middle East](https://www.lexismiddleeast.com/news/2026-10-05_21/en)
- ASAR notes that a media licence does not replace any other licence required under Kuwaiti law, so the Municipality licence still applies — [ASAR](https://www.asarlegal.com/kuwaits-new-media-regulation-law-what-businesses-need-to-know/)
- Law 3/2006 (current until about Apr 2027): a 2016 change extended the meaning of "publication" (مطبوع) to content published via websites and electronic media, and Art. 21 lists prohibited content — [Kuwait Judicial Institute, "جرائم الإعلام والنشر"](https://www.kijls.moj.gov.kw/ar/sections/section4/sub3_section4/Releases/KIJSReleases/جرائم%20الإعلام%20والنشر.pdf)
- A search summary attributed the following text to Law 3/2006: "يجوز بقرار مسبب من الوزير المختص حظر نشر أي إعلانات تجارية أو غيرها" and "لا يجوز للداعي والمعلن مباشرة نشاط الدعاية والإعلان ... إلا بعد الحصول على ترخيص بذلك من الوزارة المختصة". **This is contradicted**: another result says Art. 22 of Law 3/2006 deals with importing foreign publications, and Ministerial Resolution 58/2015, which replaced Art. 22 of the *executive regulation*, deals with cancelling newspaper licences — [mesferlaw index page for Law 3/2006](https://mesferlaw.com/قانون-رقم-3-لسنة-2006م-في-شأن-المطبوعات-والن/); [lawskw – Resolution 7/2016](https://lawskw.com/section/قرار-وزاري-رقم-7-لسنة-2016-بشأن-إلغاء-ترخيص-صحيفة-%28المدى%29-.). The "الداعي والمعلن" wording is most likely from the new law, not 3/2006 (unverified).

### Inferences
- Under current law (to about Apr 2027), no source shows the Ministry of Information has jurisdiction over *outdoor* advertising content as such. Its advertising powers attach to media it licenses (print, broadcast, e-media, production houses).
- Art. 34 is framed around the *advertiser/promoter* ("كل من يباشر الدعاية أو الإعلان التجاري"), not the medium. Read literally, it could catch a company that sells ad time on LED screens to brands, and possibly the brands themselves. All commentary found focuses on influencers and social media, so whether the Ministry will apply it to DOOH depends on the Executive Regulations (due about April 2027).

### Gaps
- Full gazette text of Decree-Law 102/2026, including Art. 1 definitions of "إعلان", "وسيلة إعلامية" and "المحتوى الإعلامي", was not read. This is the single most important document to obtain.
- The text of Law 61/2007 and Law 8/2016 on advertising was not retrieved.
- Executive Regulations: no report found that they have been issued as of mid-Nov 2026.

## 2. Does the Ministry of Information license advertising agencies or activities? How does it relate to the Ministry of Commerce and Industry (MOCI)?

### Takeaway
Yes, in part. The Ministry of Information runs an e-government service, "Issuing a new license for advertisement and publications for organizations", which needs a MOCI letter. MOCI therefore issues the commercial licence and activity, and the Ministry of Information adds a media-sector licence for regulated activities. Separately, MOCI's Consumer Protection Department licenses certain *individual ads*, such as those that collect third-party money. Under the new law, the advertising licence lasts 2 years (some sources say 5), and the Ministry must decide within 60 days.

### Cited Findings
- Kuwait Government Online lists a Ministry of Information service, "Issuing a new license for advertisement and publications for organizations". Requirements include a letter from MOCI for the required licence, the owner's civil ID and nationality, a PACI address number, and the organisation's civil number. One summary also lists a police record and a lease — [e.gov.kw (MEDIA)](https://e.gov.kw/sites/kgoenglish/Pages/Services/MEDIA/PublicityAdvertisingNewOrganaizations.aspx)
- Other Ministry of Information licensing services require "كتاب الجهات الرقابية موجه من وزارة التجارة والصناعة لوزارة الاعلام أو ترخيص ساري المدة من وزارة التجارة والصناعة", and renewals need the original Ministry of Information licence plus the MOCI licence — [e.gov.kw (MEDIA services)](https://e.gov.kw/sites/kgoArabic/Pages/Services/MEDIA/TranslationServiceIssuingPublicationPublishingLicenses.aspx)
- MOCI separately licenses publishing an advertisement that collects third-party money, through its Consumer Protection Department. It needs the commercial licence, signature authorisation and a copy of the ad. The ad must show the company name, address, phone and ad-licence number, and the ad period may not exceed one month — [e.gov.kw (MOCI)](https://e.gov.kw/sites/kgoenglish/Pages/Services/MOCI/LicenseApplicationPublishingAnnouncement.aspx)
- Buying ads on Kuwait TV and radio (state broadcaster) needs a commercial licence for the advertiser. This is handled by the Ministry of Information's Media Services & New Media Sector, Al Hamra Tower, 10th floor — [e.gov.kw](https://e.gov.kw/sites/kgoArabic/Pages/Services/MEDIA/AdvertisingThroughKuwaitRadioAndTV.aspx)
- New law: the applicant must be at least 18, the licence lasts 2 years and is renewable, and the Ministry has 60 days to decide, with silence counting as refusal for advertiser licences — [Wefaq Law](https://www.wefaqlaw.com/en/blog/kuwait-media-law-2026-what-it-means-for-influencers-companies-and-media-businesses); [kwt.one](https://kwt.one/media-law-advertisers/). **Conflict:** another source says media licences last 5 years and silence counts as *approval* for media activities generally; the advertiser-licence rule may differ — [Al-Hadath](https://www.alhadath.net/2026/09/02/الكويت-تقر-قانونا-جديدا-لتنظيم-المؤثرين-والاعلانات-الرقمية)
- Existing operators get six months after the Executive Regulations to regularise, or lose their licence — [ASAR](https://www.asarlegal.com/kuwaits-new-media-regulation-law-what-businesses-need-to-know/)
- Sept 2025 reporting on the draft: the controls would cover "كل إعلان مرئي أو مطبوع أو مقروء مع التأكد من ترخيص المنتج أو الخدمة", with **joint coordination between the Ministry of Information and MOCI** — [Al-Seyassah (Sept 2025, draft stage)](https://alseyassah.com/article/447643/)

### Inferences
- In practice, today: the startup needs a MOCI commercial licence with an advertising activity, plus Municipality ad licences (covered in earlier reports). The Ministry of Information's "advertisement and publications for organizations" licence appears aimed at publicity and publication businesses. Whether it is required for a DOOH screen seller is unconfirmed, and the MOCI licensing desk is the practical gatekeeper that would issue the "letter to the Ministry of Information" if one is needed.
- From about Apr 2027, a Ministry of Information advertiser licence under Art. 34 is plausibly required for the company selling ad time. Budget for it, and for each advertiser brand potentially needing its own licence.

### Gaps
- The exact MOCI ISIC activity code(s) for advertising (e.g. "دعاية وإعلان", "إعلانات خارجية") and whether MOCI conditions them on Ministry of Information approval were not found.
- The full e.gov.kw service page could not be opened, so the name of the licence type issued (e.g. "مكتب دعاية وإعلان") is unconfirmed.

## 3. Does the Ministry of Information pre-approve ad content? For which media? Street or LED screens?

### Takeaway
Yes, for **audiovisual works produced or shown by licensed production establishments**. Ministerial Resolution 13/2026 requires prior *ijaza* (إجازة) from the General Administration for Media Regulation, and Art. 9 sets conditions for approving the "production, display or distribution" of commercial ads. Kuwait TV/radio ads are also vetted by the Ministry as broadcaster. **No source found** that the Ministry pre-approves content on street, outdoor or LED screens. One marketing blog claims it does, but this is unverified.

### Cited Findings
- **Ministerial Resolution No. 13 of 2026** on regulating the approval (إجازة) of audiovisual works and national artistic works has 4 chapters and 21 articles. It requires prior approval from the General Administration for Media Regulation before producing or showing audiovisual works at artistic-production establishments licensed by the Ministry. It repeals Resolution 18/2015, as amended by 28/2020 — [Al-Anba](https://www.alanba.com.kw/1354121); [Al-Qabas](https://www.alqabas.com/article/5962785/); [Berwaz](https://www.berwaz.com.kw/تفاصيل-كاملة...وزارة-الإعلام-تصدر-قرارا/)
- Art. 9 of Resolution 13/2026 sets conditions for approving the production, display or distribution (إنتاج أو عرض أو توزيع) of commercial ads, including "عدم مخالفة الإعلان للتشريعات النافذة وأحكام النظام العام والآداب العامة". The resolution is grounded in Law 3/2006 and its executive regulation — [mesferlaw (Resolution 13/2026)](https://mesferlaw.com/وزارة-الاعلام-قرار-رقم-13-لسنة-2026-بشان-تنظي/)
- Approval terms: one year for production and 10 years for display — [Al-Rai](https://www.alraimedia.com/article/1762723/)
- TV/radio ads: Ministerial Decisions 992/2022 (TV ads) and 993/2022 (radio ads) were amended in January 2026 (Decision 2/2026 and a companion). The changes add a 25% surcharge for ads in live programming and redefine the "competent sector". The aim includes "compliance with technical and literary standards" — [Arab Times](https://www.arabtimesonline.com/news/kuwait-issues-amendments-to-tv-and-radio-advertising-regulations/); [Lexis Middle East](https://www.lexismiddleeast.com/news/2026-01-12_9/en)
- Unverified marketing blog claim: "the Ministry of Information reviews all proposed creative materials against cultural, religious and moral guidelines" for billboards — [media.co.uk blog](https://www.media.co.uk/blogs/blog/kuwait-static-megas-permits-billboard-regulations). Low reliability: a vendor blog with no citations.
- The Municipality's advertising regulation requires the **Municipality's advertisements committee** to approve designs and locations, plus other bodies' approvals depending on the ad type. Search summaries did not show a named Ministry of Information approval — [lawskw – Res. 599/2023 & 223/2024](https://lawskw.com/section/بلدية-الكويت-قرار-رقم-599-لسنة-2023-بشان-لائحة-الاعلانات...); [Al-Anba 2021 draft](https://www.alanba.com.kw/ar/kuwait-news/municipal-council/1066530/)

### Inferences
- A brand's *video creative* produced by a Ministry-licensed production house would go through Resolution 13/2026 approval as an audiovisual work. If the startup produces ads in-house for LED screens, it is unclear whether that counts as an "artistic production establishment" activity needing Ministry of Information licensing. That is a question for counsel.
- In practice today, outdoor content is controlled by the Municipality, through licence approval of the design and licence number display, not by the Ministry of Information.

### Gaps
- Full text of Resolution 13/2026, including whether "عرض" (display) covers public screens outside cinemas and TV. Not read.
- No Ministry of Information decision on "الشاشات الإعلانية" or digital out-of-home was found in any search.

## 4. Ministry of Information rules on social-media and influencer advertising (2023–2026): what they imply for digital ads

### Takeaway
Influencer and social-ad licensing was being drafted from 2025 and is now Chapter 8 of Decree-Law 102/2026. It covers licences, mandatory ad disclosure, bans on misleading ads, and coverage of hidden ads, reviews, gifts, contests and AI-generated content. The Ministry also gains monitoring and prosecution-referral powers. This shows the Ministry treats advertising as a *licensed activity of the advertiser*, regardless of channel.

### Cited Findings
- Sept 2025: the Ministry was preparing rules requiring influencers to obtain licences before promoting products, as part of the new media law. The draft was at the Fatwa & Legislation stage, and the Ministry would gain powers to monitor platforms and refer violations to the Public Prosecution — [Al-Seyassah](https://alseyassah.com/article/447643/); [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/world/gulf/kuwait/kuwait-to-require-licences-for-influencer-ads-under-new-media-law-1.500260303)
- Cabinet approval was reported on 1 Sept 2026. Influencers must be licensed and disclose paid or unpaid promotions aimed at Kuwaiti audiences. The law criminalises misleading ads and unlicensed funding meant to influence opinion — [Times Kuwait](https://timeskuwait.com/cabinet-approves-new-media-law-regulating-influencers-digital-advertising/)
- The law covers hidden ads, reviews, gifts, competitions and AI-generated content — [Arab Times](https://www.arabtimesonline.com/news/kuwaits-new-media-law-tightens-rules-for-influencers-advertising-ai-reviews-and-competitions/); [Al-Hadath](https://www.alhadath.net/2026/09/02/الكويت-تقر-قانونا-جديدا-لتنظيم-المؤثرين-والاعلانات-الرقمية)
- Personal accounts used for personal expression are exempt unless used for regular professional or commercial media or advertising activity — [ASAR](https://www.asarlegal.com/kuwaits-new-media-regulation-law-what-businesses-need-to-know/)
- July 2025 (a different context, charity work): contracting with celebrities, influencers or preachers needed prior Ministry approval — [Emarat Al Youm](https://www.emaratalyoum.com/politics/weekly-supplements/world-press/2025-07-01-1.1956603)
- Health-related: the tobacco and nicotine Ministerial Resolution 237/2026 bans online sales and advertising and allows blocking or removal of illegal online ads — [Arab Times](https://www.arabtimesonline.com/news/kuwait-tightens-tobacco-and-nicotine-rules-bans-online-sales-and-advertising/); [Al Tamimi (Eyes on 2026)](https://www.tamimi.com/our-knowledge/publications/eyes-on-2026/Articles/kuwait-tightens-public-health-and-digital-advertising-oversight-what-beauty-operators-brands-and-influencers-need-to-know-through-2026)

### Inferences
- The disclosure duty (label content as an ad) and the misleading-ad prohibition are channel-neutral in the summaries. A DOOH network should plan to (a) carry a visible "إعلان" label or ad-licence number, and (b) keep a record of which advertiser holds which licence. This is a low-cost hedge.

### Gaps
- No source confirms how "ad disclosure" is to be shown on non-social media.

## 5. Content restrictions and penalties

### Takeaway
Current law (Law 3/2006, Art. 21) bans content offending public morals, religion, the constitution or the Amir, among others. Fines for newspapers run KD 3,000–10,000. The new law adds general standards: accuracy, no false news, protection of children and privacy, no incitement. Fines are KD 1,000–50,000 for unlicensed advertising (Art. 55) and the same for undisclosed or misleading ads (Art. 56), with administrative sanctions up to closure and blocking. Resolution 13/2026 requires ads not to breach law, public order or public morals. No specific Ministry of Information rule on women's imagery or an Arabic-language requirement for ads was found.

### Cited Findings
- Law 3/2006 Art. 21 prohibitions include: content that offends public morals, incites against public order, or shows contempt for the constitution; and "التعرض لشخص صاحب السمو أمير البلاد بالنقد، أو أن ينسب له قول إلا بإذن خاص من الديوان الأميري". The penalty for a newspaper is a fine of KD 3,000–10,000, with the editor-in-chief and the author liable — [Kuwait Judicial Institute](https://www.kijls.moj.gov.kw/ar/sections/section4/sub3_section4/Releases/KIJSReleases/جرائم%20الإعلام%20والنشر.pdf)
- Decree-Law 102/2026 standards include respecting privacy and reputation, protecting children, accuracy, no false or misleading news or rumours, and no incitement to hatred, violence or crime — [Al-Jarida](https://www.aljarida.com/article/146388); [salahkw.com](https://salahkw.com/blog/qanun-tanzim-alilam-alkuwaiti-102-2026). Earlier project report: religion, public order and children standards — [Al Tamimi](https://www.tamimi.com/law-update/the-technology-issue-2026/articles/kuwaits-new-media-law-a-comprehensive-overhaul-of-the-media-regulatory-framework/)
- Art. 55: advertising without a licence carries a fine of KD 1,000–50,000. Art. 56: the same fine for an ad not identified as an ad, or with misleading or incomplete information. Art. 58: unlicensed media activity carries KD 1,000–10,000 plus closure — [kwt.one](https://kwt.one/media-law-advertisers/); [Mondaq/ASAR](https://www.mondaq.com/social-media/1853030/kuwaits-new-media-regulation-law-what-businesses-need-to-know)
- Additional measures: licence withdrawal, closure, content deletion, account or website blocking, confiscation, and restrictions on media appearances. Sanctions are graduated from warning to closure — [ASAR](https://www.asarlegal.com/kuwaits-new-media-regulation-law-what-businesses-need-to-know/)
- Resolution 13/2026 Art. 9: an ad must not breach "التشريعات النافذة وأحكام النظام العام والآداب العامة" — [mesferlaw](https://mesferlaw.com/وزارة-الاعلام-قرار-رقم-13-لسنة-2026-بشان-تنظي/)
- Municipality side (for comparison): an older draft required the ad language to match the commercial name, with translation into Arabic or English allowed. It is unconfirmed whether this survives in the in-force text — [Al-Anba 2021](https://www.alanba.com.kw/ar/kuwait-news/municipal-council/1066530/)

### Inferences
- For practical content policy, treat the following as off-limits on screens: religious offence, political or Amir-related content, foreign-state criticism (a long-standing Kuwaiti media prohibition, though the exact article was not retrieved here), indecency or immodest imagery, alcohol and pork (illegal products), tobacco and nicotine (Res. 237/2026), and unapproved health or real-estate claims.

### Gaps
- No specific Ministry of Information text was found on depictions of women, Arabic-language minimums, or foreign states in ads. These are customary practice, not verified articles.

## 6. Who approves outdoor ad content in practice, and how do agencies coordinate?

### Takeaway
**Kuwait Municipality is the primary and, by government direction, sole licensing authority for public advertisements**, excluding directional signs. Its advertisements committee approves design and location, and the licence number must be displayed. Sector regulators add product-specific clearances: MOCI for real estate and promotions, MOH for health, Traffic (MOI-Interior) for vehicles. The Ministry of Information has no documented role in outdoor ad approval today. A Cabinet directive in 2026 called for tighter oversight of campaign *content* on billboards, without naming the Ministry of Information.

### Cited Findings
- Earlier measures gave Kuwait Municipality full authority over licensing and regulating public advertisements, excluding directional signage. A later directive calls for a broader review of billboard sites and **tighter oversight of campaign content**. Two tender options were floated: one via the Kuwait Investment Authority, and one where the Municipality runs tenders with an outside consultancy — [Times Kuwait, "Kuwait moves to overhaul public advertising framework"](https://timeskuwait.com/kuwait-moves-to-overhaul-public-advertising-framework/) (date about April 2026 per one summary; exact date unconfirmed)
- Municipality inspections check the validity of licences for rooftop ads, **screens** and mall ads — [Al-Anba](https://www.alanba.com.kw/1196325); [Al-Jarida](https://www.aljarida.com/article/109371)
- Billboards must display the licence number with start and end dates — [Times Kuwait](https://timeskuwait.com/kuwait-municipality-sets-clear-rules-for-advertising-licenses/)
- Ad screens inside public buses and taxis are reported as a sign type regulated under the Municipality regulation 599/2023 — summarised from [Sarmad](https://sarmad.com/324214/) and [Al-Jarida](https://www.aljarida.com/article/132920/amp)
- The draft media-law controls (2025) envisaged joint Ministry of Information–MOCI coordination and verification that the advertised product or service is licensed — [Al-Seyassah](https://alseyassah.com/article/447643/)

### Inferences
- Practical approval chain for a moving LED ad today: (1) MOCI commercial licence with an advertising activity; (2) Municipality ad licence per vehicle or screen, with the design and content approved by its committee; (3) Traffic Department approval for vehicle-mounted screens; (4) sector clearance for regulated products (MOH, MOCI). From about April 2027, add (5) a Ministry of Information advertiser licence under Art. 34, and possibly display of the licence number or disclosure on the ad.
- Lowest-risk move: ask the Ministry of Information's General Administration for Media Regulation in writing whether a DOOH screen operator selling third-party ad time needs an Art. 34 licence, ideally during the Executive Regulations consultation window (Oct 2026–Apr 2027).

### Gaps
- No formal MoU or circular on coordination between the Municipality and the Ministry of Information for ad content was found.
- The MOH advertising pre-approval procedure for health ads was not found in this round.

## 7. 2024–2026 news on the Ministry of Information and outdoor/digital advertising

### Takeaway
2026 has been busy: TV and radio ad rules were amended (Jan), Resolution 13/2026 on audiovisual and ad approval was issued (about Apr), a Cabinet directive on billboard sites and content was issued (about Apr), Cabinet approved the media law (Sept), and Decree-Law 102/2026 was published (4 Oct).
