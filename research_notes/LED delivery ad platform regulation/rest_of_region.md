# Rest of Region (UAE, KSA, Qatar, Bahrain, Oman, Jordan): Regulation of LED/digital ads on delivery boxes, bikes and shop windows

Research date: 16 Nov 2026. Method: web search; direct fetches of rta.ae, balady.gov.sa, invidis.com and logisticsmiddleeast.com failed because this research session's network settings blocked those hosts. Many findings therefore come from search-result summaries of official pages, or from secondary/vendor sources. These are flagged below. Kuwait and Egypt are excluded by design.

## Q1. Who licenses outdoor/mobile/vehicle advertising, and are digital/illuminated screens on vehicles/motorcycles allowed?

### Takeaway
Every country requires a municipal or transport-authority permit for ads on vehicles. Digital screens on moving vehicles are explicitly permitted and already operating in Dubai (Flyby/Talabat delivery boxes) and Jeddah (MediaGo/JTC car screens). Oman's government portal says vehicle e-screens must be off while the vehicle is moving and charges about OMR 2,500, which makes Oman the most hostile market for the model. In Dubai, permits run through the RTA and a consolidated Dubai Municipality/Mada Media single window. Saudi Arabia needs municipal (Balady) plus traffic approval for vehicle signs.

### Cited Findings

**UAE – Dubai**
- The RTA's "Technical Manual – Managing delivery services through electronic platforms" has a section on advertising on delivery boxes. The delivery company applies for the advertising permit through the RTA website or app. The manual also sets box specifications, including Dubai Municipality food-safety requirements for food delivery. (Only an excerpt was seen through search; the full text was not fetched.) — [RTA manual (PDF)](https://rta.ae/links/licensing/delivery-services-manual-en.pdf)
- Secondary guides put the RTA fee for delivery-box and side branding at about AED 250 per vehicle per year. They say the application needs an all-sides design and mock-up plus a Dubai trade licence, and that the permit is valid for one year. Arabic content must be at least 50% of the advertising, and the Arabic company name must match the trade licence. (Vendor blogs; unverified.) — [Dubizzle guide](https://www.dubizzle.com/blog/cars/rta-vehicle-advertisement-permit-dubai/); [Printajo](https://printajo.com/blog/dubai-vehicle-branding-permit-laws-fees/); [Automobili 2026 guide](https://www.automobilimotors.com/blog/rta-vehicle-advertisement-permit-dubai-2026-how-to-apply-requirements-fees-automobili); [ChannelIAM](https://en.channeliam.com/2024/11/02/vehicle-advertising-permit-dubai-guide/)
- Fines reported under the manual (2022-era report): AED 500 per vehicle for a box that fails food-safety and security requirements, and AED 200 for not keeping the vehicle or box clean. — [Eveons summary](https://www.eveons.com/blogs/news/all-you-need-to-know-about-rtas-new-rules-fines-for-delivery-bikes-in-dubai)
- Fixed outdoor and shop advertising: a 2026 guide says Dubai consolidated outdoor-ad permitting onto a single platform, Mada Media, acting for the RTA and Dubai Municipality. Mada Media lists the electronic-screen permit fee at AED 1,000 per m² per year. Dubai Municipality's Smart Advertising Single Window handles street-facing shop signage, and Trakhees handles signage in DP World zones. DEWA issues a separate NOC for any illuminated or electrically connected sign. DM sign permits reportedly take 4–6 weeks. (Mostly vendor sources; no source specifically covers LED screens placed inside shop windows facing the street.) — [BusinessDubai guide](https://www.businessdubai.ae/blogs/signage-advertising-company-setup-dubai); [Mada Media e-screen permit](https://madamedia.com/services/permit-to-advertise-on-buildings/advertisement-permit-for-electronic-screen); [Metroplus](https://www.metroplusads.com/dubai-signage-permits-approvals-guide/); [Bebright](https://www.bebright.global/blogs/outdoor-led-screen-permit-dubai-guide/)

**UAE – Federal (Media Council)**
- The UAE Media Council launched an "Advertiser Permit" on 30 July 2025 for individuals who publish promotional content, paid or unpaid, on social media. It became mandatory from 1 Feb 2026. Holders must follow 20 content standards and display their permit number, and penalties apply under Cabinet Resolution No. 42 of 2025. The permit targets social-media individuals, not OOH operators. More than 1,800 permits had been issued by Sept 2025. — [WAM](https://www.wam.ae/en/article/bkxzoe1-uae-media-council-launches-advertiser-permit); [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/uae/new-uae-law-advertiser-permit-now-mandatory-for-influencers-and-creators-for-social-media-1.500427938); [Al Tamimi](https://www.tamimi.com/law-update-articles/uae-introduces-mandatory-advertiser-permit-for-content-creators); [The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/news/uae/2025/09/10/uae-issues-more-than-1800-advertiser-permits-for-influencers)

**UAE – Abu Dhabi and Sharjah**
- No usable source was found on Abu Dhabi ITC/DMT rules for delivery-box advertising, or on Sharjah Municipality permits for digital vehicle or window screens. See Gaps.

**Saudi Arabia**
- Balady (Ministry of Municipalities and Housing, MOMAH) issues an "operational licence for an advertising or promotional sign". It is electronic-only, needs an active commercial register, is listed as free, and takes about 1–10 days. — [Balady service](https://www.balady.gov.sa/en/services/issuing-operational-license-advertising-or-promotional-sign); [MOMAH listing](https://momah.gov.sa/en/node/15056)
- Balady's FAQ (search summary) says signs on vehicles require approval from both the municipality and the traffic authorities. — [Balady FAQ](https://balady.gov.sa/en/faq/it-required-attach-building-permit-when-issuing-operational-license-advertising-or-promotional)
- MOMAH's rules for advertising boards list "boards on commercial vehicles within city limits for the same owner of the vehicle" as a covered category. This suggests vehicle ads may be limited to the vehicle owner's own brand, which matters for a third-party ad network. (Uncertain; full text not read.) — [Rules for Regulating Advertising Boards (Qanoniah)](https://qanoniah.com/en/File/mJy4DGw9da7v68J78Qv65RrKX-%D9%82%D9%88%D8%A7%D8%B9%D8%AF-%D8%AA%D9%86%D8%B8%D9%8A%D9%85-%D9%84%D9%88%D8%AD%D8%A7%D8%AA-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AF%D8%B9%D8%A7%D9%8A%D8%A9-%D9%88%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A5%D8%B9%D9%84%D8%A7%D9%86); [Balady news on updated billboard regulations](https://balady.gov.sa/en/news/ministry-municipality-and-housing-issues-updated-regulations-advertising-billboards-saudi)
- Balady also offers a "temporary advertising permit". — [Balady temporary permit](https://balady.gov.sa/en/services/temporary-advertising-permit)
- MediaGo launched a mobile DOOH platform in Jeddah (about Oct 2026) with digital screens on moving vehicles, built with Jeddah Transport Company (JTC). MediaGo describes itself as the "first Saudi company licensed and regulated" for screens on moving vehicles and says it launched a vehicle-ad permit portal with JTC. (This is the company's own claim; no TGA or MOMAH confirmation was found.) — [Campaign ME](https://campaignme.com/mediago-mobile-dooh-platform-jeddah/); [LinkedIn profile referencing Mediago](https://sa.linkedin.com/in/sami-wali-75268830)

**Qatar**
- Advertising is governed by Law No. 1 of 2012 on advertisements, statues and memorials, substantially amended by Law No. 5 of 2025. A licence from the competent municipality is required before display, along with fees and an insurance deposit. Fines can reach QAR 30,000 plus removal costs. — [Al Tamimi on Law 5/2025](https://turtl.tamimi.com/story/law-update-issue-378-real-estate-and-construction-and-hotels-and-leisure/page/21); [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/real-estate/1649916/qatar-enacts-law-no-5-of-2025-major-reforms-to-the-regulation-of-advertisements-statues-and-memorials)
- Ad licences are available through the Single Window, with a stated 3-day turnaround (2022 announcement). The Ministry of Municipality's 2022 guide bans ads at places of worship and on traffic signboards. LED screens and vehicle ads carry separate charges. — [MOCI](https://www.moci.gov.qa/?p=27430); [Peninsula 2022](https://thepeninsulaqatar.com/article/04/11/2022/moci-launches-new-service-for-advertising-licences-through-single-window-platform); [Peninsula guide](https://s.thepeninsula.qa/article/31/05/2022/ads-not-allowed-at-places-of-worship-traffic-signboards); [QatarLiving](https://www.qatarliving.com/.well-known/article/municipality-signage-permits-qatar-businesses)

**Bahrain**
- The Ministry of Municipalities Affairs issues commercial vehicle-advertising permits at BHD 20 per m², with a 7-working-day process. The application needs vehicle ownership and insurance documents. Non-commercial vehicle-ad permits are free and go through specialist review, an Advertising Committee and engineer approval. — [Bahrain.bh service](https://www.bahrain.bh/wps/portal/en/BNP/ServicesCatalogue/GSX-UI-PServiceDetails?psID=2412); [mun.gov.bh](https://www.mun.gov.bh/newportal/en/municipal-affairs/services/issue-non-commercial-advertisement-permit-vehicle)

**Oman**
- The Gov.om service "Add a Mobile Advertisement Electronic Screen on a Commercial Vehicle" requires that the screen not operate while the vehicle is moving, and that the vehicle not park near highways or main roads. The listed fee is OMR 2,500. This conflicts directly with an ads-while-riding model. — [Gov.om](https://gov.om/en/w/add-a-mobile-advertisement-electronic-screen-on-a-commercial-vehicle)
- Royal Oman Police traffic regulations bar vehicle advertising without a permit from the Directorate. Municipal ad rules require a site permit plus separate content approval, with literary Arabic as the main language. — [ROP traffic law PDF](https://oman.om/docs/default-source/default-document-library/traffic-law.pdf?sfvrsn=ecadb4e4_2); [Curtis Oman Law Blog (2014)](https://omanlawblog.curtis.com/2014/04/compliance-with-advertising-regulations.html)

**Jordan**
- The Greater Amman Municipality (GAM) advertising licensing regulation (2015/2016 versions) requires a licence for advertisements within GAM limits, under instructions issued by the council. It lists "advertising signs on vehicles and buses" as a licensable type and makes unlicensed advertising a violation. — [JordanianLaw – GAM ad licensing regulation](https://jordanianlaw.com/?p=2705); [JordanianLaw – 2016 version](https://jordanianlaw.com/?p=2780)
- The 2019 GAM instructions for mobile vehicles used for trades require that trade names on vehicles comply with the advertising regulation. — [Jordan-lawyer.com](https://jordan-lawyer.com/2019/07/01/rules-of-movable-cars-work-in-amman/)

### Inferences
- Dubai is the most proven market. A precedent operator (Flyby) runs moving LED delivery-box screens with a major app (Talabat), so the RTA delivery-box advertising permit path evidently accommodates screens. The likely permit holder is the delivery company or fleet operator, not the rider, which pushes the business toward B2B deals with fleets and apps.
- In KSA, the "same owner of the vehicle" wording and the dual municipality-plus-traffic approval suggest a third-party ad network may need a specific licence category or a partnership with a licensed transport company. MediaGo's partnership with JTC hints at this approach.
- Oman's "screen off while moving" condition would limit screens to parked or stationary display there.
- Shop-window screens are likely to be treated as building/shop signage. In Dubai that means the DM/Mada Media e-screen permit (about AED 1,000/m²/yr) plus a DEWA NOC; elsewhere it means a municipal sign licence.

### Gaps
- The full RTA manual text could not be fetched (host blocked), so box dimensions, brightness and illumination limits, and whether the manual explicitly addresses electronic screens are not confirmed.
- Abu Dhabi (ITC/DMT) and Sharjah (Municipality / Sharjah RTA) rules for delivery-box ads and digital vehicle screens: no sources found.
- KSA: the text of the updated MOMAH advertising regulations on digital or moving screens was not retrieved, nor any TGA rule on box branding. Whether third-party ads on a rider's box are allowed is uncertain.
- Qatar, Bahrain and Jordan: nothing specific on illuminated or digital screens on motorcycles.

## Q2. Delivery-rider rules affecting box modifications or branding

### Takeaway
Dubai's RTA delivery manual regulates boxes (safety, food hygiene, cleanliness, reflective stickers) and their advertising. UAE-wide rules from January 2027 require approved loading equipment and ban backpacks. Saudi TGA rules since April 2024 limit freelance delivery to Saudis and require non-Saudis to work through licensed companies in a unified uniform. In practice, branding and box changes in both markets need the fleet company's or app's cooperation.

### Cited Findings
- Dubai RTA manual: covers box specifications, Dubai Municipality food-safety rules and advertising permits on boxes. Separate guidance says the RTA strictly regulates reflective safety stickers on boxes. — [RTA manual](https://rta.ae/links/licensing/delivery-services-manual-en.pdf); [Dubizzle](https://www.dubizzle.com/blog/cars/rta-vehicle-advertisement-permit-dubai/)
- UAE: implementing regulations of the 2024 traffic law (effective January 2027, per one report) bar pillion passengers on delivery bikes, ban backpacks for carrying orders, and require approved equipment meeting official loading requirements. (Secondary source.) — [Global Filipino Magazine](https://theglobalfilipinomagazine.com/no-more-passengers-on-delivery-bikes-as-new-uae-traffic-rules-start-in-january/)
- UAE midday summer ban (15 Jun–15 Sep): riders cannot be forced to work, and platforms are limited to 3 orders and 60 minutes of delivery during the break. — [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/amp/story/uae/transport/delivery-riders-can-choose-to-work-or-stop-during-uaes-midday-ban-1.500595610)
- KSA TGA phase 1 (April 2024): non-Saudis working on delivery apps must join licensed light-transport companies in four regions, and freelance work is limited to Saudis. TGA approved a unified uniform for non-Saudi drivers in two models (company-employed, and app-based establishments). Apps must verify drivers' faces through a TGA-linked system. — [Entarabi](https://entarabi.com/en/2024/04/tga-begins-implementing-the-first-phase-of-decisions-to-regulate-the-order-delivery-sector-in-saudi-arabia/); [The Workers Rights](https://www.theworkersrights.com/riyadh-food-delivery-rider-registration-2026/)
- Saudi freelance riders can get HRDF support, which requires TGA accreditation and a freelance certificate for "Delivery Services". — [HRDF](https://www.hrdf.org.sa/en/products-and-services/programs/individuals/enable/financial-aid-for-delivery-app-workers/); [Arabian Business](https://www.arabianbusiness.com/jobs/saudi-arabia-announces-financial-aid-for-delivery-app-workers)
- An unverified report says a Riyadh "Home Delivery Permit" via Balady applies from July 2025. — [The Workers Rights](https://www.theworkersrights.com/riyadh-food-delivery-rider-registration-2026/)

### Inferences
- In KSA, uniforms and branding for non-Saudi riders are TGA-standardised and controlled by the employing company. Adding third-party ads to boxes probably needs the licensed company's sign-off and possibly TGA acceptance. Saudi freelancers are the more direct partner pool, though a smaller one.
- The UAE "approved loading equipment" rule may create a box-certification requirement that a screen-equipped box must meet.

### Gaps
- No primary TGA text on box branding or third-party advertising was found.
- No rules found for Qatar, Bahrain, Oman or Jordan on delivery boxes.

## Q3. Labour/visa constraints on paying expatriate riders for side income

### Takeaway
In the UAE and KSA, expatriate riders are generally visa-sponsored employees of a licensed courier or transport company. Paying them directly for "hosting" a screen risks being unauthorised work. The safer structure is to pay the fleet company or app, or in KSA to pay Saudi freelancers directly.

### Cited Findings
- UAE: riders are typically employed and visa-sponsored by a licensed courier company, and the employer obtains the MoHRE work permit. One jobs site says no separate part-time visa exists for riders. (Low-quality sources.) — [BusinessDubai](https://www.businessdubai.ae/blogs/delivery-partner-ecommerce-dubai); [WorkWise Visa](https://www.workwisevisa.com/blogs/dubai-delivery-rider-warehouse-logistics-work-permit-2026)
- KSA: non-Saudi delivery workers must be attached to licensed companies, and freelancing is restricted to Saudis. — [Entarabi](https://entarabi.com/en/2024/04/tga-begins-implementing-the-first-phase-of-decisions-to-regulate-the-order-delivery-sector-in-saudi-arabia/)
- One unverified agency post claims Saudi work permits for delivery riders are suspended indefinitely. — [Falisha Manpower](https://falishamanpower.com/no-work-permit-for-delivery-riders-in-saudi-arabia-what-you-need-to-know)

### Inferences
- The "pay riders" element of the model probably has to run through employers or fleet operators in the UAE and KSA, as a revenue share to the company. In KSA, direct payments are workable only for Saudi freelancers.

### Gaps
- No authoritative source was found on whether a UAE rider could hold a MoHRE part-time or secondary permit for screen-hosting income, or on how GCC labour authorities would classify a passive "box rental" payment as opposed to work.
- Qatar, Bahrain, Oman and Jordan expatriate side-income rules were not researched (call budget ran out).

## Q4. Content rules / pre-approval and data privacy (GPS / audience measurement)

### Takeaway
Content: Dubai requires at least 50% Arabic in vehicle ads (secondary source). Oman requires separate content approval with Arabic as the main language. Social-media advertiser permits exist in the UAE (Media Council) and KSA (Mawthooq, GCAM) but target individuals online, not OOH screens. Privacy: the UAE PDPL is in force, but its Executive Regulations still appear unissued as of 2026. The Saudi PDPL is in force, with conflicting reports on whether location data is "sensitive". Qatar (Law 13/2016) and Bahrain (Law 30/2018) both regulate personal data and cross-border transfers.

### Cited Findings
- UAE Media Council advertiser permit: 20 mandatory content standards. — [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/uae/new-uae-law-advertiser-permit-now-mandatory-for-influencers-and-creators-for-social-media-1.500427938); [Khaleej Times on vape-promotion warnings](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/uae/government/uae-media-council-warns-influencers-against-covert-vape-promotions)
- KSA Mawthooq (GCAM/GMedia): SAR 15,000 for 3 years, covering online ads in all formats. Licensees must follow content, advertising and age-rating controls and remove content when requested. — [Arab News](https://www.arabnews.com/node/2176661/media); [Saudipedia](https://saudipedia.com/en/what-is-the-mawthooq-license); [Clyde & Co](https://www.clydeco.com/en/insights/2022/09/new-social-media-requirements-for-saudi-arabia)
- Oman: municipal content approval is separate from site approval, with literary Arabic as the main language. — [Curtis](https://omanlawblog.curtis.com/2014/04/compliance-with-advertising-regulations.html)
- UAE PDPL (Federal Decree-Law 45/2021): most 2026 sources say the Executive Regulations had not been issued. Some vendor pages claim otherwise, citing unverifiable decision numbers. — [Business-CISO](https://business-ciso.com/blog/uae-pdpl-executive-regulations-status); [Orentara](https://orentara.com/en/insights/uae-pdpl-executive-regulations-status)
- Saudi PDPL: guides conflict on whether location data is sensitive (the 2022 amended draft removed it) and on data-localisation obligations. — [Global Privacy Blog](https://www.globalprivacyblog.com/2022/11/saudi-arabia-issues-amended-data-protection-law-for-consultation/); [O AI](https://oai.sa/blog/data-residency-in-saudi-arabia)
- Qatar PDPPL (Law 13/2016) covers electronically processed personal data in Qatar (excluding QFC), with transfer rules in Art. 15. — [NCSA](https://ncsa.gov.qa/en/pages/personal-data-privacy-personal-data-privacy-protection-law); [DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiperdataprotection.com/countries/qatar/law.html)
- Bahrain PDPL (Law 30/2018) has been in force since 1 Aug 2019, with 10 implementing resolutions in March 2022. Transfers are restricted to adequate jurisdictions. — [DLA Piper Bahrain](https://www.dlapiperdataprotection.com/?t=law&c=BH); [Trowers](https://www.trowers.com/insights/2019/december/pdpl---bahrains-data-protection-regime)

### Inferences
- Rider GPS tracking for proof-of-play and impression counting is personal data about the rider under all of these laws, so it needs a lawful basis and notice to riders. Camera-based audience measurement would raise heavier issues. In KSA and Qatar, local hosting or transfer controls should be planned for.
- Expect municipal pre-approval of ad creative in Oman. In KSA, sensitive categories may face similar review, but no source confirms OOH creative pre-approval in KSA.

### Gaps
- No source confirms whether the Dubai RTA, Mada Media or Balady require per-campaign creative pre-approval for rotating digital content on vehicle screens.
- Oman's PDPL (Royal Decree 6/2022) and Jordan's PDPL (Law 24/2023) were not researched within budget.

## Q5. Notable existing players

### Takeaway
Flyby (UAE–Germany) is the direct precedent: LED "Smart Delivery Boxes" on Talabat bikes in Dubai and Abu Dhabi, now sold via Yango Ads (Nov 2026). MediaGo plus JTC (Jeddah) does car-mounted DOOH. In Dubai, RTA/DTC taxi and limo screens are concessions (ADTECH 7 for limos; Hala and Binary Media for in-taxi screens), and bus ads are a concession (Sky Blue Media).

### Cited Findings
- Flyby operates in Dubai and Abu Dhabi, with R&D in Munich. Its Smart Delivery Boxes include telematics and AI rider-safety monitoring. Yango Ads partnered with Flyby to sell its mobile screens UAE-wide (Nov 2026). — [Invidis](https://invidis.com/news/2026/11/mobile-dooh-yango-ads-and-flyby-bring-mobile-dooh-advertising-to-uae-streets/); [MENA Startup Digest](https://menastartupdigest.com/yango-ads-partners-with-flyby-to-bring-mobile-digital-advertising-to-uae-streets/)
- Talabat deployed the smart LED delivery boxes across its Dubai bike fleet, with ads shown only while bikes are moving (article dated about mid-2025). — [Logistics Middle East](https://www.logisticsmiddleeast.com/news/talabat-rolls-out-smart-delivery-ads-in-dubai)
- MediaGo plus Jeddah Transport Company: mobile DOOH screens on moving vehicles in Jeddah. — [Campaign ME](https://campaignme.com/mediago-mobile-dooh-platform-jeddah/)
- Dubai Taxi (DTC) and ADTECH 7 (2023): ad screens in 100+ limousines. RTA rolled out 4,500 in-taxi interactive screens (2024) after a 2022 pilot with Hala and Binary Media. RTA trialled digital ads on taxi windows (2019). — [Dubai Media Office](https://mediaoffice.ae/en/news/2023/may/27-05/dtc-signs-agreement-with-adtech-7-to-operate-advertising-screens-on-limousine-fleet); [WAM](https://www.wam.ae/en/article/b28108m-rta-rolls-out-in-car-interactive-screens-for); [The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/uae/transport/coming-to-a-dubai-taxi-near-you-digital-adverts-on-windows-1.919394)
- Sky Blue Media holds a 5-year exclusive RTA bus-advertising contract from 1 Sep 2023. — [Campaign ME](https://campaignme.com/going-digital-in-dubai/)

### Inferences
- The regional pattern is concession or partnership with the fleet owner (an app, a transport company, or a government taxi operator) rather than direct contracts with individual drivers. Flyby partners with Talabat, and MediaGo with JTC. A newcomer in Dubai competes directly with Flyby and Yango Ads.

### Gaps
- Flyby's specific RTA permit basis and its rider-compensation model were not confirmed (source hosts blocked).
- No delivery-box screen operator was found in KSA, Qatar, Bahrain, Oman or Jordan.
