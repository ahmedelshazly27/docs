## 1. Outdoor advertising: Law 208 of 2020 and its executive regulations

### Takeaway
Law 208/2020 (Regulation of Advertisements on Public Roads) requires a licence for any ad or sign visible to road users. It is run by the National Agency for Regulating Advertisements on Public Roads (NAPRA), which reports to the Prime Minister, and licences are issued by the "competent body": the local unit or governorate, the NUCA city authority, or the Roads & Bridges Authority. The licence fee is capped at EGP 10,000 and lasts up to 3 years. The April 2026 NAPRA lighting rules apply explicitly to digital screens and shopfront displays. That makes them the most important current rule for illuminated storefront screens. I found no source saying Law 208 covers ads on moving vehicles. Those appear to sit under the Traffic Law instead (see section 2).

### Cited Findings
- President Sisi ratified the Law Regulating Advertisements on Public Roads in early December 2020 — [Masrawy, 4 Dec 2020](https://www.masrawy.com/news/news_egypt/details/2020/12/4/1924720/السيسي-يصدق-على-قانون-تنظيم-الإعلانات-في-الطرق-العامة-); full text on the Court of Cassation portal — [cc.gov.eg](https://www.cc.gov.eg/legislation_single?id=406929)
- Art. 1 defines "advertisement or sign" (الإعلان أو اللافتة) as a visual product prepared for display or publication to carry an advertising, news, promotional or informational message to road users. It may be written, drawn or made of any material. The same article defines the "competent body" as the local units, the city authorities of the New Urban Communities Authority (NUCA), the General Authority for Roads & Bridges, or any other body with jurisdiction over the ad's location — [cc.gov.eg](https://www.cc.gov.eg/legislation_single?id=406929); [El Watan](https://www.elwatannews.com/news/details/5103598); [Youm7, 4 Dec 2020](https://www.youm7.com/story/2020/12/4/تعرف-على-مواد-القانون-الجديد-للإعلانات/5097251)
- The law creates a single national agency under the Prime Minister that brings together all legislation on signs and ads, both inside local units and on all types of roads. Advertising provisions in other legislation were repealed (search summary of Masrawy and Amwal Al Ghad coverage) — [Masrawy 2021](https://www.masrawy.com/news/news_egypt/details/2021/5/9/2019872/-3-وزراء-يناقشون-مشروع-اللائحة-التنفيذية-لقانون-تنظيم-الإعلانات-على-الطرق)
- The law applies to all advertisers: individuals, companies and agencies. It does not apply to ads by public bodies or state-owned companies — [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/advertising-marketing-branding/1426214/advertising-agencies-in-egypt-governing-laws-and-restrictions); [El Watan](https://www.elwatannews.com/news/details/5103598)
- Art. 3: no ad or sign may be placed without a licence from the competent body — [Youm7, 15 Feb 2026](https://www.youm7.com/story/2026/2/15/مش-كل-إعلان-محتاج-ترخيص-اعرف-الحالات-المستثناة-بالقانون/7303342)
- The licence fee is capped at EGP 10,000. Licences last up to 3 years and can be renewed. The competent body can cancel a licence for planning, traffic or public-appearance reasons, and pays no compensation for damage to the ad — [Youm7, 4 Dec 2020](https://www.youm7.com/story/2020/12/4/تعرف-على-مواد-القانون-الجديد-للإعلانات/5097251); [Masrawy, 10 Dec 2020 ("رسوم 10 آلاف جنيه")](https://www.masrawy.com/news/news_egypt/details/2020/12/10/1928342/رسوم-10-آلاف-جنيه-10-معلومات-عن-قانون-تنظيم-الإعلانات-على-الطرق-العامة-ضوابط-وعقوبات-)

  - These rules relaxed a March 2026 Cabinet directive that shut off illuminated roadside ads during an energy crisis — [EgyptToday](https://www.egypttoday.com/Article/1/146802/Egypt-sets-new-controls-on-outdoor-advertising-lighting-to-balance)

### Inferences
- A storefront screen that faces the street and shows ads is very likely an "advertisement or sign" aimed at road users under Art. 1. It therefore needs a Law 208 licence from the hay or district (local unit) or, in New Cairo, Sheikh Zayed and similar cities, from the NUCA city authority. Showing third-party brands is not covered by any Art. 7 exemption.
- The April 2026 lighting rules name "shopfront displays" and "digital screens" directly. A street-facing LED film screen would have to switch off between midnight and 5 a.m. off tourist zones, and possibly between 7 a.m. and 5 p.m. as well, unless it has automatic dimming sensors at 37.5% and 4%. This could hit daytime inventory hard and needs legal confirmation.
- Law 208 is about fixed signs on public roads. I found nothing applying it to ads on moving vehicles. Vehicle ads are regulated by Traffic Law Art. 69 bis (section 2), and it is unclear whether a dual-licence requirement exists.

## 2. Vehicle ads: Traffic Law 66/1973, as amended by Law 121/2008 and Law 161/2021

### Takeaway
Since Law 161/2021 added Art. 69 bis, any ad on a vehicle needs a licence from the competent traffic department. The fee is capped at EGP 10,000 per licence, and licences last up to 3 years. Applications for third-party ads must go through a Ministry of Interior-approved advertising company. That company must be a joint-stock company with at least EGP 30 million in issued capital. Fines run from EGP 1,000 to 5,000 per violation, and traffic licences can be cancelled. Companies and shops advertising their own name or activity on their own vehicles are exempt, which is why Talabat-branded boxes are lawful. Third-party ads on rider bikes are not exempt. No source was found that addresses motorcycles specifically, or illuminated or LED screens on vehicles.

### Inferences
- Paying riders to put third-party ad screens on their delivery boxes falls squarely under Art. 69 bis. Each bike needs a traffic-department ad licence, applied for through an Interior-approved joint-stock ad company with EGP 30m+ capital. A startup would either need that capital and approval or would have to partner with an approved company. Without a licence, each rider faces EGP 1,000–5,000 fines and possible cancellation of the bike's licence. That is a material rider-side risk.
- A box is a fixture on the vehicle, so it is very likely to count as "on the vehicle". No source distinguishes boxes from bodywork.
- Platform-branded boxes (Talabat, Breadfast) fit the own-name exemption only for the platform's own fleet. Many riders are independent contractors who own their bikes, so the exemption's fit is uncertain even for the platform's own branding.
- The Traffic Law's general rules on non-standard and dazzling lights may limit lit LED screens on motorcycles, especially rear-facing ones that could be mistaken for signal lights, but I found no specific text.

## 3. Shopfront screens: local permits, third-party ads, shop licensing

### Takeaway
A street-facing ad screen on a shopfront is most likely a Law 208 "sign/advertisement" needing a licence from the local unit (hay) or the NUCA city authority, and it is caught by the April 2026 lighting curfew on "shopfront displays". I found no specific rule that bars a shop from hosting third-party ads, but third-party commercial ads are not exempt under Art. 7. Building Law 119/2008 and shop-licensing specifics were not confirmed in this session.

### Cited Findings
- The "competent body" for licensing includes local units and NUCA city authorities, depending on location — [cc.gov.eg](https://www.cc.gov.eg/legislation_single?id=406929)
- The licensing procedure for a sign or ad on public roads is described — [Parlmany](https://www.parlmany.com/News/7/536121/إجراءات-للحصول-على-ترخيص-لوضع-إعلان-أو-لافتة-بالطرق-العامة)
- The April 2026 lighting rules reportedly cover "shopfront displays" and "shopfront lighting", which must be off from midnight to 5 a.m. on main and internal roads — [Ahram Online](https://english.ahram.org.eg/NewsContentP/1/567028/Egypt/Egypt-introduces-new-billboard-lighting-regulation.aspx)
- Egypt has introduced digital shop licensing with a three-month approval deadline — [Ahram Online](https://english.ahram.org.eg/News/573679.aspx) (shop licensing generally, Law 154/2019 context; detail not retrieved)

### Inferences
- Paying shop owners to host third-party screens probably makes the screen operator an "advertiser" that needs a Law 208 licence for each site. Unlicensed screens risk removal plus fines of 2–3 times the cost of the works (section 5).
- Screens fully inside the shop and not aimed at the street may fall outside Law 208. That is unconfirmed.

## 4. Content rules

### Takeaway
Ad content is governed by Consumer Protection Law 181/2018 (no misleading ads; consumer communications in Arabic), Law 206/2017 on health product advertising (prior licence, possible prison), and SCMR's controls. SCMR Decision 9/2025 requires Egyptian Drug Authority (EDA) registration and approval numbers to be shown for medical product ads. Whether SCMR's jurisdiction reaches DOOH screens on bikes or shopfronts is unclear.

### Cited Findings
- Law 181/2018: an ad is misleading if it creates a false impression about characteristics, origin, quality, price or effect, including through exaggeration or omission. Unsubstantiated comparisons are barred — [Chambers Advertising & Marketing 2025 – Egypt](https://practiceguides.chambers.com/practice-guides/advertising-marketing-2025/egypt)
- Consumer-facing communications, including ads, must be in Arabic or in Arabic plus another language — [Lexology](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=0dea9950-f50e-4666-952b-d490ab77432f)
- Fines for misleading ads: EGP 50,000–1,000,000 per Lexology, versus 50,000–2,000,000 per Alzayat. **The sources conflict** — [Lexology](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=0dea9950-f50e-4666-952b-d490ab77432f); [Alzayat Firm](https://alzayatfirm.com/advertising-standards-and-prohibited-content-in-egypt/)
- The Consumer Protection Agency may require correction and may suspend an ad for up to 7 days if it is not corrected within 3 days, or if it poses a health or safety danger — [Consortio translation of Law 181/2018](https://consortiolawfirm.com/egyptian-consumer-protection-law-181-2018-english-translation/)
- SCMR Decision 9/2025 amended its Controls and Standards regulation to cover ads for health services, medical products, food products and competitions. Medical products must be EDA-registered, the ad must be EDA-approved, and the registration or approval number must be shown throughout. The content must match the EDA approval exactly. Health services must show their professional licence number, and patient images are banned — [Masrawy, 4 Feb 2025](https://www.masrawy.com/news/news_egypt/details/2025/2/4/2721608/-الأعلى-للإعلام-يصدر-ضوابط-إعلانات-المستحضرات-الطبية-والمنتجات-الغذائية-والمسابقات); [Youm7, 9 Mar 2025 (Gazette publication)](https://www.youm7.com/story/2025/3/9/الجريدة-الرسمية-تنشر-قرار-الأعلى-للإعلام-بتعديل-ضوابط-الإعلانات/6911823). Sources differ on the effective date (1 Mar 2025 vs 1 Mar 2026).
- SCMR says it is coordinating with the EDA against chaotic medical-product ads (Mar 2026) — [Youm7, 29 Mar 2026](https://www.youm7.com/story/2026/3/29/الأعلى-للإعلام-تصدينا-لفوضى-إعلانات-المنتجات-الطبية-بالتنسيق-مع-هيئة/7357793)

## 5. Penalties and enforcement

### Takeaway
Penalties are moderate in money but come with removal and licence-cancellation powers. Under Law 208, the fine is 2–3 times the cost of the works, plus removal at the violator's expense and double fees. Under Traffic Law Art. 69 bis, fines are EGP 1,000–5,000 per violation plus possible cancellation of the vehicle licence. Content breaches carry fines up to EGP 1–2 million (consumer law) and prison (health ads).

### Cited Findings
- Law 208 Art. 9: a fine of 2 to 3 times the cost of the works, removal of the ad, and payment of double fees. Removal can be done at the violator's expense — [Youm7, 29 Oct 2024](https://www.youm7.com/story/2024/10/29/الغرامة-والإزاله-عقوبة-وضع-الإعلانات-المخالفة-بالطرق-العامة/6755370); [Youm7, 28 Dec 2024](https://www.youm7.com/story/2024/12/28/خلى-بالك-الإزالة-عقوبة-الإعلانات-المخالفة-بالطرق-العامة/6825680); [Parlmany](https://www.parlmany.com/News/7/505903/)

### Inferences
- Regulatory shutdown risk (energy-driven lighting curfews) is real and has recent precedent. Business plans should model curfew hours.

At least two Cairo companies already market screens on vehicles and delivery bikes: Street Level and Routen Media. How they are licensed is not public.

### Cited Findings
- Street Level (Cairo) says it puts digital screens on trucks and delivery bikes, with a platform that lets advertisers choose areas and time slots and pay per play — [streetleveleg.com](https://streetleveleg.com/)
- Routen Media describes itself as a route-based digital out-of-home network of screens on vehicles across Cairo — [routenmedia.com](https://routenmedia.com/)
- Directories of Egyptian DOOH screen operators — [Entasher](https://entasher.com/eg/m/outdoor-screen-advertising); [TechBehemoths](https://techbehemoths.com/companies/outdoor-advertising/egypt)

### Inferences
- These players presumably either operate through an Interior-approved ad company (EGP 30m JSC) or operate in a grey zone. Asking them or their advertisers directly is the fastest way to learn the real licensing practice.
