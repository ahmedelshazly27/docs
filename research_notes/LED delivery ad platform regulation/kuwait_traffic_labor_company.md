## Q1. Traffic law: are illuminated or screen-equipped delivery boxes allowed, and do modifications need registration?

### Takeaway
No Kuwaiti rule found expressly permits or bans an illuminated, screen-equipped delivery box. The Traffic Department actually *requires* rear lighting and reflective strips on delivery boxes. However, the amended Traffic Law (Decree-Law 5/2025, in force 22 April 2025) penalises "illegal lights and other banned devices" and unapproved modifications. A bright, animated LED screen on a moving motorcycle is therefore a grey-to-high-risk area, and the Municipality's ad rules separately say delivery-vehicle ads must not protrude. Written pre-clearance from the MOI General Traffic Department is needed before deployment.

### Cited Findings
- Decree-Law No. 5/2025 amended Traffic Decree-Law No. 67/1976. It was issued in January 2025 and published in the Official Gazette, and it took effect after a three-month grace period, on **22 April 2025** — [Kuwait Times](https://kuwaittimes.com/article/26783/kuwait/kuwait-enforces-tougher-traffic-laws-tuesday); [STA Law Firm overview](https://www.stalawfirm.com/en/blogs/view/kuwaits-decree-law-no-5-of-2025); [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/world/gulf/kuwait/kuwait-enacts-tougher-traffic-laws-and-harsher-penalties-for-violations-1.500022722)
- Reporting on the 2025 law lists **illegal lights and other banned devices**, and changing a car's colour or replacing major parts, among prohibited acts. The specific fine for modifications could not be confirmed from snippets — [Arab Times, "Kuwait revamps traffic law"](https://www.arabtimesonline.com/news/kuwait-revamps-traffic-law-new-penalties-for-violations-and-changes-to-legal-procedures); [Lexis ME](https://www.lexis.ae/2025/03/20/kw-new-traffic-law-community-service-and-stricter-penalties/)
- Under the 2025 law, failing to use required lights carries a KD 45–75 fine. Phone use rose from KD 5 to **KD 75**, which shows how the law treats distraction. Outlets conflict on some figures, for example KD 150 versus KD 450 for reckless driving — [Arab Times, "triples fines"](https://www.arabtimesonline.com/news/kuwait-triples-fines-empowers-police-under-a-stricter-traffic-law); [Zawya](https://www.zawya.com/en/world/middle-east/kuwait-triples-fines-empowers-police-under-a-stricter-traffic-law-d8upg0q6)
- The 2025 law also introduces alternative penalties, such as community service, at judicial discretion — [Zawya](https://www.zawya.com/en/world/middle-east/kuwaits-new-traffic-law-introduces-community-service-as-alternative-penalty-gvo9zc86)
- **The General Traffic Department rule list for delivery riders (late 2026)** requires riders to keep right, not overtake between vehicles, and wear a helmet and a **company reflective vest**. Riders must use **rear lighting and reflective strips on the delivery box** and must not ride on sidewalks or pedestrian bridges. Violations can mean **impoundment of the motorcycle for up to 60 days**, plus administrative action against operating companies whose riders repeatedly offend — [Kuwait Times, "Traffic department lists rules for delivery motorcycle riders"](https://kuwaittimes.com/article/51768/kuwait/traffic-department-lists-rules-for-delivery-motorcycle-riders/); [Arab Times, sidewalks/60 days](https://www.arabtimesonline.com/news/kuwait-warns-delivery-riders-stay-off-sidewalks-or-lose-your-bike-for-60-days/)
- Riding on a sidewalk carries a KD 30 traffic settlement — [Arab Times](https://www.arabtimesonline.com/news/kuwait-warns-delivery-riders-stay-off-sidewalks-or-lose-your-bike-for-60-days/)
- An October 2026 enforcement campaign cited riders for **having no lights on their delivery boxes**, among other violations — [Kuwait Times, "Traffic campaign targets delivery motorcycle violations"](https://kuwaittimes.com/article/52132/kuwait/traffic-campaign-targets-delivery-motorcycle-violations/); [Pravasi News Daily, 22 Oct 2026](https://www.pravasinewsdaily.com/2026/10/22/kuwait-inspects-delivery-riders-for-traffic-violations/)
- At an October 2026 meeting, the MOI warned delivery companies that violations can lead to two-month impoundment of motorcycles or vehicles — [Times Kuwait](https://timeskuwait.com/kuwait-tightens-traffic-controls-on-delivery-drivers/)
- An older MOI instruction (c. 2023) told companies to have riders put a **reflective sticker on the motorcycle box** — [Zawya, "Ban on delivery bikes postponed till Nov 7"](https://www.zawya.com/en/legal/ban-on-delivery-bikes-in-kuwait-postponed-till-nov-7-d0ste675)
- **Municipality advertising rules (draft or updated in 2025):** "advertising medium" now covers fixed or mobile, traditional or digital means, *including vehicles and screens*. Ads on taxis and **delivery vehicles must be confined to the vehicle's sides without protrusion**. In October 2025 the revised draft was reportedly still going to the Municipal Council for final approval, so whether it has been adopted is unconfirmed — [Arab Times, "New Advertising Rules Introduced in Kuwait"](https://www.arabtimesonline.com/news/new-advertising-rules-introduced-in-kuwait-fines-fees-and-standards-updated/); [Times Kuwait, "Municipality updates outdoor advertisement regulations"](https://timeskuwait.com/municipality-updates-outdoor-advertisement-regulations/); [Times Kuwait, street advertising/road safety](https://timeskuwait.com/kuwait-tightens-street-advertising-rules-to-boost-road-safety-and-urban-aesthetics/)

### Inferences
- Box lighting is mandatory, so lit boxes are not banned in themselves. The legal risk sits in two places. The first is *what kind* of light: flashing or coloured light, or video content, could be treated as an "illegal light/banned device" or a distraction under the 2025 law. The second is the Municipality ad licence, since the expanded definition explicitly covers vehicles and screens.
- A box-mounted screen is likely to need **two separate approvals**: MOI Traffic, for the vehicle and equipment, and a **Kuwait Municipality advertising licence**, with per-m² fees.
- Enforcement focuses on the *company*, through impoundment and administrative action against operators. A 3PL fleet owner will therefore be very sensitive to anything on its bikes that could trigger a stop.

### Gaps
- The exact article of Decree-Law 5/2025, as amended, on vehicle modifications, extra lights and display devices, and its fine, was not confirmed.
- No rule was found on delivery box **dimensions** in Kuwait.
- I could not determine whether modifications must be registered or inspected at the Technical Inspection Division of the GTD for motorcycles.
- No MOI statement was found on video screens on vehicles, such as the bans on moving digital ads on vehicles that exist in some other GCC states. Verify directly with the GTD.

## Q2. Delivery-sector regulation: delivery company licensing, bike rules, uniforms/branding, highways, 3PL structure

### Takeaway
Kuwait regulates delivery through MOI ministerial resolutions. The base is **Ministerial Resolution 271/2020**, later amended. In 2026 came **Ministerial Decision 109/2026**, in force from 1 Sept 2026, which a court partly annulled at Article 15. These cap vehicles per licence, limit motorbikes, ban bikes from ring roads and expressways, and require company-sponsored riders, company stickers and uniform vests. MOCI separately regulates delivery *platforms* through Ministerial Decision 10/2026 under the new Digital Commerce Decree-Law 10/2026. Bikes and riders sit with licensed delivery companies, typically 3PL fleets or restaurants, not with the platforms or the riders themselves.

### Cited Findings
- MR 271/2020 as amended: motorbikes are **not allowed on ring roads and expressways**. A company may add motorbikes only after owning a minimum number of cars. One report gives this as a minimum of 5 cars, then up to 30 motorcycles, and earlier a cap of 15 — [Arab Times, "New Regulations for Delivery Vehicles"](https://www.arabtimesonline.com/news/new-regulations-for-delivery-vehicles-issued-by-kuwait-ministry/); [Zawya](https://zawya.com/en/world/middle-east/kuwaits-ministry-issues-new-regulations-for-delivery-vehicles-w3qpoyef); [Times Kuwait, 15 bikes per food outlet](https://timeskuwait.com/news/ministry-limits-home-delivery-bikes-to-15-per-food-outlet)
- The per-licence vehicle cap was raised from **45 to 90** (Times Kuwait). Sources conflict on the prior cap, 45 versus 30, and on the issuing minister, and the year is unclear — [Times Kuwait, limit raised to 90](https://timeskuwait.com/kuwaits-delivery-sector-thrives-with-vehicle-limit-raised-to-90/); [Arab Times](https://arabtimesonline.com/news/new-regulations-for-delivery-vehicles-issued-by-kuwait-ministry)
- The MOI at one point **suspended new vehicle licences for delivery companies**, allowing only replacement of out-of-service vehicles. The current status is unclear — [Indians in Kuwait](https://www.indiansinkuwait.com/news/Strict-restrictions-for-delivery-companies-to-add-new-vehicles/); [Kuwait Times, "New rules for delivery companies; permits for new firms suspended"](https://kuwaittimes.com/new-rules-for-delivery-companies-permits-for-new-firms-suspended/)
- Delivery vehicles carry **yellow plates**, and riders must wear a company-specific vest whose specifications the Traffic Department's Inspection Division sets — [Court Cast](https://courtscast.com/kuwait-revamps-delivery-vehicle-regulations-what-you-need-to-know/); [Arab Times](https://www.arabtimesonline.com/news/new-regulations-for-delivery-vehicles-issued-by-kuwait-ministry/)
- From **1 Oct 2022**, the MOI Traffic Department required delivery companies to meet four conditions. Drivers need health certificates. **Vehicles must carry company stickers.** **Drivers must hold residency under (be sponsored by) the employer company.** Drivers of cars and motorcycles must wear a uniform. Violators risk losing their licence — [Al-Rai, "«المرور»: 4 اشتراطات لشركات توصيل الطلبات"](https://www.alraimedia.com/article/1604983/محليات/أخبار-محلية/المرور-4-اشتراطات-لشركات-توصيل-الطلبات)
- **Ministerial Decision 109/2026** governs delivery and took effect on 1 Sept 2026. A court annulled its **Article 15**, the penalties article, which raised fears of weak enforcement — [Kuwait Times, "Court strikes down delivery penalties"](https://kuwaittimes.com/article/50539/kuwait/other-news/court-strikes-down-delivery-penalties-raising-fears-of-weak-enforcement/). Details of the decision's other articles were not retrieved.
- **Summer midday ban:** delivery motorbikes are banned from roads from 11:00 to 16:00 daily, **1 June–31 Aug 2026**, in line with the outdoor-work heat ban — [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/world/gulf/kuwait/kuwait-bans-delivery-bikes-during-peak-summer-heat-hours-1.500559094); [Khaleej Times](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/world/gulf/midday-ban-delivery-bikes-june-1-kuwait); [Times Kuwait](https://timeskuwait.com/delivery-motorbikes-ordered-off-the-roads-from-1100-am-to-400-pm/)
- **MOCI Ministerial Decision No. 10 of 2026** sets binding rules for food delivery platforms. It bans forced exclusivity and discriminatory algorithms, and penalties run from warnings to closure and licence revocation. The total a platform may charge a restaurant, **including advertising fees, featured placement and ranking**, is reportedly capped at **17% of order value**. It draws on **Decree-Law No. 10/2026 Regulating Work in the Digital Commerce Sector** — [Madhyamam](https://madhyamamonline.com/middle-east/kuwait/kuwait-issues-binding-rules-for-restaurant-food-delivery-platforms-1491805); [GLA & Company](https://www.glaco.com/blog/kuwaits-new-rules-for-delivery-platforms-what-restaurants-delivery-companies-and-consumers-need-to-know/)
- There are concerns about domestic workers (Article 20 visas) entering delivery work — [Arab Times](https://www.arabtimesonline.com/news/need-to-stop-domestic-workers-entering-delivery-service-sector)

### Inferences
- Kuwaiti delivery runs on a fleet-licence model: licensed delivery companies own yellow-plated vehicles and sponsor riders. The 2022 company-residency requirement means platforms such as Talabat, Keeta and Jahez depend on these fleet companies or on restaurants' own fleets. **The counterparty for box-space deals should be the fleet licence holder**, which owns the bikes and boxes and bears the MOI liability.
- Platform branding on boxes and vests is a commercial contract matter between the platform and the fleet. MOI only requires a *company* sticker and vest. Platform-fleet contracts may restrict third-party ads on boxes, so check them.
- The 17% cap under MD 10/2026 covers fees platforms charge restaurants. It does not appear to regulate third-party out-of-home (OOH) ads, but it signals active MOCI scrutiny of delivery-ad economics.

### Gaps
- The full text of MD 109/2026, the MOI delivery decision, was not retrieved. Its rules on boxes, branding or ads are unknown.
- The text and rider provisions of Decree-Law 10/2026 (digital commerce) were not retrieved.
- No source confirmed the current status of the permit suspension for new delivery firms.

## Q3. Labor & residency: can a third party pay riders for carrying ads? How to structure legally?

### Takeaway
Paying expatriate riders directly is high-risk. **Article 19 of Decree-Law 114/2024** (Foreigners' Residence) bars foreign workers from working for anyone other than their sponsor, and breach is a deportation ground even with a valid residence. The only lawful worker-level route is a **PAM part-time permit**, capped at 4 hours a day, with the sponsor's NOC, applied for by the second employer. Structure the deal **B2B with the fleet/3PL company**, as a lease of box space or advertising-media rights. The fleet can then pass incentives to riders through its own payroll.

### Cited Findings
- **Decree-Law 114/2024** on Foreigners' Residence was issued in late 2024 and replaced the 1959 law. The Library of Congress summarises it — [LoC Global Legal Monitor](https://www.loc.gov/item/global-legal-monitor/2024-12-17/kuwait-new-law-adopted-regulating-foreigners-residency/); [ARALF](https://www.arazzaqlaw.com/new-foreign-residency-law-issued-under-amiri-decree-no-114-2024/); [Arab Times, "takes effect Sunday"](https://www.arabtimesonline.com/news/kuwaits-new-expats-residence-law-takes-effect-sunday)
- **Article 19** says foreign workers must not work for any employer other than their designated sponsor, and employers must not withhold wages — [Arab Times, "Expats face hefty fines"](https://www.arabtimesonline.com/news/expats-face-hefty-fines-under-kuwaits-new-residency-law); [EGIC analysis](https://egic.info/analyse/presenting-the-amiri-decree-114-2024-on-immigration-in-kuwait/)
- Working for another party without permission, in violation of Art. 19, is listed among grounds for **deportation despite a valid residence permit** — [Arab Times, "Valid residency permit won't prevent deportation in these 3 cases"](https://www.arabtimesonline.com/news/valid-residency-permit-wont-prevent-deportation-of-expats-in-these-3-cases)
- **Article 18** targets **residency trading**: recruiting or renewing residence for money or benefit. It carries 3–5 years' imprisonment and/or a KD 5,000–10,000 fine, and legal entities face fines and possible licence loss. One Arab Times piece states "up to 3 years", so the sources conflict — [ARALF](https://www.arazzaqlaw.com/new-foreign-residency-law-issued-under-amiri-decree-no-114-2024/); [Haqq.ai](https://www.haqq.ai/blog/kuwait-recruiting-workers-without-need-crime); [Times Kuwait](https://timeskuwait.com/kuwaits-new-residency-law-ensures-justice-for-workers-and-imprisonment-for-residency-traders/)
- One law blog notes a separate offence under the 2024 law of letting workers "work for others without a licence", which can stack with Labour Law penalties. Article 138 of the Labour Law applies "without prejudice to any harsher penalty" — [Haqq.ai](https://www.haqq.ai/blog/kuwait-recruiting-workers-without-need-crime)
- Art. 18 private-sector workers and Art. 20 drivers who leave their sponsor face restrictions, including a reported 2-year lock on returning for a different employer — [EGIC](https://egic.info/analyse/presenting-the-amiri-decree-114-2024-on-immigration-in-kuwait/)
- **Part-time work permit:** a ministerial decision effective **January 2024** lets private-sector expats work for a second employer for up to **4 hours a day**. This requires a PAM part-time permit and **the primary employer's approval (NOC)**. It does not apply to contracting-sector workers. The *second employer* applies through PAM's Ashal platform. Fees are KD 5 for 1 month, KD 10 for 3, KD 20 for 6 and KD 30 for 12 — [Fragomen](https://www.fragomen.com/insights/kuwait-launch-of-part-time-work-permits.html); [Lexis ME](https://www.lexismiddleeast.com/news/2024-02-05_43/en); [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/world/gulf/kuwait/kuwait-approves-part-time-work-all-you-need-to-know-1.1703846396025); [Arab Times how-to](https://www.arabtimesonline.com/news/how-to-take-up-a-part-time-job-in-kuwait-everything-you-need-to-know/)
- From 2022, MOI conditions require delivery drivers to be on their employer company's residency — [Al-Rai](https://www.alraimedia.com/article/1604983/محليات/أخبار-محلية/المرور-4-اشتراطات-لشركات-توصيل-الطلبات)

### Inferences
- Paying a rider directly for "carrying an ad" during their sponsored delivery shift is very likely to be treated as work for a non-sponsor (Art. 19). Exposure falls on the rider (deportation) and potentially on the payer, for employing a worker not sponsored by it. The part-time permit model fits badly: the ad is carried *during* the main job, the permit requires the payer to be a licensed employer, and every rider would need a sponsor NOC.
- The cleanest structure is a **B2B media-space lease or licence agreement with the fleet/3PL licence holder**. The ad company pays the fleet a fee per box per month, or a revenue share. The fleet owns or holds the box-mounted asset or the right to mount it, keeps MOI compliance, and decides whether to pay riders bonuses through its own payroll. The contract should set out who holds the Municipality ad licence, the MOI/GTD approvals, insurance, damage, and the right to remove screens on authority instruction.
- Self-employed and freelance routes for expats on Art. 18 visas were not found to exist for this purpose (see Gaps).

### Gaps
- The exact fine under the 2024 Residence Law for a worker or third party breaching Art. 19 was not retrieved from the gazette text.
- I found no 2025–2026 update to the part-time permit regime, and nothing on whether it covers delivery-company workers.
- No Kuwaiti expat freelance or self-employment permit was found that would let an Art. 18 worker earn side income. Treat this as unconfirmed rather than proven absent.
- The text of MD 109/2026 may contain sponsorship-specific rules for riders. Not retrieved.

## Q4. Paying shop owners to host storefront screens

### Takeaway
Payment to a shop owner is a private commercial arrangement, a lease or licence of window or facade space, with no VAT in Kuwait. The screen itself, however, almost certainly needs a **Kuwait Municipality advertising licence**. Electronic screens carry per-m² annual fees, and unlicensed commercial ads face large fines. Facade ads are tied to the shop's commercial licence validity, and the 2025 municipal enforcement campaign specifically targets **screen displays**.

### Cited Findings
- The Kuwait Government Online service "Issuing Licenses for Advertisements on Store Facades and Light Boxes" is a Kuwait Municipality service. Fees are fixed per m² per side, and the financial guarantee equals half the ad fees — [e.gov.kw](https://e.gov.kw/sites/kgoenglish/Pages/Services/KM/IssuingLicensesForAdvertisementsOnStoreFacadesAndLightBoxes.aspx)
- Under the updated rules, facade ads share the validity period of the shop's commercial licence, and lightbox ads are formally regulated — [Times Kuwait](https://timeskuwait.com/municipality-updates-outdoor-advertisement-regulations/)
- Electronic screens are reported at **KD 75/m² per side per year minimum**, while another report says **KD 100/m²**. Commercial advertising companies reportedly pay an annual KD 100 fee — [Arab Times](https://www.arabtimesonline.com/news/new-advertising-rules-introduced-in-kuwait-fines-fees-and-standards-updated/); [Times Kuwait](https://timeskuwait.com/municipality-updates-outdoor-advertisement-regulations/)
- Penalties of **KD 3,000–5,000** are reported for displaying commercial ads without a licence, with KD 100–500 for lesser violations. Kuwait Times notes fines of up to KD 5,000 — [Arab Times](https://www.arabtimesonline.com/news/new-advertising-rules-introduced-in-kuwait-fines-fees-and-standards-updated/); [Kuwait Times, inspection campaign](https://kuwaittimes.com/article/32900/kuwait/other-news/municipality-launches-inspection-campaign-on-shop-ad-licenses/)
- In September 2025, Municipality inspections checked ad permits, **particularly for rooftop and screen displays**, and shops have been penalised for ad violations — [Arab Times, "Nine shops penalized"](https://www.arabtimesonline.com/news/nine-shops-penalized-for-advertising-violations-in-the-latest-inspections/); [Times Kuwait, licensing controls](https://timeskuwait.com/kuwait-tightens-controls-on-commercial-licensing/)
- Advertising content is also subject to MOCI rules on misleading ads and to permit requirements — [Yousef Law Firm (YLF)](https://ylf.com.kw/blogs/%D8%AA%D9%86%D8%B8%D9%8A%D9%85-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A5%D8%B9%D9%84%D8%A7%D9%86%D8%A7%D8%AA-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AA%D8%AC%D8%A7%D8%B1%D9%8A%D8%A9-%D9%81%D9%8A-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%83%D9%88%D9%8A%D8%AA-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AA%D8%B1%D8%A7%D8%AE%D9%8A%D8%B5-%D9%88%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A5%D8%B9%D9%84%D8%A7%D9%86-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%85%D8%B6%D9%84%D9%84-%D9%88%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%85%D8%B3%D8%A4%D9%88%D9%84%D9%8A%D8%A9-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%82%D8%A7%D9%86%D9%88%D9%86%D9%8A%D8%A9?lang=en)
- Kuwait has no VAT. A 2026 tracker says no VAT law was in force as of 31 July 2026 — [TaxinME](https://www.taxinme.com/articles/kuwait-vat-status-2026.php)

### Inferences
- A screen *inside* the window that faces the street, showing third-party ads, will likely be treated as a commercial advertising medium needing a licence, because the 2025 definition covers digital and screen media. Budget for per-m² fees and a guarantee deposit. The draft rules suggest the licensee may be the shop, as facade-licence holder, or the ad company.
- The payment to the shop owner is ordinary taxable business income only if the shop is a foreign-owned corporate body, given Kuwait's limited tax base. There is no withholding or VAT on the payment itself, though the 5% retention rule on payments to contractors may apply in some contexts. The retention rule is unverified here.

### Gaps
- I could not confirm whether the revised municipal ad regulation was finally adopted by the Municipal Council, or the definitive fee schedule.
- It is unclear whether in-window screens, as opposed to external facade signage, need a licence. Not confirmed.

## Q5. Company setup: activity licence, foreign ownership, corporate taxes, DMTT, VAT

### Takeaway
A foreign founder can own up to 49% of a Kuwaiti WLL by default, or up to **100% with a KDIPA licence** under Law 116/2013 and its executive regulations. Advertising-specific KDIPA eligibility was not confirmed. Foreign corporate shareholders pay **15% corporate income tax** on their share of Kuwait-source profits. Kuwaiti/GCC-owned entities pay no CIT but may face **Zakat at 1%** and, if listed, **NLST at 2.5%**. The **15% DMTT** (Decree-Law 157/2024, from 1 Jan 2025) applies only to MNE groups with revenue of at least €750m. **No VAT** in 2026.

### Cited Findings
- A non-Kuwaiti's shareholding in a WLL should be at most 49%, except with KDIPA approval — [PwC Doing Business in Kuwait 2025](https://www.pwc.com/m1/en/tax/documents/doing-business-guides/dbiku.pdf)
- KDIPA can permit up to **100% foreign ownership** of approved investment entities, under the Direct Investment Promotion Law. Most sources cite **Law 116/2013**, while one cites "502/2014", which is likely the executive regulations — [KDIPA](https://kdipa.gov.kw/); [KDIPA "Invest in Kuwait" brochure, Nov 2025](https://kdipa.gov.kw/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/Invest-in-Kuwait-Brochure-November-2025-1.pdf); [Wefaq Law](https://www.wefaqlaw.com/en/blog/investing-in-kuwait-through-kdipa-answers-to-the-questions-foreign-investors-ask-most); [GLA & Co](https://www.glaco.com/blog/understanding-the-kdipa-license-in-kuwait-essential-considerations-for-foreign-investors/)
- Proposed amendments to the KDIPA law would allow investment entities **without a Kuwaiti partner or local agent**. This is reported as a *proposal*, not current law — [Arab Times, "KDIPA and Kuwait's new investment proposition"](https://www.arabtimesonline.com/news/kdipa-and-kuwaits-new-investment-proposition/)
- **15% CIT** applies to foreign corporate bodies on Kuwait-source profits. Companies wholly owned by Kuwaiti or GCC nationals pay no CIT (PwC, per secondary source) — [Commenda](https://www.commenda.io/kuwait/corporate-tax-rates); [Tax Efficiency Lab](https://taxefficiencylab.com/kuwait-tax-guide/)
- **DMTT:** Decree-Law 157/2024 imposes a 15% top-up on MNE groups with consolidated revenue of at least €750m, for periods from **1 Jan 2025**. In-scope entities transition out of CIT, Zakat and NLST — [KPMG Kuwait](https://kpmg.com/kw/en/insights/2025/01/domestic-minimum-top-up-tax.html); [KPMG Kuwait Tax Alert, April 2026](https://assets.kpmg.com/content/dam/kpmgsites/kw/pdf/insights/2026/kuwait-tax-alert-april-2026.pdf.coredownload.inline.pdf); [Al Tamimi, "Kuwait 2026"](https://www.tamimi.com/our-knowledge/publications/through-the-looking-glass/kuwait-2026-the-strategic-shifts-investors-need-to-watch-now/); [Boursa/Zawya](https://www.zawya.com/en/press-release/events-and-conferences/boursa-kuwait-concludes-awareness-session-on-ifrs-developments-and-the-new-dmtt-law-for-multinational-enterprises-mwrv5nnq)
- The KPMG April 2026 alert says NLST (Law 19/2000) is expected to remain in force with certain paragraph exclusions. The legacy regime it lists is CIT (Decree 3/1955), Zakat (Law 46/2006) and the Neutral Zone Tax (Law 23/1961) — [KPMG Kuwait Tax Alert, April 2026](https://assets.kpmg.com/content/dam/kpmgsites/kw/pdf/insights/2026/kuwait-tax-alert-april-2026.pdf.coredownload.inline.pdf)
- Al Tamimi (June 2026) references **1% Zakat** on GCC-owned companies and **2.5% NLST** on listed entities, and says Kuwait is moving toward a fuller corporate tax system — [Al Tamimi](https://www.tamimi.com/our-knowledge/publications/through-the-looking-glass/kuwait-2026-the-strategic-shifts-investors-need-to-watch-now/)
- **VAT:** none in force as of 31 July 2026. One secondary source says the government's plan rules out VAT before 2028, which is a single-source claim — [TaxinME VAT tracker](https://www.taxinme.com/articles/kuwait-vat-status-2026.php); [TaxinME guide](https://www.taxinme.com/guides/kuwait.php)
- **Business Profits Tax:** a broader business profits tax on Kuwaiti companies has been discussed, but no source confirmed it enacted as of late 2026 — [TaxinME guide](https://www.taxinme.com/guides/kuwait.php); [Al Tamimi](https://www.tamimi.com/our-knowledge/publications/through-the-looking-glass/kuwait-2026-the-strategic-shifts-investors-need-to-watch-now/)

### Inferences
- A startup ad agency is far below the €750m DMTT threshold. The relevant taxes are the 15% CIT on foreign shareholders' profit share and possibly Zakat, plus NLST if listed (unlikely).
- The practical routes are a WLL with a Kuwaiti majority partner (≥51%), or a KDIPA application for 100% foreign ownership. KDIPA favours tech, knowledge-transfer and job-creating projects, so an "ad-tech platform" pitch may fit better than a "publicity agency" pitch. This is an inference; it was not checked against KDIPA's negative list.

### Gaps
- MOCI ISIC activity codes for "advertising agency / publicity" and any minimum capital were not retrieved.
- I could not confirm whether advertising is on the **KDIPA negative list**, the activities excluded from 100% ownership.
- The status of the Business Profits Tax law (draft or enacted) in late 2026 is unconfirmed.

## Q6. Data protection: GPS tracking, cameras, audience measurement

### Takeaway
Kuwait has **no general data-protection statute**. CITRA's **Data Privacy Protection Regulation No. 26/2024**, which replaced 42/2021, is reported by Chambers 2026 as narrowed to CITRA licensees (telecom/ICT providers), so an ad company may fall outside it. However, the scope reading conflicts between sources. The prudent approach is to design to its standards: explicit consent, deletion after purpose, 24-hour breach notification, and cross-border adequacy.

### Cited Findings
- CITRA Decision No. 26/2024 replaced the 2021 regulation (No. 42/2021). Its effective date is reported as 19 Feb 2024, with one source giving 26 March 2024 — [Al Tamimi, Feb 2024](https://www.tamimi.com/news/communications-and-information-technology-regulatory-authority-has-issued-new-data-privacy-regulations); [dig.watch](https://dig.watch/?p=205967); [Clym](https://clym.io/regulations/kuwait-data-privacy-protection-regulation-26-2024)
- **Scope conflict:** early summaries say the regulation applies to all entities processing personal data in Kuwait. Chambers 2026 and others say the 2024 version applies mainly to CITRA-licensed telecom and internet providers — [dig.watch](https://dig.watch/?p=205967); [Chambers Data Protection 2026, Kuwait](https://practiceguides.chambers.com/practice-guides/data-protection-privacy-2026/kuwait); [ConsentStack](https://www.consentstack.io/regulations/kw-dppr)
- Reported obligations are explicit consent (guardian consent under 18), the right to withdraw, deletion when the purpose is fulfilled, **breach notification to CITRA within 24 hours**, and cross-border transfers only to adequate jurisdictions — [ConsentStack](https://www.consentstack.io/regulations/kw-dppr); [Michalsons](https://www.michalsons.com/blog/kuwait-data-protection-regulation-2024-update/72816)
- Privacy is otherwise governed by a patchwork of e-transactions law, telecom rules, cloud guidance and cybercrime law — [Chambers 2026](https://practiceguides.chambers.com/practice-guides/data-protection-privacy-2026/kuwait)

### Inferences
- GPS on the boxes tracks the *rider's* location, which is personal data of a worker who is not the ad company's employee. Get the fleet's and riders' written consent through the B2B contract.
- Cameras or face-based audience measurement in public streets are higher risk, given the cybercrime and privacy patchwork. Anonymous, on-device counting with no images stored is safer.
- If the platform uses SIM connectivity, the telco (a CITRA licensee) is in scope. Expect contractual flow-down of obligations.

### Gaps
- I could not resolve the official scope of DPPR 26/2024 from primary text.
- No specific Kuwaiti rule was found on CCTV or cameras on vehicles or in public-facing commercial displays.

## Q7. Electrical/product conformity for LED film displays

### Takeaway
LED displays imported to Kuwait likely fall under PAI's **KUCAS** scheme for regulated electrical and electronic products. Shipments need a Technical Evaluation Report (TER) and a Technical Inspection Report (TIR) from a PAI-approved certification body before customs clearance. The GCC G-Mark applies to listed low-voltage products. I could not confirm whether LED display panels specifically are on the regulated list.

### Cited Findings
- PAI has run KUCAS since June 2006. It verifies conformity of "Regulated Products", whether domestic or imported — [TÜV SÜD Kuwait](https://www.tuvsud.com/en/services/product-certification/global-market-access/kuwait); [TÜV Rheinland KUCAS](https://www.tuv.com/market-access-services/pt/certification-filter/kucas-certification/index.html)
- Exports of regulated products require a **TER** (technical standards conformity) and a **TIR** (import rules) for customs clearance, and customs may take random samples — [Intertek KUCAS](https://www.intertek.com.hk/government/coc-kuwait)
- Conformity procedures must be completed before shipment through PAI-approved Certification and Inspection Bodies (CIBs) — [TÜV SÜD](https://www.tuvsud.com/en/services/product-certification/global-market-access/kuwait)
- A Certificate of Conformity is primarily required for electrical and electronic goods and building materials — [JAICC](https://www.jaicc.ie/certificate_of_conformity)
- An older source mentions 230V/50Hz, a UK-type plug, and RoHS reports for battery products. It is possibly outdated — [Anbotek](https://www.anbotek.com/kuwaiti-kucas-cert/)

### Inferences
- Box-mounted LED films will likely be powered from 12V/48V lithium packs or the bike, while storefront units will be mains-powered. Both may trigger regulated-product rules, for electronics, lithium batteries and power supplies. Ask a CIB such as Intertek, TÜV or BV for a product classification before the first import.
- A wireless (4G/Wi-Fi) module would also need **CITRA type approval**. I did not verify this here; it is standard practice for radio equipment.

### Gaps
- No confirmation that LED display panels are on PAI's current regulated-products list.
- No details found on whether the GCC G-Mark (GSO low-voltage technical regulation) covers LED displays in Kuwait.
- CITRA type-approval requirements for SIM or Wi-Fi modules were not researched.
