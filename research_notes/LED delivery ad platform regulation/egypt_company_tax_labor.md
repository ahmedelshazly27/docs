# Egypt: Company Setup, Tax, Labor, Payments, Data and Device Rules for an LED-Screen Rider/Shop Ad Agency (as of Nov 2026)

Method note: research ran 2026-11-16 through web search, with about 16 tool calls. Direct page fetches were blocked in this session for taxsummaries.pwc.com, enterpriseam.com and masaar.net. Claims sourced to those sites come from search-engine extracts of the pages and were not read in full. Most findings come from secondary sources (law firms, Big 4, press). None were checked against the Official Gazette. Treat all figures as "verify with counsel/ETA before relying".

## 1. Company setup: GAFI incorporation, advertising activity, licences, foreign ownership

### Takeaway
A normal LLC set up through GAFI is the usual vehicle. The main sector-specific problems are these:
- Under Decree 962/2012, advertising and marketing agencies apparently must register in the Commercial Agents' Registry.
- Commercial agency activity is reserved for Egyptian-owned companies, which may limit 100% foreign ownership of an ad agency.
- Ads on public roads need a licence under Law 208/2020. Whether that law covers vehicle-mounted or shopfront screens is unclear.

### Cited Findings
- GAFI centralises company registration. The LLC is the most common vehicle for foreign investors. — [Legal 500 guide](https://www.legal500.com/guides/?p=137014); [Andersen Egypt – register a company](https://eg.andersen.com/register-a-company-in-egypt/)
- Foreigners can fully own certain company types, such as LLCs and JSCs, but industry-specific restrictions may apply. — [Andersen Egypt](https://eg.andersen.com/register-a-company-in-egypt/)
- Foreign ownership is generally unrestricted, apart from activities reserved for Egyptians: commercial agency, importation for trading purposes, and acquiring land. Commercial agency is limited to companies wholly owned by Egyptians, or by persons who have held Egyptian nationality for at least 10 years. — [DLA Piper Corporate Law in Egypt](https://intelligence.dlapiper.com/global-expansion-corporate/?c=EG)
- Importation for resale was reportedly eased by Law 173/2023. — [HAQQ blog (secondary)](https://www.haqq.ai/blog/tasees-sharika-misr)
- "All agencies and natural persons working in the field of marketing and advertising are required to register their company in the Commercial Agents' Registry, even if it is not the main activity." This cites Ministerial Decree No. 962 of 2012 on registering advertising and media-marketing agencies in the Register of Commercial Agents and Brokers. — [Mondaq: Advertising agencies in Egypt – governing laws and restrictions](https://webiis08.mondaq.com/advertising-marketing-branding/1426214/advertising-agencies-in-egypt-governing-laws-and-restrictions)
- Outdoor advertising Law 208/2020 governs advertisements on public roads. It applies to all advertisers (individuals, companies and agencies) except state-owned entities. It requires a licence before any sign or billboard is erected, with a three-year renewable term. The regulator is the National Authority for Public Roads Advertisements. The law was issued 30 Nov 2020 and took effect 2 Dec 2020. — [Mondaq (same article)](https://webiis08.mondaq.com/advertising-marketing-branding/1426214/advertising-agencies-in-egypt-governing-laws-and-restrictions); [manshurat.org record](https://manshurat.org/node/68932); [CEFAN text page](https://axl.cefan.ulaval.ca/afrique/egypte-loi-208-2020.htm)
- A Masrawy headline from Dec 2020 mentions EGP 10,000 fees under the road-advertising law. The body was not retrieved. — [Masrawy](https://www.masrawy.com/news/news_egypt/details/2020/12/10/1928342/رسوم-10-آلاف-جنيه-10-معلومات-عن-قانون-تنظيم-الإعلانات-على-الطرق-العامة-ضوابط-وعقوبات-)
- Egypt launched a GIS database and a digital platform for roadside advertising. In 2026 the press reported the billboard regulator tightening renewal rules. — [Amwal Al Ghad – GIS database](https://en.amwalalghad.com/?p=229141); [Amwal Al Ghad – digital platform](https://en.amwalalghad.com/?p=229365); [Enterprise, 16 Sep 2026](https://enterpriseam.com/egypt/2026/09/16/billboard-regulator-tightens-renewal-rules-as-market-nears-saturation/)
- In 2026, Parlmany (Parliament news) covered fines for placing advertisements on public roads without a licence. — [Parlmany, Jul 2026](https://www.parlmany.com/News/7/505903/%D9%85%D8%A7-%D9%87%D9%89-%D8%B9%D9%82%D9%88%D8%A8%D8%A9-%D9%88%D8%B6%D8%B9-%D8%A5%D8%B9%D9%84%D8%A7%D9%86-%D8%B9%D9%84%D9%89-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B7%D8%B1%D9%82-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B9%D8%A7%D9%85%D8%A9-%D8%A8%D8%AF%D9%88%D9%86-%D8%AA%D8%B1%D8%AE%D9%8A%D8%B5); [Parlmany, Aug 2026](https://www.parlmany.com/News/7/510995/الغرامة-عقوبة-وضع-إعلانات-دون-ترخيص-ومحددات-واضحة-فى-إزالتها)

### Inferences
- If Decree 962/2012 still applies and the advertising activity counts as "commercial agency", a foreign-owned startup may need Egyptian majority or full ownership, or a structure that keeps the advertising activity in an Egyptian-owned entity. This is the single biggest setup question for counsel.
- Screens on delivery boxes and shop windows facing the street could plausibly fall under Law 208/2020 ("advertisements on public roads"). Mobile vehicle ads are not clearly addressed. This is a material licensing risk.

### Gaps
- No source confirmed the exact GAFI/commercial-registry activity code for "advertising agency / digital out-of-home".
- I found no source on any separate SCMR or Chamber of Commerce "advertising division" licence. The Commercial Agents' Registry (Decree 962/2012) is the closest match found.
- The Law 208/2020 fee schedule, whether vehicle-mounted or in-shop screens are in scope, and the executive regulations were not retrieved.
- No source covered minimum capital or GAFI timelines for 2026.

## 2. Tax: CIT, VAT on advertising, stamp tax, WHT, simplified regime, e-invoicing/e-receipts

### Takeaway
- Standard CIT is 22.5%.
- Advertising moved from 20% stamp tax to 14% VAT under Law 3/2022.
- Domestic WHT under Art. 59 of Law 91/2005 applies to payments above EGP 300: 1% for supply/contracting, 3% for services, 5% for commissions.
- The small-business simplified regime is Law 6/2025, not "6/2023". It taxes turnover up to EGP 20m at 0.4% to 1.5%.
- E-invoicing (B2B) and e-receipts (B2C) are mandatory through ETA systems.

### Cited Findings
- **CIT 22.5%:** standard rate. Sources note it should be verified against the current PwC page. — [Ecovis tax guide](https://global.ecovis.com/?p=10635); [KPMG Egypt business taxation](https://kpmg.com/eg/en/services/tax/investment-in-egypt/business-taxation.html)
- **VAT:**
  - Standard rate is 14%. Mandatory registration threshold is EGP 500,000 annual taxable turnover, per the PwC "other taxes" page (search extract; page last reviewed 17 Aug 2026). — [PwC Tax Summaries – Egypt other taxes](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/egypt/corporate/other-taxes)
  - Conflict: one vendor source says the threshold was cut to EGP 250,000 from 1 Jan 2026. This is unverified, and PwC (Aug 2026) still shows EGP 500,000. — [vendor sources via search: lookuptax](https://lookuptax.com/docs/country/egypt-vat-guidelines-indirect-tax); [PwC](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/egypt/corporate/other-taxes)
- **Advertising stamp tax abolished in favour of VAT:**
  - Advertising services are no longer subject to the 20% stamp tax after Law No. 3 of 2022. They are now subject to 14% VAT.
  - Exempt categories include advertisements of donations for medical treatment at hospitals and government institutes, and mandatory sale, election and job-seeker notices.
  - Sources: [PwC Tax Summaries – other taxes](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/egypt/corporate/other-taxes); [PwC alert on Law 3/2022](https://pwc.com/m1/en/tax/documents/2022/egypt-issuance-of-law-number-three-to-update-vat-and-stamp-tax-laws.pdf)
- **Second Tax Facilitation Package (2026):** enacted around late July / early August 2026. It amends stamp duty on EGX-listed securities, which is not relevant to advertising. — [PwC alert 2026](https://www.pwc.com/m1/en/tax/documents/2026/egypt-second-tax-facilitation-package.pdf)
- **Domestic WHT:**
  - Rates on local payments above EGP 300: 1% for contracting and supplying, 3% for services, 5% for commissions. The withholding is an advance payment of CIT, not an additional cost.
  - Legal basis is Art. 59 of Income Tax Law 91/2005. Scope is set by Ministerial Decree 537/2005, amended by Decree 380/2018 (effective 15 Aug 2018).
  - Sources: [Andersen – Withholding taxes in Egypt](https://eg.andersen.com/withholding-taxes-in-egypt); [Andersen Tax Alert 105](https://eg.andersen.com/tax-alert-105/); [PwC – withholding taxes](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/egypt/corporate/withholding-taxes)
  - In Oct 2023 the ETA urged taxpayers to apply withholding rates on purchases. — [Diacron Group](https://www.diacrongroup.com/en/tax-news/egyptian-tax-authority-urges-taxpayers-to-apply-withholding-rates-on-purchases/)
- **Non-resident WHT:** 20% on royalties and interest paid abroad (treaty relief possible). — [PwC – withholding taxes](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/egypt/corporate/withholding-taxes); [KPMG](https://kpmg.com/eg/en/services/tax/investment-in-egypt/business-taxation.html)
- **Simplified regime is Law No. 6 of 2025** (effective 1 March 2025), not 6/2023:
  - Eligibility: businesses with annual turnover up to EGP 20m.
  - Turnover tax rates (Andersen translation of the official text):
    - 0.4% below EGP 500k
    - 0.5% for EGP 500k to under 2m
    - 0.75% for EGP 2m to under 3m
    - 1% for EGP 3m to under 10m
    - 1.5% for EGP 10m to 20m
  - Other benefits: CIT/VAT inspections deferred for 5 years, and quarterly VAT filing. Businesses must apply to the ETA to join.
  - Sources: [Andersen – Law 6/2025 translation](https://eg.andersen.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/Law-No.-6-of-2025-edited.pdf); [EY tax alert](https://www.ey.com/en_gl/technical/tax-alerts/egypt-introduces-tax-incentives-and-benefits-for-small-enterprises); [Daily News Egypt – ETA chief](https://www.dailynewsegypt.com/?p=851383)
  - Conflict: secondary sources cite 1.25% for the EGP 3–10m band, or describe the range as 0.4–1%. — [Beancount guide (secondary)](https://beancount.io/blog/2026/07/26/egypt-law-6-2025-sme-simplified-tax-regime-guide)
  - Exceeding EGP 20m by no more than 20% in any year within the 5-year period lets the business stay in the regime at 1.5% (ETA FAQ via press). — [Amwal Al Ghad – tax FAQs](https://en.amwalalghad.com/?p=200775)
- **E-invoicing and e-receipts:**
  - B2B e-invoices go through ETA's system. E-receipts cover B2C sales to consumers without a tax registration number.
  - A vendor source cites a 31 Mar 2026 deadline for newly in-scope businesses to register for e-invoicing. This is unverified.
  - Sources: [VATit – Egypt e-invoicing mandate](https://vatit.com/?p=90013); [Invopop – Egypt coverage](https://invopop.com/coverage/egypt.md); [Cygnet](https://www.cygnet.one/eg/products/e-invoicing/)

### Inferences
- Sales to advertisers (B2B) would carry 14% VAT and need e-invoices. A sale to an individual or unregistered buyer would need an e-receipt.
- Payments to shop owners for "hosting" a screen could be classed as rent (or rent-like), as services, or as commission. Which category applies changes the WHT rate (3% vs 5%). Payments to riders may be services (3%) if the rider is not an employee, or payroll tax and social insurance if they are. This classification should be confirmed with ETA or an adviser.
- Under Law 6/2025, a startup below EGP 20m turnover could pay 0.4–1.5% turnover tax instead of 22.5% CIT. It must still handle VAT and e-invoicing.

### Gaps
- I could not confirm whether WHT applies when the payee is an unregistered individual (rider) rather than a company, or what the ETA expects when the payee has no tax card.
- I found no current official ETA source on the EGP 250k vs 500k VAT threshold conflict.
- No source covered whether any residual stamp duty applies to advertising contracts themselves (for example, per-document stamp duty).

## 3. Paying riders: employee vs freelancer vs 3PL; Labor Law 14/2025; Social Insurance Law 148/2019

### Takeaway
- In practice, Talabat's Egyptian riders are mostly treated as independent contractors, often recruited and managed through third-party (3PL) contractors.
- New Labor Law 14/2025 (in force 1 Sep 2025) recognises platform work and irregular workers for the first time. Its platform provisions are brief and depend on executive regulations.
- Under Art. 99, proof of employment by any means is allowed. A long-term, controlled, regular payment relationship therefore carries reclassification risk.
- Social insurance (Law 148/2019) for 2026: 18.75% employer and 11% employee contributions on wages between EGP 2,700 and 16,700 a month.

### Cited Findings
- **Labor Law 14/2025:**
  - Took effect 1 September 2025. Replaced the 2003 Labor Law and Law 125/2010. — [Deloitte Middle East](https://www.deloitte.com/middle-east/en/services/tax/perspectives/egypt-new-labor-law-entering-into-effect.html); [JURIST explainer](https://www.jurist.org/features/2025/08/29/explainer-what-egypts-new-labor-law-means-for-workers-and-employers/)
  - English translation: [Andersen translation of Law 14/2025](https://eg.andersen.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/Translation-of-Labor-law-No.-14-of-2025.pdf)
- The law is described as the first Egyptian legislation to formally recognise platform-based work. Masaar/Defense say the platform provisions are "extremely brief" and rely entirely on executive regulations. Art. 99 requires a written contract but allows proof by other means, without specifying minimum data. — [Masaar](https://masaar.net/en/masaar-and-defense-release-research-paper-on-platform-work-in-egypts-new-labor-law); [Masaar (Arabic)](https://masaar.net/modern-work-patterns-in-the-new-labor-law)
- The law formally recognises irregular workers. Employers of irregular workers must contribute to a Protection and Employment Fund / Emergency Aid and Social and Health Services Fund (Art. 8). — [Al-Ahram Weekly](https://english.ahram.org.eg/News/553681.aspx); [Staffing Industry Analysts](https://www.staffingindustry.com/editorial/staffing-industry-review/egypt-introduces-landmark-labor-reforms-with-global-implications)
- Critics say the new law keeps the status quo for gig workers and leaves room for "incomplete or sham contracts". — [Al Manassa](https://almanassa.com/en/stories/31763); [Al Manassa (Talabat)](https://almanassa.com/en/news/27264)
- **Talabat riders:**
  - Treated as independent service providers, not employees, and so outside labour-law protections. — [Zawia3](https://zawia3.com/en/delivery-workers-rights-egypt/); [ARIJ investigation](https://arij.net/investigations/delivery-workers-rights-egypt/en/)
  - Talabat said it has "no obligations" towards couriers and provides insurance "out of goodwill". Its insurance schemes are run through external contractors that recruit and manage couriers. — [Al-Monitor 2022](https://www.al-monitor.com/originals/2022/04/egypt-gig-economy-workers-face-rough-ride)
  - When Delivery Hero rebranded Otlob as Talabat, it announced 50,000 "freelance rider jobs". — [KrASIA](https://kr-asia.com/delivery-hero-is-rebranding-otlob-as-talabat-plans-to-create-50000-freelance-rider-jobs-in-egypt)
- ARIJ survey of delivery workers:
  - 80% had not signed contracts with companies.
  - 77% are paid per order.
  - 97% said their company did not register them with NOSI.
  - Source: [ARIJ](https://arij.net/investigations/delivery-workers-rights-egypt/en/)
- Under Social Insurance Law 148/2019, couriers usually fall in the irregular or self-employed category. Ride-hailing has a separate 2018 law requiring registration; delivery work has no equivalent. The Ministry of Social Solidarity's "Tareeqak Amen" scheme (with Mrsool, later joined by Talabat) offers pension, injury and death coverage. — [Egyptian Streets 2023](https://egyptianstreets.com/2023/04/02/demanding-fairness-towards-better-conditions-for-egypts-digital-platform-workers/); [Fairwork Egypt](https://fair.work/?p=14370)
- **Social insurance 2026:**
  - Insurable wage band EGP 2,700 to 16,700 a month from 1 Jan 2026. The band rises about 15% a year under Law 148/2019.
  - Employer pays 18.75%, employee pays 11%.
  - Sources: [Business Today Egypt](https://www.businesstodayegypt.com/Article/1/6950/Egypt-s-Social-Insurance-Authority-announces-increase-in-wages-starting); [BDO](https://www.bdo.global/en-gb/insights/tax/expatriate-tax/egypt-maximum-and-minimum-salaries-subject-to-social-insurance-increased); [accounts-os payroll guide (secondary)](https://accounts-os.com/eg/questions/what-social-insurance-does-an-employer-pay-in-egypt)
  - A 1% Emergency Aid Fund charge is also reported. This is unverified. — [Teamed guide (secondary)](https://www.teamed.global/country-hiring-guides/egypt/tax-and-payroll)
- **Freelancer registration (Enterprise guide, 11 Oct 2026, search extract only):**
  - Freelancers should issue e-invoices to ETA-registered companies and e-receipts to individuals.
  - A temporary tax card cannot be used to issue e-invoices or receipts.
  - The simplified turnover regime applies since March 2025.
  - Companies can deduct freelancer fees only if the freelancer's unified tax registration number appears on the contract and invoice.
  - The guide also claims most freelancers must register for VAT from day one, and that a "10% table tax" applies. Both are unverified.
  - Source: [Enterprise – registering as a freelancer](https://enterpriseam.com/egypt/2026/10/11/your-guide-to-registering-as-a-freelancer-in-egypt/)

### Inferences
- The startup's arrangement is an ad-hosting fee for carrying a screen. This is ancillary to the rider's main work for Talabat or a 3PL. If the startup does not direct hours, routes or attendance, it looks more like a services or rental contract than employment.
- Reclassification risk rises if the startup:
  - mandates routes or hours,
  - pays a fixed periodic wage, or
  - imposes discipline.
  Art. 99's "proof by any means" makes informal arrangements risky.
- Expense deductibility: if riders lack a tax registration number, payments to them may not be deductible for CIT. This is per the Enterprise guide's deduction rule, extended here by analogy and unverified for non-creator businesses. Consider paying through a 3PL or rider-aggregator that issues e-invoices.
- Talabat's own rider contracts or 3PL terms may forbid third-party branding on bags or boxes. This is a commercial rather than legal risk and was not researched.

### Gaps
- The Law 14/2025 executive regulations on platform work were not found, so their issuance status as of Nov 2026 is unconfirmed.
- I could not retrieve the exact article numbers defining platform work in Law 14/2025.
- I found no source on how ETA treats small one-off payments to unregistered individuals (for example, below a threshold).

## 4. Payment channels: CBE rules for mobile wallets / InstaPay, AML limits

### Takeaway
InstaPay caps are reportedly EGP 70k per transaction, 120k per day and 400k per month, with a 0.1% fee (EGP 0.50–20). Individual mobile-wallet limits were doubled to EGP 60k a day and 200k a month. Freelancers and self-employed people have the same limits; micro-enterprises have 80k/400k. Both are well above typical rider or shop payouts. Bulk disbursement to many wallets would still need a licensed bank or PSP and KYC'd recipients.

### Cited Findings
- InstaPay limits: EGP 70,000 per transaction, 120,000 per day, 400,000 per month. A 0.1% fee (min EGP 0.50, max EGP 20) has applied since April 2025. No 2026 change was found. — [Egypt Independent](https://www.egyptindependent.com/central-bank-of-egypt-modifies-transaction-limits-on-instapay-application/); [PhotonPay guide](https://www.photonpay.com/hk/blog/article/instapay-egypt?lang=en); [Zawya – InstaPay profitable after fee](https://www.zawya.com/en/economy/north-africa/egypt-instapay-turns-profitable-a-year-after-transfer-fee-rollout-rt18pyrt)
- CBE doubled limits for mobile payment services, prepaid cards and financial-inclusion accounts:
  - Individuals: daily EGP 30k to 60k, monthly 100k to 200k.
  - Micro-enterprises: daily 40k to 80k, monthly 200k to 400k.
  - Unlicensed micro-enterprises, self-employed and freelancers: 60k daily, 200k monthly.
  - Sources: [Business Today Egypt](https://www.businesstodayegypt.com/Article/1/3521/CBE-raises-limits-on-prepaid-cards-mobile-banking-transactions); [CairoScene](https://cairoscene.com/Buzz/Central-Bank-of-Egypt-Raises-Limits-on-Prepaid-Cards-by-100)
- Some bank wallets list lower caps, for example 30k a day and 50k a month. — [Banky – AlAhli Bank of Kuwait wallet](https://www.bankygate.com/en/products/ewallets/174)
- Through Sep 2026 there were 67 million mobile wallets, with transaction values above EGP 4 trillion, and instant payments above EGP 7 trillion. — [Ahram Online](https://english.ahram.org.eg/News/581997.aspx)
- Legal requirements for the Instant Payment Network are summarised by Andersen. — [Andersen – IPN](https://eg.andersen.com/instant-payment-network-egypt/)

### Inferences
- Per-rider monthly ad fees will be far below the wallet and InstaPay caps. The practical constraints are KYC, bulk-payout tooling (bank or PSP disbursement APIs) and keeping payee records for WHT and e-receipts.

### Gaps
- The effective date of the CBE wallet-limit doubling and the original CBE circular were not found.
- No source covered CBE or EMLCU (AML unit) rules specific to business-to-individual bulk disbursements or reporting thresholds.

## 5. Data protection: PDPL 151/2020 and Executive Regulations

### Takeaway
The Executive Regulations (Decree 816/2025) were dated 1 Nov 2025 and published 2 Nov 2025. They started a one-year compliance window that ended about 1–2 Nov 2026. Most controllers and processors now need a licence or permit from the Personal Data Protection Centre (PDPC), and cross-border transfers need a separate licence. GPS tracking of riders or audience-measurement cameras would be processing under the law.

### Cited Findings
- The Executive Regulations were issued as Decree No. 816 of 2025, dated 1 Nov 2025, and in force from Gazette publication on 2 Nov 2025. — [CMS](https://cms.law/en/are/legal-updates/egypt-s-pdpl-executive-regulations-issued-one-year-compliance-countdown-begins); [Al Tamimi](https://www.tamimi.com/news/from-policy-to-practice-egypt-issues-executive-regulations-of-the-personal-data-protection-law); [Digital Policy Alert](https://digitalpolicyalert.org/event/36529-executive-regulations-of-the-personal-data-protection-law-decision-no-816-of-2025-including-organisational-requirements-entered-into-force)
- Conflict on issuing authority: Al Tamimi says it was a Minister of Communications decree; Kennedys says it was a Prime Ministerial decree. — [Kennedys 2026](https://www.kennedyslaw.com/en/thought-leadership/article/2026/egypt-s-personal-data-protection-law-the-compliance-countdown-has-begun)
- The text reached law firms only around 25 Dec 2025. — [Clyde & Co, Jan 2026](https://www.clydeco.com/en/insights/2026/01/egypt-regulatory-update-on-data-privacy)
- Requirements:
  - One-year compliance deadline, about 1 Nov 2026.
  - Most controllers and processors must obtain a licence or permit from the PDPC.
  - Cross-border transfers need a separate PDPC licence or permit (Art. 16).
  - Mandatory breach notification.
  - Non-compliance can bring financial or criminal penalties.
  - Sources: [CMS](https://cms.law/en/are/legal-updates/egypt-s-pdpl-executive-regulations-issued-one-year-compliance-countdown-begins); [Chambers](https://chambers.com/articles/a-first-look-at-egypt-s-personal-data-protection-executive-regulations); [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/privacy-protection/1731130/the-issuance-of-the-executive-regulations-of-the-data-protection-law-and-the-establishment-of-the-data-protection-centre)

### Inferences
- Rider GPS location is personal data. Camera-based audience analytics in public places could involve biometric (sensitive) data, which typically carries stricter licensing and consent. The startup should plan to hold a PDPC licence before launch, plus a cross-border transfer licence if it uses foreign cloud or CMS. Anonymised counting at the edge (no images stored) would reduce exposure.

### Gaps
- PDPC licence fees, processing timelines and the sensitive-data rules for camera or biometric analytics were not retrieved.
- I found no confirmation of any post-Nov-2026 extension or grace period.

## 6. Product import & conformity (LED displays) and 7. Telecom (NTRA type approval, SIMs)

### Takeaway
- Imported LED products may fall under the GOEIC factory/brand-registration regime (Decree 43/2016, amended 2022). Imports must come from registered factories or trademark owners.
- Any unit with a cellular, Wi-Fi or Bluetooth radio needs NTRA type approval, and cellular units need IMEI registration before import.
- Type approval reportedly does not by itself grant an import permit. 3G-only devices are reportedly banned from 1 Oct 2025 (unverified).

### Cited Findings
- **Decree 43/2016:** imported goods on the list are released only if made in GOEIC-registered factories or imported from registered trademark owners. In force from 15 March 2016. Registration needs a trademark certificate and an accredited QC-system certificate. — [GTAI (German trade agency)](https://www.gtai.de/gtai-de/trade/zoll/aegyptische-standards-und-registrierungspflichten-fuer-hersteller-546126.pdf); [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/International-Law/505276/Trade-Mark-Registration-Now-Required-To-Export-Certain-Products-To-Egypt)
- GTAI lists "LED lamps, except for vehicles" among covered goods. The HS range it gives looks odd, so verify against the annex. — [GTAI](https://www.gtai.de/gtai-de/trade/zoll/aegyptische-standards-und-registrierungspflichten-fuer-hersteller-546126.pdf)
- Conflict on the 2022 amending decree: GTAI cites Ministerial Decree 195/2022 (simplified registration, added a time limit). TUV SUD cites Decree 96/2022 (May 2022, covering discharge lamps, ballasts and energy-saving bulbs). — [GTAI](https://www.gtai.de/gtai-de/trade/zoll/aegyptische-standards-und-registrierungspflichten-fuer-hersteller-546126.pdf); [TUV SUD Egypt market access](https://www.tuvsud.com/en-us/services/product-certification/global-market-access/egypt)

- **NTRA type approval:** NTRA publishes Light, Intermediate and Tight type-approval scheme procedures, with a 2026 version of the Intermediate procedure. Requirements include:
  - samples, including a cellular WAN conducted sample, for cellular products
  - IMEI numbers per carton submitted to NTRA before market entry
  - a ban on counterfeit devices
  - model names in shipments that match the approved product
  - a separate approval for locally manufactured units
  - Sources: [NTRA Intermediate TA Procedure v1 2026](https://www.tra.gov.eg/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/Doc-03-Intermediate-Type-Approval-Scheme-Procedure-Version1-2026.pdf); [NTRA Light TA Procedure v1 2025](https://www.tra.gov.eg/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/Doc-01-Light-Type-Approval-Scheme-Procedure-Version-1-2025.pdf); [NTRA Tight TA Procedure v1 2025](https://www.tra.gov.eg/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/Doc-02-Tight-Type-Approval-Scheme-Procedure-Version-1-2025.pdf)

### Inferences
- Choosing an LTE module that already has NTRA approval (for example, Cat-1 modules like the EG915U) should simplify approval of the finished screen controller. The finished device may still need its own approval.
- IoT/M2M SIMs would come from licensed Egyptian MNOs (Vodafone, Orange, e&, WE). No source covered NTRA rules on M2M SIM registration, so this is a gap.
