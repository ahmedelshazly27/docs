# Park-first LED trucks clear the licensing bar

Mobile LED billboard trucks can be licensed in every target market except possibly Saudi Arabia, but **no market other than Dubai and Oman publishes a licence category written for them**. Everywhere else, the founder will be asking a regulator to apply an existing vehicle-ad route by analogy. In **Kuwait** the closest template is the Municipality's taxi "investment ad" licence (TR133). It already permits **third-party moving TV screens at KD 30/m², with a KD 1,000 guarantee and General Traffic Department approval**. The December 2025 amendments to the Advertising Regulation bring vehicles and electronic screens into scope, and unlicensed commercial ads draw **KD 3,000–5,000** fines. In **Egypt**, Traffic Law Art. 69 bis covers any vehicle ad. The licence costs up to **EGP 10,000 and lasts up to 3 years**, and only an Interior-Ministry-approved joint-stock ad company with **at least EGP 30m in capital** may apply. An ad-only vehicle fleet (SeenX) already runs in Cairo. Licensing is not the binding constraint. The operational rules are: approval of the screen-and-generator conversion as an "external modification", heavy-truck time bans (Cairo reportedly **6 a.m. to midnight**, Kuwait at peak hours), and an unwritten but consistent regional hostility to video on moving vehicles, which **Oman makes explicit by requiring the screen to be off while driving**. Compared with the delivery-box model from the earlier report, trucks are **easier to license than motorcycle boxes** and roughly as easy as shop windows. They avoid Kuwait's "identifying ad only" motorcycle rule, the rider-sponsorship problem and platform kit conflicts. But they are exactly the "primary-purpose advertising vehicle" that New York and Los Angeles ban. The recommended launch is **light-chassis box vans that run static or switched-off screens on the move and play video only while parked at licensed private sites**, piloted with one or two converted vehicles and written Municipality and Traffic positions before any fleet purchase. Nearly all evidence comes from search-engine extracts, because most official and news sites were blocked, and several figures conflict. Every number below is a planning figure for local counsel to verify, not a quote.

## Evidence is thin on trucks specifically, so confidence is rated throughout

The research sessions could not open the primary sources that matter most. Blocked sites included the Kuwait Municipality site, Arab Times, Al-Anba's PDF archive, Times Kuwait, Al-Jarida, gov.om, Khaleej Times, Dubizzle, the Dubai Out-of-Home Advertising Manual, gov.uk, Balady and the Madinah Amanah document library. Findings therefore rest on search-result summaries of the cited pages. More importantly, **almost no source anywhere in the region addresses LED trucks by name**. Most of this report is an inference from general vehicle-ad, traffic and signage rules, with the inference flagged as such. The company-setup, tax, labour, privacy and product-conformity material from the earlier delivery-box report (*LED delivery ad platform regulation*) applies to a truck business largely unchanged. That covers Kuwait WLL/KDIPA ownership, Egypt's Decree 962/2012 agency question, VAT and withholding, PDPL licensing, and KUCAS/NTRA import approvals. It is not repeated here.

## Kuwait: a taxi licence is the template, and the truck bans are the trap

### Licensing would run through the Municipality by analogy to TR133

Kuwait Municipality licenses all outdoor advertising. The amended Advertising Regulation that the Municipal Council approved in late December 2025 defines an advertising medium as any **fixed or mobile, traditional or electronic means, expressly including vehicles and electronic screens** ([Times Kuwait](https://timeskuwait.com/municipal-council-approves-sweeping-amendments-to-advertising-regulations/)). A truck carrying third-party screens is therefore inside the regime. The open question is which fee line it falls under, because no category called "advertising truck" or "mobile screens" (شاحنات إعلانية / عربات إعلانية متنقلة) has been found. The gazetted ministerial resolution giving effect to the December 2025 text was also not located (confidence: high on scope, low on category).

The best precedent is **transaction TR133**, the "investment advertisement" licence on taxis. It already licenses third-party ads, **including moving TV screens, at KD 30/m², other ads at KD 20/m² per face, a KD 1,000 guarantee, Traffic Department approval and a copy of the vehicle registration** ([Kuwait Municipality TR133](https://www.baladia.gov.kw/sites/ar/Pages/transactionsRequirements/TR133.aspx)). A parallel service exists for public-transport vehicles ([e.gov.kw](https://e.gov.kw/sites/kgoarabic/Pages/Services/KM/ApplyingforIssuingAdvertisementLicensesonPublicTransportVehicles.aspx)). This matters more for trucks than it did for delivery boxes. It shows that Kuwait already accepts **third-party video screens on a licensed commercial vehicle** in principle. A purpose-built ad truck is the same thing at larger scale. The Municipality could instead classify a truck's screens as electronic screens, charged at the fixed-site rate of KD 75–100/m², or apply the 2024 proposal of **KD 5/month for "mobile vehicles" and KD 75/m²/year for moving electronic ads** ([Al-Jarida](https://www.aljarida.com/article/56921); [Arab Times/PressReader](https://www.pressreader.com/kuwait/arab-times/20240325/281543705922571)). The December 2025 amendments also reportedly set a **KD 100 annual fee for commercial ads placed through advertising companies** ([Al-Anba PDF, 23 Dec 2025](https://pdf.alanba.com.kw/pdf/2025/12/23-12-2025/06.pdf)). The Kuwait Portal's ad-licence e-service states only that fees are "determined according to the Advertising Regulation and the measurements entered" ([e.gov.kw](https://e.gov.kw/sites/kgoArabic/Pages/eServices/KM/AdvertisementLicenseInquiry.aspx)).

The table below shows what those candidate rates imply. These are illustrative calculations, not published truck fees. They assume the TR133 rate is annual, which the source extract does not state.

| Fee basis (by analogy) | Van, two 3×2 m faces (12 m²) | Large truck, two 6×3 m faces (36 m²) |
|---|---|---|
| TR133 moving-screen rate, KD 30/m² | ~KD 360/yr + KD 1,000 guarantee | ~KD 1,080/yr + KD 1,000 guarantee |
| Electronic-screen rate, KD 75–100/m² | ~KD 900–1,200/yr | ~KD 2,700–3,600/yr |
| 2024 mobile-vehicle proposal, KD 5/month | KD 60/yr (if adopted) | KD 60/yr (if adopted) |

At any of these rates the licence fee is small next to the cost of an LED truck. The real exposure is operating without a licence: **KD 3,000–5,000 for a commercial ad "by any means" without a Municipality licence** ([Al-Anba PDF](https://pdf.alanba.com.kw/pdf/2025/12/23-12-2025/06.pdf)). Enforcement is active. A single "renew your ad" campaign logged **1,464 violations in 10 days**, though none involved trucks ([Al-Jarida](https://www.aljarida.com/articles/1471687134064055700)). From about April 2027, Media Regulation Decree-Law 102/2026 adds a Ministry of Information licensing layer for advertising content ([GLA & Co](https://www.glaco.com/blog/kuwaits-new-media-law-one-framework-for-print-broadcast-digital-media-and-influencers/)), as the earlier report describes.

### Converting a truck is an "external modification" that needs Traffic sign-off

No Kuwaiti procedure for converting a truck body to carry screens and a generator was found. The 2025 Traffic Law treats unapproved external changes as offences. **Decree-Law 5/2025** raised fines to KD 15–10,000 and set fines of **up to KD 200 for unauthorised external modifications** ([Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/world/gulf/kuwait/kuwait-enacts-tougher-traffic-laws-and-harsher-penalties-for-violations-1.500022722); [Kuwait Times](https://kuwaittimes.com/article/26783/kuwait/kuwait-enforces-tougher-traffic-laws-tuesday)). Vehicles that breach safety requirements or make disturbing noise face **60-day impoundment** ([Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/world/gulf/kuwait/kuwait-to-impound-noisy-cars-to-curb-reckless-driving-1.500040167)). The Traffic Department already treats **driver-controlled electronic boards on rear glass as a "serious" violation leading to impoundment** ([Al-Jarida](https://www.aljarida.com/article/47209)). An unapproved screen box is plainly within reach of these rules. Licence renewal includes a technical inspection ([e.gov.kw](https://e.gov.kw/sites/kgoArabic/Pages/Services/MOI/RenewVehicleLicense.aspx)), so a converted truck will meet an inspector at least once a year. The practical step is to convert one vehicle, get a written General Traffic Department no-objection, and pass inspection before ordering a fleet. No published limits on screen-body dimensions, weight or plate class for ad vehicles were found (confidence: high that approval is needed; low on criteria).

### Peak-hour truck bans favour a van over a heavy chassis

Kuwait's MOI banned trucks from main roads at peak hours from **1 September 2025 to 14 June 2026**, with Ramadan peak-hour bans, and narrowed the ban to **12:30–3:30 p.m. for 15 June–31 August 2026** ([Arab Times](https://www.arabtimesonline.com/news/kuwait-to-ban-trucks-on-main-roads-during-peak-hours-from-sept-1); [Arab Times, Ramadan](https://www.arabtimesonline.com/news/trucks-banned-during-peak-hours-in-ramadan-across-kuwait); [Al-Rai](https://www.alraimedia.com/ampArticle/1738312)). Specific corridors have their own hours, such as Wafra Highway's 6–8 a.m. and 1–3 p.m. ([Kuwait Times](https://kuwaittimes.com/new-truck-ban-hours-on-wafra-highway-306/major-general-abdullah-al-muhanna-3/)). Peak hours are when an ad truck earns most of its impressions, so the business case depends on staying outside the "truck" definition. **The weight or class threshold used for these bans was not found**, and the schedule after August 2026 is unconfirmed. The working assumption is that a light commercial box van or pickup, below the heavy-vehicle class, escapes the bans. That is an inference counsel must confirm against the MOI's vehicle categories (confidence: medium on the bans, low on the threshold).

### Video on the move is unwritten but disfavoured; parking is the real business

No Kuwaiti rule addresses video on a moving truck. The signals point in both directions. TR133 explicitly allows **moving TV-screen ads** on taxis. The draft December 2025 rules, however, limited delivery motorcycles to a **fixed electronic panel with non-moving content**, approved by General Traffic ([Arab Times](https://www.arabtimesonline.com/news/new-advertising-rules-introduced-in-kuwait-fines-fees-and-standards-updated/)). An MP has also questioned ad brightness and noise standards ([Al-Jarida](https://www.aljarida.com/article/113889)). Fixed electronic boards must sit about 400 m apart on highways and are **banned in residential areas and on bridges** ([Times Kuwait](https://timeskuwait.com/kuwait-tightens-street-advertising-rules-to-boost-road-safety-and-urban-aesthetics/)). A Municipality that bans fixed screens in residential areas is unlikely to welcome a parked LED truck there. Residents were already complaining in June 2026 about trucks parked at random in their neighbourhoods ([Arab Times](https://www.arabtimesonline.com/news/random-parking-of-trucks-buses-irks-area-residents/)).

Parking on the street to advertise carries the heaviest sanction found. Administrative Circular 9/2025 allows **two-month impoundment for parking in prohibited places** and for obstructing the road ([Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/world/gulf/kuwait/kuwait-imposes-tougher-penalties-on-dangerous-driving-vehicles-to-be-impounded-up-to-two-months-1.500318661)). The defensible model is to **run static content or screens off while moving, and play full video while parked on private land** (mall forecourts, event sites, showroom lots) with the landowner's consent. For events, the Municipality licenses occasion ads, with a reported **KD 500 fine for an unlicensed occasion ad**. It kept a **KD 500 fee for temporary-exhibition licences** ([Al-Anba PDF, 14 Nov 2025](https://pdf.alanba.com.kw/pdf/2025/11/14-11-2025/04.pdf)), and a **KD 5/m²/month seasonal-ad fee** was proposed ([Al-Jarida](https://www.aljarida.com/article/56921)). One of these is the likeliest route for a truck parked at an event. On sound, given the noise-impoundment campaign, external speakers should be off except under an event permit, and a battery/inverter system is safer than an open-frame generator. No Kuwait EPA rule on mobile generators was found.

### Nobody visible is running LED trucks in Kuwait

No Kuwaiti LED-truck operator surfaced in Arabic or English searches. Only event screen-rental firms such as Alhafiz ([alhafiz.com](https://alhafiz.com/event-management-company/LED-screens-and-displays-rental-for-events)) and fixed DOOH players turned up. A market report lists "mobile LED trailers and truck-mounted displays" as a segment of a **US$370m GCC outdoor LED display market (2025)**, with no Kuwait breakdown ([Ken Research](https://www.kenresearch.com/industry-reports/gcc-outdoor-led-display-market.md)). An empty field is ambiguous. It may mean opportunity, or it may mean earlier entrants found the two-regulator approval too slow. Either way, there is no Kuwaiti precedent to cite to an official, which makes written pre-clearance more valuable (confidence: medium that no operator exists; searches were limited).

## Egypt: one statute governs every vehicle ad, and Cairo's truck curfew shapes the fleet

### Art. 69 bis licenses the truck, but only through a gatekeeper company

Since Law 161/2021, **Traffic Law Art. 69 bis requires a licence from the competent traffic department for any ad on a vehicle**. The fee is capped at **EGP 10,000** (bands set by executive regulations), and the licence lasts up to **3 years**, renewable ([Ahram Gate](https://gate.ahram.org.eg/News/3082998.aspx); [Dostor](https://www.dostor.org/3615067)). Under Art. 69 bis 2, **only Interior-Ministry-approved advertising companies may apply, and each must be a joint-stock company with at least EGP 30m of issued capital** ([Youm7](https://www.youm7.com/story/2022/1/14/ما-هى-الشركات-التى-يحق-لها-الإعلان-على-المركبات-قانون/5615118)). Violations cost **EGP 1,000–5,000 each**, and the vehicle's licence can be cancelled ([El Balad](https://www.elbalad.news/5399695)). In 2025 the Interior Ministry confirmed two offences: an unlicensed ad on a vehicle, and **using a vehicle for a purpose other than the one it is licensed for**. It said owners can regularise by obtaining "licences to advertise for others" (تراخيص إعلان للغير) ([Egypt Telegraph](https://www.egypttelegraph.com/article/201488/بعد-شكوى-سائقي-التوصيل،-الداخلية-وضع-الإعلانات); [El Watan](https://www.elwatannews.com/news/details/8184906)).

The second offence matters more for trucks than for delivery boxes. A delivery bike carries food and an ad. An ad truck carries nothing but the ad. If the truck is registered as a goods vehicle, it is arguably being used for an unlicensed purpose even when the ad itself is licensed. **Register the vehicle's purpose as advertising from the outset**, and get the traffic department to confirm that the registration and the Art. 69 bis licence are consistent (inference; confidence: medium). The fee bands and the list of approved companies were not found. Fixed roadside ads fall under a separate statute, Law 208/2020, which NAPRA administers with a GIS register and new sign-licensing rules from April 2026 ([Amwal Al-Ghad](https://en.amwalalghad.com/?p=229141); [Masrawy](https://www.masrawy.com/news/news_egypt/details/2026/4/27/2978681/-تنظيم-الإعلانات-وضع-قواعد-وضوابط-جديدة-لترخيص-اللافتات-على-الطرق-)). No source applies Law 208 to vehicles. A truck parked for hours at a roadside could still look to a local inspector like an unlicensed roadside sign, so parked operation belongs on private land.

### Lit vehicle ads are spreading, but no screen standard exists

Egypt is moving toward more illuminated vehicle advertising, not less. In July 2026 Cairo Governorate was preparing **illuminated ads on white taxis**, with door ads approved and contracts pending ([Parlmany](https://www.parlmany.com/News/7/247427/القاهرة-تتجه-لوضع-إعلانات-مضيئة-بالتاكسى-الأبيض)). No rule on vehicle screen brightness, video or sound was found. Brightness is nonetheless a live public issue: drivers complain about dazzling roadside LED billboards at night ([Asharq](https://asharq.com/reports/153110/إعلانات-الطرق-بالقاهرة-زيادة-في-الإيرادات-وإرهاق-للعيون); [inkl](https://www.inkl.com/news/egypt-s-roads-face-a-new-hazard-flashing-billboards-and-adverts)). The April 2026 NAPRA lighting rules require illuminated roadside ads to be off from midnight to 5 a.m. Signs with sensors that auto-dim to 37.5% by day and 4% at night are exempt from the reported daytime ban ([Ahram Online](https://english.ahram.org.eg/NewsContentP/1/567028/Egypt/Egypt-introduces-new-billboard-lighting-regulation.aspx)). Whether these rules reach a vehicle is unknown. Building the same dimming profile into the truck costs little and pre-empts the obvious objection. Headlamp-style enforcement against non-compliant xenon/LED lighting also exists ([Banker.news](https://banker.news/136650)), though its reach to ad screens is unclear. Egypt's Environment Law 4/1994, its Decree 338/1995 Annex 7, and Law 9/2009 set noise and exhaust limits ([FAOLEX](https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/egy4984E.pdf); [ECOLEX](https://www.ecolex.org/details/legislation/environmental-law-no9-of-2009-amending-some-provisions-of-law-no4-of-1994-on-environment-lex-faoc152133/)). The numeric values were not retrieved. That points again to quiet power and no external audio.

### Greater Cairo's heavy-truck curfew rules out big chassis

In April 2026 the Cairo Chamber of Commerce asked for a change to Greater Cairo rules under which **heavy transport is banned inside cities, including downtown, from 6 a.m. to midnight** ([Maspero](https://www.maspero.eg/economy/2026/04/17/946313/غرفة-القاهرة-تطالب-بتعديل-سير-سيارات-النقل-الثقيل)). A separate ban covers heavy transport on the Ring Road from 6 a.m. to noon, enforced by checkpoints ([Hatla2ee](https://eg.hatla2ee.com/news/60166/حظر-سير-النقل-الثقيل-على-الدائري-من-6-ص-الي-12-م/); [Hatla2ee](https://eg.hatla2ee.com/news/50454/أكمنة-مرورية-ودوريات-على-الدائري-لضبط-سيارات-النقل-المخالفة-للحظر/)). A heavy LED truck would be off Cairo's streets for 18 hours a day, so **the Egyptian fleet must be light commercial vehicles**. The governing decree and its weight threshold were not found, and a new traffic law was still being debated in July 2026 ([Parlmany](https://www.parlmany.com/News/7/231301/النواب-يناقشون-قانون-المرور-الجديد)). Both could change the categories (confidence: medium on the bans, low on detail).

### Ad-only vehicle fleets already exist in Cairo

Egypt has the region's clearest evidence that the model is tolerated. **SeenX**, launched around 2025, describes a fleet of vehicles "used only for ads" carrying GPS-tracked LED screens, sold with route targeting and impression reporting ([Startup Scene](https://thestartupscene.me/MenaEcosystems/SeenX-Launches-to-Disrupt-Traditional-Outdoor-Advertising-in-Egypt)). Its claim of 15–24m monthly impressions, attributed to CAPMAS, is unverified. **Street Level** markets digital screens on trucks and delivery bikes, sold per play by area and time slot ([streetleveleg.com](https://streetleveleg.com/)), and **Routen Media** runs a route-based vehicle screen network across Cairo ([routenmedia.com](https://routenmedia.com/)). None publishes its licensing basis. Each either works through an approved EGP 30m company or operates in a grey zone. Asking them, or their advertisers, which approved company files their Art. 69 bis licences is the fastest way to learn real enforcement practice. Any of them could also be a launch partner (confidence: high that they exist; unknown how they are licensed).

## The wider region ranges from an explicit Dubai permit to Oman's parked-only rule

Only two of the six later markets have something written for this product. **Dubai's RTA** "Advertising on Vehicles" permit has a category for **trailers, semi-trailers and mobile advertising vehicles**. It requires a Dubai-issued advertising trade licence, an all-sides design mock-up and approval from the RTA Vehicle Licensing Department, and a reported annual fee of **AED 50,000 for advertising companies with mobile vehicles or trailers**, against AED 1,500 for a fully wrapped pickup ([Khaleej Times](https://khaleejtimes.com/uae/dubai-want-to-put-company-logo-ad-on-your-car-how-to-get-a-permit-from-rta?amp=1); [Meydan FZ](https://www.meydanfz.ae/blog/vehicle-advertising-permit)). The figure may be dated and is ambiguous between per company and per vehicle. The authoritative Dubai Out-of-Home Advertising Manual could not be read ([Dubai OOH manual](https://visitdubai.com/-/media/Files/invest-in-dubai/rta-manuals/out-of-home-new-manual-from-rta.pdf)). **Oman** licenses an "electronic screen on a commercial vehicle" for a reported **OMR 2,500**, on condition that the screen **does not operate while the vehicle is moving** and the vehicle does not park near highways or main roads. Ads on private vehicles are prohibited ([Gov.om](https://gov.om/en/w/add-a-mobile-advertisement-electronic-screen-on-a-commercial-vehicle); [Gov.om licence](https://gov.om/w/get-advertising-license-4)). That makes Oman a parked-billboard market by law.

Saudi Arabia is the hardest to read. MOMAH's sign conditions list a separate category for **signs on mobile vehicles** ([Amana Madinah PDF](https://services.amana-md.gov.sa/DigitLibPanel/StaticFile/PdfFile//9eec7309-c603-4785-97cf-2b29fe992fa6.pdf)). An August 2025 MOMAH document on **municipal conditions for mobile vehicles** defines advertising signs and screens ([MOMAH PDF](https://momah.gov.sa/sites/default/files/2025-08/alashtratat%20albldyt%20ll%60rbat%20almtnqlt%20%283%29.pdf)). Vehicle signs need both municipal and traffic approval ([Balady FAQ](https://balady.gov.sa/ar/help-and-support/faq-list?page=43)). But the operative wording found covers boards on commercial vehicles "for the same owner of the vehicle", which suggests third-party ads are not the default ([Qanoniah](https://qanoniah.com/en/File/mJy4DGw9da7v68J78Qv65RrKX-%D9%82%D9%88%D8%A7%D8%B9%D8%AF-%D8%AA%D9%86%D8%B8%D9%8A%D9%85-%D9%84%D9%88%D8%AD%D8%A7%D8%AA-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AF%D8%B9%D8%A7%D9%8A%D8%A9-%D9%88%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A5%D8%B9%D9%84%D8%A7%D9%86)). **MediaGo** claims, with Jeddah Transport Co., to be "the first Saudi company licensed and regulated" for screens on moving vehicles ([Campaign ME](https://campaignme.com/mediago-mobile-dooh-platform-jeddah/)). That points to a partner-with-a-licensee route. Qatar, Bahrain and Jordan license vehicle ads generically and have nothing truck-specific.

## Abroad, the ad-only vehicle is the regulator's favourite target

International precedent is a risk lens, not law for these markets, and it points at the truck's core weakness. **Regulators distinguish vehicles whose primary purpose is advertising from vehicles that carry ads incidentally**, and they restrict the former. New York City already treats mobile billboard trucks as illegal under traffic rule **34 RCNY §4-12(j)**. Courts upheld summonses against cargoless "moving billboard" trucks, and Council bills (Int. 75-2022, Int. 541-2024) sought to codify the ban ([Int. 541](https://intro.nyc/0541-2024+); [FindLaw](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/ny-supreme-court-appellate-division/1132850.html)). California defines a "mobile billboard advertising display" by its **primary purpose of advertising** (Vehicle Code §395.5) and lets cities regulate it. Los Angeles banned parking such displays on any public street in 2010, and the **Ninth Circuit upheld the ordinances as content-neutral in 2016** ([CBS LA](https://www.cbsnews.com/losangeles/news/la-city-council-approves-ban-on-mobile-billboards); [Justia](https://us9thcircuitcourtofappealsopinions.justia.com/2016/07/08/lone-star-sec-video-v-city-of-los-angeles)). San Francisco's Transportation Code has findings against parking trucks and vans on public streets to display ads ([SF Transportation Code](https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/san_francisco/latest/sf_transportation/0-0-0-51846)). The UK requires express consent for ad vehicles **parked in fields, verges or lay-bys**, and exempts only vehicles normally used as moving vehicles ([PAS forum](https://khub.net:443/web/planningadvisoryservicepas/forum/-/message_boards/message/6221997)). In Australia, NSW vehicle-standards guidance governs mobile advertising units ([NSW RMS VSI 35](https://www.nsw.gov.au/sites/default/files/2021-02/RMS-12.045-Mobile-advertising-units-Vehicle-Standards-Information-No-35-September-2012.pdf)). One operator says it **cannot show video within sight of drivers** because road rules ban dazzling or distracting lights ([CarAdvice](https://www.Caradvice.Com.Au/caradvice/are-mobile-billboards-legal/)), and Brisbane moved to ban flashing or moving digital signs ([B&T](https://www.bandt.com.au/Brisbane-to-ban-flashing-and-moving-digital-signs/)).

Five concerns recur: kerb-space occupation by a vehicle that exists only to advertise, driver distraction from video, congestion from slow looping, visual clutter, and idling emissions. Gulf regulators already show the first two. Oman switches screens off on the move, Saudi Arabia ties vehicle signs to the owner's own business, and Kuwait restricts motorcycles to non-moving content. A truck application in Kuwait or Dubai will likely be read through the same lens. The mitigations are cheap, and they should be offered up front rather than conceded later: static or slow-transition content while moving, ambient-light auto-dimming, no audio, fixed and declared routes that avoid congestion hot spots, no slow looping, no parking on public streets without a permit, and battery power instead of an idling engine.

## Trucks beat delivery boxes on licensing but carry more operating risk

The earlier report found that the delivery-box model depends on other people's vehicles. In Kuwait, licensing hits the motorcycle "identifying ad only, non-moving content" rule. Paying sponsored expatriate riders breaches Article 19 of the Residence Law. Platforms such as Talabat control rider kit and already have an incumbent partner in Flyby. Trucks remove most of that. The company owns or leases the vehicle, employs drivers on its own sponsorship, holds the licence in its own name, and needs no platform's consent. Kuwait's TR133 shows the Municipality already licenses third-party moving screens on a commercial vehicle. Egypt's SeenX shows an ad-only fleet operating under Art. 69 bis. Dubai has a named permit category. On licensing, **trucks are clearly easier than motorcycle boxes**.

Trucks are not easier on every axis. A truck is the archetypal "primary-purpose advertising vehicle" that foreign cities ban, while a delivery box rides on a trip that would happen anyway. Trucks also face weight-based curfews, conversion approval and parking enforcement that a bike box mostly escapes, and each unit is a large capital outlay that one impoundment idles for two months. Shop windows remain the simplest per-site licence, though Kuwait's third-party facade question and Egypt's lighting curfew still apply to them.

The recommended approach follows from that comparison. **Launch Kuwait with a small fleet of light-chassis box vans** and set the operating model to the strictest plausible rule: screens off or showing static frames while driving, full video only while parked on private sites under landowner contracts and, for events, an occasion or temporary licence. Pair the trucks with the shop-window product, which shares the Municipality relationship and creative pipeline. **Enter Egypt through a licensed partner**, either an approved Art. 69 bis company or an existing operator such as SeenX or Street Level, rather than by capitalising a JSC on day one. Then **sequence the region by regulatory clarity**: Bahrain and Dubai first (explicit or simple routes), Qatar next, Oman as a parked-only event market, Jordan once evidence improves, and Saudi Arabia only through a transport or advertising licensee in the MediaGo–JTC mould.

## Conclusion

The truck question turns the earlier report's problem inside out. Delivery-box screens were hard to license because someone else owned the vehicle, the rider and the platform relationship. Trucks put all of that in the founder's hands, and the remaining friction is the founder's own operating discipline: what plays while moving, where the truck parks, and how heavy it is. That makes the regulatory risk controllable by product design in a way the box model never was. The cost is that a truck is the most conspicuous form of ad vehicle there is, and the global record shows that cities which tolerate the format at first often end up banning it from their kerbs.

The practical implication is that the first licence matters more than the first fleet. In Kuwait no visible operator exists, so whoever obtains the first written Municipality and Traffic position will effectively write the category, including whether moving video is allowed at all. A single approved van with a "parked video, static moving" operating plan is a stronger asset than ten unapproved trucks. Since the market is unoccupied, that precedent is worth pursuing before Flyby-style incumbents or the 2027 media-licensing layer change the terms. Every fee, threshold and rule cited here should be confirmed by local counsel against gazetted texts before vehicles are bought.
