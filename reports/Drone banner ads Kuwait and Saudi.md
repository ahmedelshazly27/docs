# Kuwait grounds drones; Saudi sells sky shows

**Side street cannot fly any drone advertising in Kuwait today.** The Ministry of Interior (MOI) banned all civilian drone flying and aerial photography nationwide on **3 March 2026**, after the Iranian strikes that included drone attacks on Kuwait International Airport. It reaffirmed the ban "until further notice" on **15 September 2026**, and the researchers found no report that it has been lifted. Drone sales have also been banned in shops since April 2026. A new drone law, **Decree-Law 89/2026**, requires registration, an operating licence and MOI approval before any flight, with penalties of up to three years' prison and KD 5,000. Its executive regulations, which will hold the rules that matter (altitude, crowds, night, payloads, insurance), are due by 31 December 2026 and had not appeared by mid-November. Saudi Arabia is the opposite case. Brand drone light shows with logos and scannable QR codes are an established event product there, run by operators licensed by the General Authority of Civil Aviation (GACA) for clients such as Ceer and stc, and they cost from about **SAR 275,000** a show. Banner-towing or screen-carrying drones over Saudi streets have no public precedent and would need a bespoke "Specific-category" authorisation that GACA is unlikely to grant over traffic. The commercial answer is the same in both countries: drones fit side street as a **resold, subcontracted premium event tier** (light shows paired with its LED trucks at the viewing site), not as a fleet that runs beside its trucks, taxis and bikes. One caveat applies throughout. The researchers could not open most primary pages (gazettes, gaca.gov.sa, Kuwaiti newspapers) because of network restrictions, so almost every finding rests on search-engine extracts. Article numbers, dates and amounts below are "as reported" and are listed for verification at the end.

## Kuwait has grounded every civilian drone since March 2026

### Three layers of prohibition sit on top of each other

The first layer is the **MOI's blanket ban of 3 March 2026** on "aerial photography and drone operation across all governorates", effective immediately and until further notice, "in light of the current situation in the country and the region" ([Arab Times](https://www.arabtimesonline.com/news/kuwait-imposes-immediate-nationwide-ban-on-drones-and-aerial-photography/); [Zawya](https://www.zawya.com/en/world/middle-east/kuwait-imposes-immediate-nationwide-ban-on-drones-and-aerial-photography-r873lxdq); [Anadolu](https://www.aa.com.tr/ar/%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AF%D9%88%D9%84-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B9%D8%B1%D8%A8%D9%8A%D8%A9/%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%83%D9%88%D9%8A%D8%AA-%D8%AA%D8%AD%D8%B8%D8%B1-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AA%D8%B5%D9%88%D9%8A%D8%B1-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AC%D9%88%D9%8A-%D9%88%D8%AA%D8%B4%D8%BA%D9%8A%D9%84-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AF%D8%B1%D9%88%D9%86-%D8%AD%D8%AA%D9%89-%D8%A5%D8%B4%D8%B9%D8%A7%D8%B1-%D8%A2%D8%AE%D8%B1-/3848623)). It followed the **2026 Iranian strikes on Kuwait, including drone strikes on the airport** ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Kuwait_International_Airport_drone_strikes)), and a drone strike on a residential complex ([Gulf Business](https://gulfbusiness.com/en/2026/news/drone-strike-hits-kuwait-residential-complex-as-authorities-contain-fire/)). Gatherings, weddings and concerts were banned for a time in the same month ([Lexis Middle East](https://www.lexis.ae/2026/03/17/kwt-gatherings-weddings-banned-over-security-concerns/)). On **15 September 2026 the MOI reiterated that the ban stays in force nationwide**, with legal action against violators ([Kuwait Times](https://kuwaittimes.com/article/49809/kuwait/moi-reiterates-nationwide-ban-on-drones-and-aerial-photography/); [Arab Times](https://www.arabtimesonline.com/news/kuwait-reiterates-ban-on-drones-aerial-photography-until-further-notice/)). No October or November 2026 news of a lifting or of exemptions for licensed companies was found (confidence that the ban stands: **high** for September, **medium** for today).

The second layer is commercial. **MOCI Ministerial Decision 31/2026** (issued 2 April 2026) bans the sale and trading of drones in all shops and markets until further notice, with closure of violating premises ([lawskw](https://lawskw.com/section/%D9%82%D8%B1%D8%A7%D8%B1-%D8%B1%D9%82%D9%85-31-%D9%84%D8%B3%D9%86%D8%A9-2026-%D8%A8%D8%B4%D8%A3%D9%86-%D8%AD%D8%B8%D8%B1-%D8%A8%D9%8A%D8%B9-%D9%88%D8%AA%D8%AF%D8%A7%D9%88%D9%84-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B7%D8%A7%D8%A6%D8%B1%D8%A7%D8%AA-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%85%D8%B3%D9%8A%D8%B1%D8%A9-%D8%A8%D8%AF%D9%88%D9%86-%D8%B7%D9%8A%D8%A7%D8%B1-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AF%D8%B1%D9%88%D9%86); [Emarat Al Youm](https://www.emaratalyoum.com/politics/news/2026-04-02-1.2031361)). The third layer is older. Around **April 2022 the civil aviation authority (DGCA) stopped issuing drone permits to individuals and private entities** and limited them to government agencies ([Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/world/gulf/kuwait/kuwait-restricts-drone-permits-1.1649760804340); [Emarat Al Youm](https://www.emaratalyoum.com/politics/news/2022-04-11-1.1620323)). Whether that freeze was ever formally lifted is unknown, so private commercial drone work in Kuwait has been effectively closed since 2022, not just since March 2026.

### Decree-Law 89/2026 builds a licensing route but leaves the details blank

**Decree-Law 89 of 2026**, on drones, light sport aircraft and air sports, was published in Kuwait Al-Youm issue 1808 on 12 or 13 September 2026 (sources disagree) and took effect on publication ([Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/world/gulf/kuwait/kuwait-brings-drones-under-new-law-with-jail-terms-and-fines-up-to-kd5000-1.500672815); [The Peninsula](https://thepeninsulaqatar.com/article/13/09/2026/kuwait-toughens-law-on-drones-adds-prison-terms-and-fines-for-violations)). As reported, it gives the civil aviation authority (now styled PACA) supervision in coordination with the MOI and the security and military bodies. It requires the owner to **register the drone and obtain an airworthiness certificate**, an **operating licence** from the authority, and **MOI approval before any use** ([Al-Seyassah](https://alseyassah.com/article/471682/); [Al-Anba](https://www.alanba.com.kw/1376056)). Importing, assembling, programming or modifying drones or parts needs a permit plus MOI approval, and commercial drone activity needs its own commercial licence ([halalkhalij](https://halalkhalij.com/kuwait/251477/)). Flying near airports and in restricted or sensitive areas is prohibited ([avi-go](https://avi-go.com/news/articles/0afaf4b636ac719b710c42b83a20bb65)). An Al-Seyassah subheading reports a **ban from sunset to sunrise** ([Al-Seyassah](https://alseyassah.com/article/471682/)), which, if it is in the decree and has no event exemption, would rule out drone light shows altogether. That provision is unconfirmed.

Penalties are tiered. As reported, **Art. 35 sets up to 3 years and/or KD 3,000–5,000**, Art. 34 up to 2 years and/or KD 1,000–2,000 (including flying without MOI approval), and Art. 33 up to 6 months and/or KD 300–700 ([Arab Times](https://www.arabtimesonline.com/news/kd5000-fine-or-3-years-in-jail-kuwaits-tough-message-to-drone-users/); [halalkhalij](https://halalkhalij.com/kuwait/251477/)). Existing owners have one year to regularise, and the **executive regulations setting procedures, conditions and fees are due by 31 December 2026** ([Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/world/gulf/kuwait/kuwait-brings-drones-under-new-law-with-jail-terms-and-fines-up-to-kd5000-1.500672815)). Everything an advertising operator needs to know (category for a 25–55 kg banner drone or a 500-drone swarm, altitude, distance from crowds and roads, external loads, insurance minimums) will sit in those regulations. The older DGCA circular (2019-34, EU-style categories A1–A3) reportedly required insurance of KD 10,000–30,000 by category, but another guide says insurance was not required, and neither figure is verified ([drone-laws.com](https://drone-laws.com/drone-laws-in-kuwait); [drone-traveller](https://drone-traveller.com/drone-laws-kuwait/)).

### Every Kuwaiti drone show so far ran under a state umbrella

Large drone shows have flown in Kuwait, but all the documented ones were **national celebrations or the state-backed Ya Hala shopping festival**. In February 2025 shows over the Gulf Road beach formed the Kuwaiti flag and "الكويت حلوة" ([KUNA](https://www.kuna.net.kw/ArticleDetails.aspx?id=3217337&language=ar)), and Ya Hala added drones and fireworks at Al-Shaheed Park for the national holidays ([KUNA](https://www.kuna.net.kw/ArticleDetails.aspx?id=3220769)). India's BotLab Dynamics ran more than eight Ya Hala shows at Al-Shaheed Park, Al Khiran Mall and Marina Crescent ([Times Kuwait](https://timeskuwait.com/?p=37308)). Reports put the total at more than 8,000 drones, while BotLab's own figure is 1,500, so the numbers conflict ([kuwaitlocal](https://kuwaitlocal.com/news/ya-hala-drone-shows-in-kuwait)). Cyberdrone says it flew **600 drones over Kuwait City for the Ministry of Interior itself at Eid al-Fitr 2025** ([Cyberdrone](https://www.cyberdrone.com/drone-show-kuwait)). Green Island hosted national-holiday drone shows in 2023 ([Al-Jarida](https://www.aljarida.com/article/15765)). The 2026 National Day had a military air show and no reported civilian drone show ([KUNA](https://www.kuna.net.kw/ArticleDetails.aspx?id=3277319)).

Ya Hala 2025 had a commercial main sponsor, Deliveroo, but **no source says sponsor branding appeared in the drone formations** ([Zawya](https://www.zawya.com/en/press-release/companies-news/deliveroo-joins-ya-hala-shopping-festival-2025-as-main-sponsor-nzza1jlr)). Vendors such as MIRS and AO market brand-logo shows in Kuwait, but those are sales claims, not evidence of events ([MIRS](https://mirsdroneshow.com/drone-show/kuwait/)). The researchers found **no documented commercial brand-logo drone show and no drone banner or drone screen ad in Kuwait**, and no source naming who approved any show. The pattern, with shows continuing through the 2022–2025 private-permit freeze, fits a model in which a government body or festival organiser holds the permit and the drone company works as its contractor. That is an inference, but it is the most useful one for side street: even after the ban lifts, the first realistic door is sponsorship inside a state-sanctioned event.

### The Municipality's "flying balloon" clause is the nearest ad-licence hook

Kuwait Municipality is the sole licensing authority for public advertising under **Advertising Regulation 599/2023 as amended by 223/2024**. That regulation requires approval from the **General Fire Force and the DGCA for flying-balloon advertisements (البالونات الطائرة)** ([mesferlaw](https://mesferlaw.com/%D8%A8%D9%84%D8%AF%D9%8A%D8%A9-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%83%D9%88%D9%8A%D8%AA-%D9%82%D8%B1%D8%A7%D8%B1-%D8%B1%D9%82%D9%85-599-%D9%84%D8%B3%D9%86%D8%A9-2023-%D8%A8%D8%B4%D8%A7%D9%86-%D9%84%D8%A7%D8%A6%D8%AD/)). No clause on drones, blimps or banner-towing aircraft was found. Because the amended regulation fines a **commercial ad "by any means" without a licence KD 3,000–5,000** ([Al-Anba](https://www.alanba.com.kw/1329133)), a drone banner, screen or logo-forming show would almost certainly also need a Municipality ad licence, most plausibly by analogy to the balloon category: Municipality licence, Fire Force approval, and on the aviation side the Decree-Law 89 licence plus MOI approval. Site owners such as malls or the managers of Green Island and Al-Shaheed Park would add a consent layer (inference, untested).

The Ministry of Information has no aerial-ad role today. From about **April 2027, Decree-Law 102/2026 (Art. 34)** requires a Ministry licence for "anyone who carries out commercial advertising or promotion aimed at a public in Kuwait", with fines of KD 1,000–50,000 for advertising without one ([kwt.one](https://kwt.one/media-law-advertisers/); [Mondaq/ASAR](https://www.mondaq.com/social-media/1853030/kuwaits-new-media-regulation-law-what-businesses-need-to-know)). The licence attaches to the advertiser, not the medium, so side street would need it for its ground business anyway, and it would cover drone inventory sold in the same way. Content limits from the ground business carry over: tobacco advertising is banned through any medium, and health and promotional ads need MOH and MOCI pre-approval.

## Saudi Arabia licenses brand drone shows, not street banners

### GACAR Part 107 puts advertising flights in the "Specific" category

Saudi civil drones fall under **GACAR Part 107** (non-recreational small drones) and **Part 48** (registration through GACA's online UAS portal) ([GACA Part 107](https://new.gaca.gov.sa/-/media/Files/PDF/LawsAndRegulation/Aviation-Safety-and-Environmental-Sustainability/GACAR-Safety-Regulations/Part-107---Non-recreational-civil-small-unmanned-aircraft-systems.pdf); [GACA Part 48](https://www.gaca.gov.sa/-/media/Files/PDF/LawsAndRegulation/Aviation-Safety-and-Environmental-Sustainability/GACAR-Safety-Regulations/part-48-gacar.pdf)). A vendor report says **Version 5, around January 2026**, replaced case-by-case approvals with a risk-based framework of "Open" and "Specific" categories, added Standard Scenarios (STS-01, STS-02) and set Remote ID requirements ([Terra Drone Arabia](https://terra-drone.com.sa/major-update-on-gaca-regulation-part-107-operation-of-uas-v5/)). GACA's own advisory circular lists **night operations as Specific-category**, and remote pilots need endorsement training for advanced operations ([GACA AC 107-01](https://gaca.gov.sa/-/media/Files/PDF/LawsAndRegulation/Aviation-Safety-and-Environmental-Sustainability/Safety-and-Aviation-Standards-Advisory-Circulars/GACA-AC-107-01-Advanced-Operations-with-Unmanned-Aircraft-Systems.pdf)). Guides add that flights over populated sites need Specific authorisation, and one cites a **400 ft (120 m) ceiling**, unconfirmed in official text ([Fly GACA](https://www.flygaca.com/guides/drone-uas-rules-in-ksa/); [Shaaheen](https://shaaheen.sa/en/blog/a-comprehensive-guide-to-operating-drones-in-saudi-arabia-gacar-part-107/a-2108200842)). An aggregator says the rules bar reckless operation "including dropping objects from the aircraft" ([drone-laws.com](https://drone-laws.com/drone-laws-in-saudi-arabia/)).

Put together, a banner or screen drone over people at night carrying an external load is a Specific-category operation needing a bespoke risk assessment, pilot endorsements, Remote ID and a site approval for each event. No rule specific to towing, banners, swarms or light shows was found, and advertising over crowds is unlikely to fit a Standard Scenario (inference). The commercial process, as reported in 2020, has two steps: register the drone and obtain an operator permit, then get GACA approval for the specific site ([Okaz](https://www.okaz.com.sa/news/local/2037408)). 2020 fees were SAR 500 for registration, SAR 500 for the exam and SAR 300 for the card, likely restructured since ([Al-Muraba](https://www.almuraba.net/?p=254191)). **Imports need GACA registration or an import certificate before Saudi Customs releases a drone** ([Saudi Gazette](https://saudigazette.com.sa/article/596734/SAUDI-ARABIA/GACA-issues-thousands-of-drone-permits)). Unlicensed operation carries up to **one year's prison and/or SAR 100,000** under the Civil Aviation Law (same source), and GACA publishes quarterly enforcement figures, such as four unpermitted-drone cases fined SAR 25,000 in Q3 2024 ([Okaz](https://www.okaz.com.sa/local/na/2233440)). The Ministry of Interior surfaced only as a security actor, imposing permits after a 2018 drone incident near a royal palace ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2018/4/22/saudi-drone-enthusiasts-to-require-permit-after-palace-incident)). No current Absher or MOI licensing step was found, which needs checking.

### Brand shows with QR codes are routine at licensed venues

Saudi Arabia has the region's clearest record of **commercial brand drone shows**. Ceer, the national EV brand, launched in November 2022 with **1,346 drones at Boulevard Riyadh City** forming brand imagery and a Guinness-record QR code linking to its website ([Arabad Online](https://arabadonline.com/en/article/3693-Ceer-Saudi-Arabias-first-EV-brand-launches-in-spectacular-style-); [Euronews](https://www.euronews.com/2022/11/24/watch-world-record-breaking-drone-display-in-riyadh)). **stc Group set two Guinness records over Riyadh on Founding Day, 22 February 2026**, for the largest drone QR code and the largest animated aerial image ([Leaders MENA](https://www.leaders-mena.com/founding-day-stc-group-breaks-two-guinness-records-with-historic-drone-display/)). A "House of the Dragon" show promoted the series on Riyadh Boulevard ([Arab News PK](https://www.arabnews.pk/node/2144281/corporate-news)). Riyadh Season shows carry the season's logo ([Arab News](https://arabnews.com/node/2046311/amp)), and Noor Riyadh 2022 flew 1,990 drones in one display ([Guinness World Records](https://www.guinnessworldrecords.com/news/commercial/2022/11/massive-light-art-festival-in-saudi-arabia-breaks-six-world-records-726790)).

The operators are specialist show companies. Riyadh-based **Cyberdrone** claims a 7,000-drone fleet and lists the SLS Expo 2025 (600 drones with the expo logo), the AFC Champions League Elite final in Jeddah and national-day shows; it says about **300 drones** can render logos and text ([Cyberdrone KSA](https://www.cyberdrone.com/drone-show-saudi-arabia)). An Al-Eqtisadiah interview with the founder of a **GACA-licensed Saudi show company** reported **157 shows since October 2021**, for clients including Sela, the General Entertainment Authority (GEA) and the National Events Center, with prices **from about SAR 275,000** ([Al-Eqtisadiah](https://www.aleqt.com/2025/09/08/article_2766305.html)). The only explicit description of approvals comes from a vendor: **GACA airspace and event approvals, registration of drones over 250 g, and import permits** ([Cyberdrone KSA](https://www.cyberdrone.com/drone-show-saudi-arabia)). In practice the venue or event already holds its entertainment permits, the show company holds the GACA licence, and the brand buys the show.

**No operator of banner-towing or screen-carrying drones over Saudi streets was found.** Given the Specific-category burden and the falling-object concerns, the format is either not authorised or not commercially established.

### Municipal and media layers apply as for any ad

No final MOMAH rule on drone advertising was found. A **draft MOMAH advertising-signs regulation reportedly lists "ads on flying objects and balloons" (الإعلانات على المجسمات الطائرة والبالونات)** among the ad types it covers within urban boundaries, alongside screens and temporary event ads ([Al-Watan](https://www.alwatan.com.sa/article/1154617)). If adopted, an aerial ad would need a Balady temporary advertising permit on top of GACA approval. Its adoption status and fee are unconfirmed. GAMR's advertising-agency licence, issued through the Iʿlam portal to a company with a commercial register (CR) listing the activity ([GAMR e-services](https://eservices.gamr.gov.sa/user/services/13)), would cover selling drone inventory as it covers any other medium. A Kuwaiti company would need a Saudi entity (MISA licence if any shareholder is non-GCC, then a CR) and Saudi-resident certified pilots, since foreign residents reportedly struggled to register drones without an iqama ([Saudi Gazette](https://saudigazette.com.sa/article/596734/SAUDI-ARABIA/GACA-issues-thousands-of-drone-permits)). Sector pre-approvals (SFDA for food, SAMA for finance, REGA for real estate) and the tobacco ban apply to drone creative as to any ad.

## Physics and climate narrow five formats to one

Five drone ad formats exist, and only one is mature in the Gulf. **Banner-towing** needs heavy hybrid aircraft: Sustainable Skylines in Miami, the only operator with FAA approval, flies drones of up to **55 kg including the banner**, needed a Section 44807 exemption plus waivers from 2023 to 2024, and uses ground spotters ([DroneXL](https://dronexl.co/2025/09/14/sustainable-skylines-faa-banner-drones); [Hogan Lovells](https://hlc.com/en/news/hogan-lovells-advises-sustainable-skylines-in-first-ever-faa-authorization-for-drone-advertising-banners)). Small camera drones can carry only about 100–180 g, so any banner they lift is too small to read, and a banner "will catch the wind a bit like a sail" ([MavicPilots](https://mavicpilots.com/threads/banner-advertising-with-the-mavic-pro.14256)). **Drone-carried LED screens** fly for only about **10–20 minutes** per battery ([sixteen-nine](https://www.sixteen-nine.net/2024/10/17/this-led-ad-display-flies-around)). Flying-display rigs weigh 23–215 kg at take-off ([iDisplayLED](https://www.idisplayled.com/newsdetails/Magicsky)). **Tethered** ad drones exist and are pitched as safer near the public because the tether limits fly-aways ([Technical.ly](https://technical.ly/startups/ryan-media-lab-atom-4000.md)). **Drone projection** has no ad precedent. **Drone light shows**, with hundreds of drones forming logos and QR codes at night, are the format GCC buyers already purchase.

Kuwait's climate cuts the daytime formats further. A heavy enterprise reference drone (DJI Matrice 400) is rated to 50°C for the aircraft but **only 40°C for its batteries**, and to winds of 12 m/s with no payload ([DJI](https://enterprise.dji.com/matrice-400/specs)). Heat makes batteries "drop voltage without notice", and ground winds of 5–10 mph can be much stronger at 200 ft ([Pilot Institute](https://pilotinstitute.com/drone-hot-weather/); [Scanifly](https://scanifly.com/blog/flying-a-drone-in-the-wind-cold-and-other-challenging-environments/)). With Kuwaiti summer afternoons of 45–50°C, shamal winds and dust storms, banner and screen drones would be realistically limited to cool mornings and evenings for much of May to September, and grounded on windy days. No GCC no-fly-day data was found (inference). A reported Kuwaiti night-flying ban would remove the evening window too. Against side street's existing assets, a small drone banner is less legible than a truck's LED screen, and a legible one needs a 25–55 kg aircraft run to aviation standards.

## Costs are event-scale and safety drives the rules

### A small branded show costs about KD 6,000–15,000

Show companies price per drone. Typical quotes run **$200–300 per drone with a minimum of about 100**, or $20,000–30,000 at the base ([The Drone Girl](https://www.thedronegirl.com/2025/07/17/drone-light-show-cost/)). Cyberdrone's dataset of 1,000 shows puts the **MENA average at 401 drones and $112,300 a show** (about $316 per drone), the highest of any region ([Cyberdrone pricing](https://cyberdrone.com/pricing)). UAE private bookings run **AED 50,000–500,000** ([Drone Show UAE](https://droneshowuae.com/?p=9105)), and the Saudi floor is the SAR 275,000 quoted above. Converted at about KWD 0.307 per dollar, a 100-drone logo show is roughly **KD 6,000–15,000**, the MENA average is about **KD 34,000**, and the Saudi starting price is about KD 22,500 (own arithmetic; no Kuwait-specific quote was found). Drone banner pricing is unpublished: Sustainable Skylines has not posted rates, and manned banner planes, the comparison, cost anywhere from $400–600 an hour to $7,000–9,000 a day depending on the source ([Flying Magazine](https://www.flyingmag.com/startup-aims-to-replace-banner-towing-aircraft-with-drones); [MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/388810/)). Owning a show fleet makes little sense for side street: shows need hundreds of drones at about **$1,500 each**, plus choreography software and crews ([Cyberdrone pricing](https://cyberdrone.com/pricing)), demand is seasonal, and in Kuwait importing them requires a permit and MOI approval.

### Orlando shows why regulators keep drones away from crowds and roads

The defining incident is **Orlando on 21 December 2024**, when drones in a Sky Elements holiday show collided and fell into the crowd; a **seven-year-old boy was struck in the chest and needed surgery** ([CBS News](https://cbsnews.com/news/florida-holiday-show-drone-collision-orlando); [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/news/us-news/falling-drone-seriously-injures-boy-florida-holiday-show-mother-says-rcna185183)). Drones also fell on tourists at a Tsingtao beer festival in China ([DroneDJ](https://dronedj.com/?p=63062)). Regulators' logic follows from weight: a design team chose an 8 lb limit for its ad octocopter precisely to keep failure harm acceptable ([The Robot Report](https://www.therobotreport.com/hoovy-advatar-octocopter-drone-advertising-coming-to-a-sky-near-you/)), while a full-size banner drone weighs up to 55 kg. The FAA allows sustained flight over moving vehicles only within closed or restricted-access sites ([FAA](https://www.faa.gov/uas/commercial_operators/operations_over_people)). EASA bans Open-category flights over "assemblies of people", a term that covers "commercial streets during the opening hours of the shops" and busy beaches ([EASA](https://www.easa.europa.eu/en/the-agency/faqs/provisions-applicable-both-open-and-specific-category)). Side street's core idea, ads moving with traffic, is the hardest category to permit anywhere.

Insurance follows the same split. In the US, liability cover for a single small drone costs about $275–750 a year for $1M, but events commonly require **$2M–5M** ([SkyWatch](https://www.skywatch.ai/blog/drone-insurance-flying-at-events); [BWI](https://bwifly.com/blog/drone-insurance-for-events-protect-your-aerial-operations-at-public-private-gatherings/)). In Saudi Arabia, insurance became available for licensed drones in 2020 only for uses such as survey and aerial photography, and advertising was not listed ([Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/business/banking/licensed-drones-now-have-insurance-coverage-in-saudi-arabia-1.1598336379999)). No GCC premium data was found. A heavy banner drone over a road would need specialist aviation underwriting (inference), and in a subcontracted show the operator's policy, with side street and the client as named insureds, is the practical route.

## Recommended route: resell shows, watch Kuwait, test in Riyadh

**Kuwait, now to the end of 2026: do not buy, import or fly anything.** Possession and import controls under Decree-Law 89, the MOCI sales ban and penalties of up to three years' prison make any early hardware purchase a liability. The useful work is paper. Side street should write to PACA/DGCA and the MOI to ask whether the March 2026 ban has an exemption route for licensed events, and to be notified when the executive regulations issue. It should ask the Municipality's advertisement licensing section which category and fee would apply to an aerial ad (balloon analogy or a new line). It should also sign a non-exclusive reseller or referral agreement with one or two show companies with Kuwaiti track records (Cyberdrone, which has flown for the MOI, and BotLab Dynamics, which flew Ya Hala), so that it is ready when flights resume.

**Kuwait, once the ban lifts and the regulations issue (probably 2027): sell sponsorship, not flights.** The first realistic product is a **brand segment inside a state-sanctioned show**, such as Ya Hala in February, National Day, Eid or a major mall event, with the organiser holding the aviation permits and the show company flying. Side street's edge is the package: its LED trucks parked at the viewing site repeat the QR code and message, its taxi screens and bike boxes run the countdown in the week before, and it handles the Municipality ad licence and, from April 2027, the Media Law layer. Banner and screen drones over roads should stay an R&D watch item until the regulations expressly allow external loads over people, which nothing currently suggests.

**Saudi Arabia: a pilot is possible now, through partners.** If side street sets up its Saudi entity for the ground business (MISA if needed, CR, GAMR agency licence), it can resell GACA-licensed shows from Cyberdrone or the Al-Eqtisadiah company to brands at Riyadh Boulevard, Riyadh Season or expo venues, where permits already exist. That builds a track record that Kuwaiti regulators and clients can see. Price expectations need resetting: at SAR 275,000 or more a show, this is a launch-event product for telecoms, autos and developers, not a recurring media line. Side street should not attempt its own banner or screen drones in Saudi Arabia.

## Conclusion

The question side street asked, whether drones can join its moving-ad fleet, turns out to be two different questions. Drones that move with traffic carrying banners or screens fail on every axis at once: Kuwaiti law, Saudi risk categories, Gulf heat and wind, payload physics and insurance. No regulator anywhere has made that use routine, so it is not a product to plan around. Drone light shows are different. They are a mature, state-tolerated spectacle that the Gulf already buys, and they reward exactly what side street has: street-level screens that can turn a five-minute sky moment into a week of reach and a scannable call to action.

The Kuwait ban is also a timing signal. Drone policy there is now set by the security services, not the aviation authority. Because the ban followed actual drone attacks, private aerial advertising will be among the last uses to reopen, and it will probably return first under the same state-sponsored umbrella that carried the 2023–2025 shows. A company that is already the trusted ground partner of Ya Hala and the national-day organisers when that happens will be better placed than one that bought drones early. Saudi Arabia is where side street can learn the product meanwhile.
