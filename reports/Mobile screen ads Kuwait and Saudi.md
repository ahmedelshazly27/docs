# Taxis open Kuwait; concessions gate Saudi screens

A moving LED ad network can launch lawfully in **Kuwait now, but only in the formats the rules already name**. The clearest one is the taxi: a third-party "investment advertisement" licence (TR133) with Traffic approval, a **static roof panel of at most 40×120 cm** and an **interior screen of at most 25×40 cm**. Light vans carrying screens can follow if they run static content while moving and play video only while parked on private land. Delivery-bike boxes remain the hardest product. Kuwait Municipality's motorcycle clause allows only the fleet's **own "identifying" ad, as a fixed panel with non-moving content, at KD 40 a year**. Expatriate riders cannot be paid directly without breaching Article 19 of the Residence Law. Saudi Arabia is harder for a newcomer. **Cabinet Decision 590 (2020) lets buses and taxis carry other parties' ads but lets commercial vehicles carry only their owner's own ads.** The national sign requirements allow only side stickers on private and company vehicles. Riyadh sells taxi and bus ad inventory as a municipal concession, held since 2024 by Al Arabia's subsidiary Fadn. Neither country's media regulator clearly licenses outdoor screens today. Kuwait's **Decree-Law 102/2026 (in force about April 2027)** requires a licence from the Ministry of Information for anyone who advertises commercially to a Kuwaiti audience, and Saudi Arabia's GAMR licenses advertising agencies. Both therefore add a company-level licence on top of the municipal and traffic permits for each vehicle. One caveat applies throughout. The researchers could not open most primary government and gazette pages because of network restrictions, so nearly every finding rests on search-engine extracts of the cited pages. The figures and article numbers below are "as reported" and must be checked by counsel before money is committed.

## Kuwait: the Municipality licenses each screen, Traffic licenses each vehicle

### The Municipality's vehicle categories decide what can run

**Kuwait Municipality is the sole licensing authority for public advertising.** A Cabinet directive in about April 2026 told it to regularise the legal status of public-space advertising and to tighten oversight of campaign content ([Times Kuwait](https://timeskuwait.com/kuwait-moves-to-overhaul-public-advertising-framework/)). The governing text is the **Advertising Regulation, Ministerial Resolution 599/2023**, issued 28 December 2023 ([KUNA](https://www.kuna.net.kw/ArticleDetails.aspx?id=3130349&language=ar)). It was amended by **Resolution 223/2024** and by further amendments the Municipal Council approved in late December 2025 ([Times Kuwait](https://timeskuwait.com/municipality-finalizes-amendments-to-advertising-regulations-with-stricter-fines-and-new-licensing-rules/)). **No gazette number has been found for the 2025 amendments.** The base text known to be in force is still 599/2023 as amended by 223/2024. The 2026 enforcement reporting, however, is consistent with the amendments being applied (confidence: medium).

The regulation sets a rule for each vehicle type, and each rule fits one of the three products:

- **Taxis.** Exterior ads must sit on unused sides without protruding, and a taxi company owner must hold a valid ad licence. Interior screens need the contract between investor and vehicle owner, a valid ownership book, Traffic approval, a size of at most 25×40 cm, and a position not in front of the driver ([lawskw](https://lawskw.com/section/%D8%A8%D9%84%D8%AF%D9%8A%D8%A9-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%83%D9%88%D9%8A%D8%AA-%D9%82%D8%B1%D8%A7%D8%B1-%D8%B1%D9%82%D9%85-599-%D9%84%D8%B3%D9%86%D8%A9-2023-%D8%A8%D8%B4%D8%A7%D9%86-%D9%84%D8%A7%D8%A6%D8%AD%D8%A9-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A7%D8%B9%D9%84%D8%A7%D9%86%D8%A7%D8%AA%D9%88%D8%B2%D8%A7%D8%B1%D8%A9-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AF%D9%88%D9%84%D8%A9-%D9%84%D8%B4%D8%A6%D9%88%D9%86-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A8%D9%84%D8%AF%D9%8A%D8%A9-%D9%82%D8%B1%D8%A7%D8%B1-%D8%B1%D9%82%D9%85-223-%D9%84%D8%B3%D9%86%D8%A9-2024-%D8%A8%D8%B4%D8%A7%D9%86-%D8%AA%D8%B9%D8%AF%D9%8A%D9%84-%D9%84%D8%A7%D8%A6%D8%AD%D8%A9-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A7%D8%B9%D9%84%D8%A7%D9%86%D8%A7%D8%AA)).
- **Car roofs** (taxis and private or commercial cars). Only a static unit of at most 40×120 cm, with GTD approval. The 2006 regulation already capped roof ads at 40 cm high, so the height limit is long-standing ([baladia.gov.kw 172/2006](https://www.baladia.gov.kw/sites/ar/municipalityServices/Pages/municipalityRegulations/advs/172-2006.aspx)).
- **Delivery motorcycles.** **KD 40/yr "for the identifying advertisement of the activity only"**, which "may be a fixed electronic panel with non-moving content" with Traffic approval and self-contained fixed lighting. The establishment that owns the bike must hold a valid commercial licence ([Al-Anba](https://www.alanba.com.kw/1329133); [Al-Rai](https://www.alraimedia.com/article/1751030/)).
- **Trucks.** No truck or "mobile screen" category exists. The general vehicle rules (sides, no protrusion, self-contained lighting, Traffic approval) would apply by analogy.

Read together, these rules mean a **rotating third-party video network fits only inside the taxi cabin**. Roofs and motorcycle boxes are static by rule. Trucks are unaddressed. Given the pattern, regulators are most likely to accept static content while a truck is moving (confidence: medium on the text, low on how GTD would treat a roof unit that cycles still images).

### Fees are small; the guarantee and the fines are what count

The only published product for third-party ads on vehicles is the **taxi and public-transport "investment advertisement" licence (TR133)**. It charges **KD 30/m² for a moving TV-screen ad and KD 20/m² per face for variable ads, with one KD 1,000 guarantee covering all the holder's licences**. Conditions are Traffic approval, Municipality inspection and a copy of the registration book ([baladia.gov.kw TR133](https://www.baladia.gov.kw/sites/ar/Pages/transactionsRequirements/TR133.aspx); [e.gov.kw](https://e.gov.kw/sites/kgoarabic/Pages/Services/KM/ApplyingforIssuingAdvertisementLicensesonPublicTransportVehicles.aspx)). TR133 was read in an earlier session and could not be re-checked this time.

Other fee lines conflict:

- **Electronic screens in general:** KD 75/m² per face per year according to [Arab Times](https://www.arabtimesonline.com/news/new-advertising-rules-introduced-in-kuwait-fines-fees-and-standards-updated/); KD 100/m² according to [Times Kuwait](https://timeskuwait.com/kuwait-tightens-street-advertising-rules-to-boost-road-safety-and-urban-aesthetics/).
- **Commercial ads placed through advertising companies:** KD 100/yr.
- **Delivery motorcycles under 223/2024:** one extract gives KD 50–100/yr by area instead of KD 40.
- **"Mobile vehicles licensed by MOCI":** a 2024 proposal of KD 5/month ([Al-Jarida](https://www.aljarida.com/article/56921)), never confirmed as adopted.

At these rates the licence fee per vehicle is small. As an illustration, assuming TR133 rates are annual (the extract does not say): a two-face 40×120 cm roof unit is about 0.96 m², or roughly KD 19/yr at KD 20/m² per face. A 25×40 cm interior screen is 0.1 m², or about KD 3/yr. The fixed cost of entry is the **KD 1,000 guarantee**, plus an engineering drawing from a Municipality-approved office for each design ([Times Kuwait](https://timeskuwait.com/municipality-updates-outdoor-advertisement-regulations/)).

Licences are issued and renewed electronically through baladia.gov.kw/egovadv and, by some accounts, the Sahel app. Guides conflict on whether a *new* licence can be filed online. The Municipality may revoke any licence in the public interest without compensation ([Arab Times](https://www.arabtimesonline.com/news/new-advertising-rules-introduced-in-kuwait-fines-fees-and-standards-updated/)).

The penalties are larger. The amended regulation sets **KD 3,000–5,000 for a commercial ad by any means without a licence**, KD 500–3,000 for ads promoting prohibited goods, and KD 500 for event ads. Unlicensed ads are removed at the violator's cost, and a licence is withdrawn after two warnings ([Al-Anba](https://www.alanba.com.kw/1329133); [Times Kuwait](https://timeskuwait.com/municipal-council-approves-sweeping-amendments-to-advertising-regulations/)). June 2026 campaigns ran in every governorate, removing reported ads within 48 hours ([Al-Rai](https://www.alraimedia.com/article/1768991/)). **Whether the fine applies per vehicle, per screen or per campaign is unknown.** That determines whether one mistake costs KD 5,000 or KD 500,000 across a fleet.

### Traffic sees an unpermitted screen as an illegal addition

The **Traffic Law (Decree-Law 67/1976, amended by Decree-Law 5/2025, in force 22 April 2025)** creates the second permit layer ([KUNA](https://www.kuna.net.kw/ArticleDetails.aspx?id=3215300)). It contains two relevant offences:

- **Using a vehicle for advertising without a permit:** KD 20 settlement, or KD 45–75 in court ([MOI GDT](https://www.moi.gov.kw/gdt/trafficviolations)).
- **Writings, stickers, images or additions on a vehicle without a permit:** KD 50 settlement ([kuwaiteservices](https://kuwaiteservices.com/4100/%D8%A7%D8%B3%D8%B9%D8%A7%D8%B1-%D9%85%D8%AE%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%81%D8%A7%D8%AA-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%85%D8%B1%D9%88%D8%B1/)).

The 2025 law also targets illegal lights and banned devices ([Arab Times](https://www.arabtimesonline.com/news/kuwait-revamps-traffic-law-new-penalties-for-violations-and-changes-to-legal-procedures)). In December 2023, Traffic treated driver-controlled electronic boards on rear windows as a **serious violation leading to detention and impoundment** ([Al-Jarida](https://www.aljarida.com/article/47209)).

The fines are small. Impoundment is the real risk:

- **Delivery bikes:** up to **60 days**.
- **Vehicles obstructing traffic or parked in prohibited places:** up to **two months** (executive regulations Art. 207 and Administrative Circular 9/2025) ([Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/world/gulf/kuwait/kuwait-imposes-tougher-penalties-on-dangerous-driving-vehicles-to-be-impounded-up-to-two-months-1.500318661)).

No published GTD standard for brightness, size or motion was found. Approval is case by case, through the GTD and its Technical Inspection Department. The Municipality licence requires the GTD letter first (confidence: high that both permits are needed; low on what GTD will demand).

Each vehicle type also has its own operating limits:

- **Trucks:** the 2026–27 peak-hour ban removes the morning and early-afternoon rush from September to mid-June. **The weight or plate class that defines a "truck" for the ban was not found**, so a light box van needs written GTD confirmation that it falls outside it.
- **Delivery motorbikes:** barred from ring roads and expressways, and off the road **11:00–16:00 from 1 June to 31 August** ([Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/world/gulf/kuwait/kuwait-bans-delivery-bikes-during-peak-summer-heat-hours-1.500559094)). The GTD's October 2026 rules make **rear box lighting and reflective strips mandatory** ([Kuwait Times](https://kuwaittimes.com/article/51768/kuwait/traffic-department-lists-rules-for-delivery-motorcycle-riders/)). A box screen that covers them is a direct, actively enforced violation ([Kuwait Times](https://kuwaittimes.com/article/52132/kuwait/traffic-campaign-targets-delivery-motorcycle-violations/)).
- **App-based ride-hailing cars:** governed by MOI Resolution 893/2026, published 5 July 2026. It requires a MOCI licence plus GTD approval, an annual GTD vehicle permit, an in-cabin camera with 120-day retention, and Kuwaiti drivers ([HFW](https://www.hfw.com/insights/kuwait-regulatory-update-new-ride-hailing-framework-and-enhanced-cctv-compliance-requirements/); [Al-Anba](https://www.alanba.com.kw/1366313)). No advertising clause was found. Because these cars are often privately owned, each one would need its own licence.

### The Ministry of Information matters from April 2027, not today

Until about April 2027, the Ministry of Information's advertising powers attach to the media it licenses: print, broadcast, e-media and production houses. **No source shows it approving or licensing outdoor or vehicle screens today.** One vendor blog claims otherwise, but it is low reliability ([media.co.uk](https://www.media.co.uk/blogs/blog/kuwait-static-megas-permits-billboard-regulations)). Two existing instruments still touch the business:

- **Ministerial Resolution 13/2026** requires prior approval (إجازة) from the General Administration for Media Regulation for audiovisual works made or shown by licensed production establishments. Its Art. 9 requires commercial ads not to breach law, public order or public morals ([mesferlaw](https://mesferlaw.com/وزارة-الاعلام-قرار-رقم-13-لسنة-2026-بشان-تنظي/); [Al-Anba](https://www.alanba.com.kw/1354121)). Video creative from a licensed production house therefore already passes through the Ministry.
- **An e-government licence, "advertisement and publications for organizations"**, is issued on a letter from MOCI ([e.gov.kw](https://e.gov.kw/sites/kgoenglish/Pages/Services/MEDIA/PublicityAdvertisingNewOrganaizations.aspx)). Whether a DOOH screen seller needs it is unconfirmed.

The change comes with **Decree-Law 102/2026 on Media Regulation**. It was published in Kuwait Al-Youm issue 1811 on 4 October 2026, takes effect about 4 April 2027, and repeals the three existing media laws ([Al-Jarida](https://www.aljarida.com/article/146388)). As reported, **Art. 34** subjects "anyone who carries out commercial advertising or promotion aimed at a public in Kuwait", paid or unpaid, to a **Ministry licence** ([kwt.one](https://kwt.one/media-law-advertisers/)). It is framed around the advertiser, not the medium, so on a literal reading it could cover a company selling screen time and possibly its brand clients. The reported penalties are:

- **Art. 55** (advertising without a licence): **KD 1,000–50,000**.
- **Art. 56** (an ad not identified as an ad, or misleading): the same range.
- **Art. 58** (unlicensed media activity): **KD 1,000–10,000** plus closure ([Mondaq/ASAR](https://www.mondaq.com/social-media/1853030/kuwaits-new-media-regulation-law-what-businesses-need-to-know)).

Sources disagree on the licence term (two years or five) and on whether the Ministry's silence after 60 days means refusal ([Wefaq Law](https://www.wefaqlaw.com/en/blog/kuwait-media-law-2026-what-it-means-for-influencers-companies-and-media-businesses); [Al-Hadath](https://www.alhadath.net/2026/09/02/الكويت-تقر-قانونا-جديدا-لتنظيم-المؤثرين-والاعلانات-الرقمية)). **No reported extract mentions billboards, screens or vehicles.** The answer depends on the Executive Regulations, due by about April 2027. Existing operators then get six months to regularise ([ASAR](https://www.asarlegal.com/kuwaits-new-media-regulation-law-what-businesses-need-to-know/)). A media licence does not replace the Municipality licence. The Ministry's compliance window for this business is therefore about **April–October 2027**, and the cheapest hedge is a written inquiry to the General Administration for Media Regulation while the regulations are being drafted.

### Content rules come from four directions

Content rules for screens draw on four sources:

- **Media law.** Law 3/2006 (Art. 21) bans content that offends public morals, religion or the constitution, or that criticises the Amir ([Kuwait Judicial Institute](https://www.kijls.moj.gov.kw/ar/sections/section4/sub3_section4/Releases/KIJSReleases/جرائم%20الإعلام%20والنشر.pdf)). The new law adds standards on accuracy, privacy, child protection and incitement.
- **Health ads.** MOH pre-licensing applies under Ministerial Decision 9/2025 ([Al Tamimi](https://www.tamimi.com/our-knowledge/publications/eyes-on-2026/articles/kuwait-tightens-public-health-and-digital-advertising-oversight-what-beauty-operators-brands-and-influencers-need-to-know-through-2026/)).
- **Commerce.** MOCI licenses promotional ads, which must show the licence number and run for at most one month ([e.gov.kw](https://e.gov.kw/sites/kgoenglish/Pages/Services/MOCI/LicenseApplicationPublishingAnnouncement.aspx)).
- **Tobacco.** Ministerial Resolution 237/2026 bans tobacco and nicotine advertising through any medium ([Arab Times](https://www.arabtimesonline.com/news/kuwait-tightens-tobacco-and-nicotine-rules-bans-online-sales-and-advertising/)).

From January 2026 the Municipality also enforces MOI brightness limits on illuminated signs ([Lexis ME](https://www.lexismiddleeast.com/news/2026-01-26_17/en)). Alcohol and pork are illegal products and cannot be advertised. No Ministry text on depicting women or on a minimum share of Arabic was found; Arabic-first creative is customary, not verified law.

### Delivery fleets, not riders, are the counterparty

Kuwaiti delivery is a licensed-company business. Since 2022, riders must hold residency with the company they ride for and carry its sticker and uniform ([Al-Rai](https://www.alraimedia.com/ampArticle/1604983)). **Article 19 of Residence Decree-Law 114/2024** makes working for anyone other than the sponsor a ground for deportation ([Arab Times](https://www.arabtimesonline.com/news/kuwaits-new-residency-regulations-specify-grounds-for-expat-deportation)). Paying an expatriate rider to carry a screen is therefore unlawful for both sides. Calling the payment "box rent" does not help, because the fleet owns the bike and the box.

The workable deal is a B2B media lease with the licensed delivery company. More than 250 such companies jointly protested MOCI 109/2026 ([Al-Jarida](https://www.aljarida.com/article/137453)). They are squeezed by the KD 1 fee cap and the commission caps, so per-box income is attractive to them. But the MOI now sanctions *companies* whose riders keep offending ([Kuwait Times](https://kuwaittimes.com/article/51033/kuwait/other-news/firms-told-to-rein-in-riders/)), so fleets will want GTD approval in hand and an indemnity before fitting anything.

Platforms are both gatekeeper and competitor:

- Talabat audits rider gear and "brand compliance" through its third-party logistics providers ([Delivery Hero careers](https://www.smartrecruiters.com/DeliveryHero/744000068183465-team-lead-rider-quality-support)).
- Talabat already runs LED box ads with Flyby in Dubai ([Brand Berries](https://thebrandberries.com/?p=24463)).
- Talabat is hiring retail-media staff in Kuwait ([Delivery Hero careers](https://careers.deliveryhero.com/job/manager-retail-media-ads-growth-in-kuwait-city-kuwait-jid-8175)).

Fleets that serve several apps with unbranded boxes are the realistic opening.

### Company licensing: a commercial licence first, media licences next

The operating company needs:

- **A MOCI commercial licence with an advertising activity.** The exact activity code was not found.
- **A Kuwaiti partner holding at least 51% of a WLL, or KDIPA approval for up to 100% foreign ownership** under Law 116/2013 ([PwC](https://www.pwc.com/m1/en/tax/documents/doing-business-guides/dbiku.pdf); [KDIPA](https://kdipa.gov.kw/)). Whether advertising is on KDIPA's negative list is unconfirmed.
- **Registration or classification with the Municipality as an advertising company**, which the KD 100 "through advertising companies" fee line assumes.
- Possibly the Ministry of Information's organisation licence, and from 2027 the Art. 34 advertiser licence.

Foreign corporate shareholders pay 15% corporate income tax on their share of profit, GCC-owned shares pay 1% Zakat, and Kuwait has no VAT ([Commenda](https://www.commenda.io/kuwait/corporate-tax-rates)).

## Saudi Arabia: owner-only rules push entry through concessions

### GAMR licenses the advertising business; municipalities license the sign

GCAM was reorganised by **Council of Ministers Decision 174 (27/02/1445H, about September 2023)** into the **General Authority for Media Regulation (GAMR, "Gmedia")**, chaired by the Minister of Media ([Zawya](https://www.zawya.com/en/legal/regulations/saudi-general-authority-of-media-regulation-is-tasked-to-supervise-entire-media-sector-y215a5za); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Authority_of_Media_Regulation)). Its Iʿlam portal issues a licence for **advertising offices, marketing offices and advertising agencies**. The applicant needs a commercial register (CR) listing the media activity, a national address and valid ID ([GAMR e-services](https://eservices.gamr.gov.sa/user/services/13)). Its fee and term were not found.

**No GAMR licence for outdoor or vehicle screens was found**, and no rule coordinating GAMR with the municipal ministry (MOMAH) on screen content. The likely split is:

- **GAMR** licenses the business selling ad time and enforces content standards.
- **MOMAH/Balady and Traffic** permit the physical sign.
- **Sector regulators** pre-approve regulated products.

This is an inference from the scope of GAMR's e-service (confidence: medium).

The Mawthooq licence (SAR 15,000 for three years) is for individuals advertising on social media ([Saudipedia](https://saudipedia.com/en/what-is-the-mawthooq-license)). It matters only if the startup pays creators. A unified Media Law has been drafted and consulted on since 2020. **Its enactment could not be confirmed.** A claimed effective date of 30 December 2026 comes only from a personal LinkedIn profile ([LinkedIn](https://sa.linkedin.com/in/sultan-khallaf)).

GAMR's content rules ban material that conflicts with national identity or social values, exploits children or bullies ([Khaleej Times](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/world/gulf/no-bullying-misleading-content-saudi-authority-issues-new-media-regulations?amp=1)). Tobacco promotion is banned on every channel ([Okaz](https://www.okaz.com.sa/specialized-corners/na/2213015)). Sector regulators add their own approvals:

- **SFDA** licenses food ads through its "Iʿlan" system ([SFDA](https://www.sfda.gov.sa/ar/eservices/358)).
- **SAMA** requires Arabic as the primary language, plus APR and VAT-inclusive pricing, in financial ads ([SAMA](https://www.sama.gov.sa/ar-sa/LawsRegulations/ConsumerProtectionRules/Rules_for_Advertising_Products_and_Services_Provided_by_Financial_Institutions-AR.pdf)).
- **REGA** has licensed real-estate ads since 1 May 2026 ([Gulf Construction Online](https://gulfconstructiononline.com/Article/464369/Understanding_REGA’s_new_marketing_rules)).

Reported ad penalties run from **SAR 100 to SAR 500,000**. In cover-up (tasattur) cases involving foreign advertisers they reach **SAR 5m and up to five years' prison** ([Okaz](https://www.okaz.com.sa/news/local/2212992)). Every enforcement case found involved individuals and influencers. None involved outdoor operators.

### The municipal rules fit stickers and taxi roofs, not screen fleets

MOMAH's advertising board requirements (1443H/2021, published 2022) define boards to include electronic screens "attached to a facility **or a vehicle**". They list buses, taxis, light transport vehicles and goods trucks. They require **General Traffic Directorate approval before any vehicle licence** ([MOMAH 2022 PDF](https://momah.gov.sa/sites/default/files/2022-03/%D8%A7%D8%B4%D8%AA%D8%B1%D8%A7%D8%B7%D8%A7%D8%AA%20%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%84%D9%88%D8%AD%D8%A7%D8%AA%20%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AF%D8%B9%D8%A7%D8%A6%D9%8A%D8%A9.pdf); [Istitlaa](https://istitlaa.ncc.gov.sa/ar/Municipality/momra/paintings/Pages/default.aspx)). Within that scope, the text sets three limits:

- **Private and company vehicles:** "a sticker ad only, no protrusion, on the two sides only".
- **Taxi roofs:** a **3-D roof sign (الإعلان المجسم)** with concealed fixing, in approved model dimensions. The extract does not say whether a digital unit qualifies.
- **Fixed roadside screens** (for comparison only): a MOMAH concession booklet caps brightness at **1,000 candela** ([Furas booklet](https://furas.momah.gov.sa/sites/default/files/%D9%83%D8%B1%D8%A7%D8%B3%D8%A9%20%20%D8%A7%D8%B3%D8%AA%D8%AB%D9%85%D8%A7%D8%B1%20%D9%84%D9%88%D8%AD%D8%A7%D8%AA%20%D8%AF%D8%B9%D8%A7%D8%A6%D9%8A%D8%A9%20_compressed.pdf)).

Combined with **Cabinet Decision 590's owner-only rule for commercial vehicles**, the text leaves no routine path for a third-party LED truck or a third-party screen on a delivery vehicle. MOMAH updated its board conditions in August 2025 ([Aleqt](https://www.aleqt.com/2025/08/13/article_2765245.html)), but no vehicle-specific change was found.

On procedure and fees:

- The Balady operational sign licence is online-only and takes 1–10 days. It requires a CR and an **investment contract** ([MOMAH e-service](https://momah.gov.sa/en/e-services/issuing-operational-license)). That requirement ties outdoor licences to municipal investment deals rather than open permits for each vehicle.
- A separate instrument sets sign fees ([qanoonsa](https://qanoonsa.com/p/503902/)), but no vehicle rate was retrievable.
- Placing an ad board or sticker on a vehicle without a licence is a municipal violation charged **per vehicle**, at about **SAR 500–2,500**, doubled on repeat ([MOMAH penalties schedule, 2026](https://momah.gov.sa/sites/default/files/2026-03/jdwl%20aljzaat%20%60n%20almkhalfat%20albldyt%20-%20m%60dl4.pdf)).

### Riyadh sells taxi and bus inventory as a concession

Riyadh shows how the market works in practice. Remat Al-Riyadh, the Amanah's development arm, tendered ads on public taxis and buses through the Furas platform ([Al-Eqtisadiah](https://www.aleqt.com/2024/05/30/article_2730781.html)). **Fadn Advertising Agency (wholly owned by Al Arabia) won two two-year contracts to build, operate and maintain ad boards on 500 taxis and buses, worth about SAR 6.84m and SAR 5.8m.** The second was signed 30 May 2024 ([Saudi Exchange](https://www.saudiexchange.sa/wps/portal/saudiexchange/newsandreports/issuer-news/issuer-announcements/issuer-announcements-details/?locale=enar&anCat=91&anId=77778&cs=4071); [Argaam](https://www.argaam.com/ar/article/articledetail/id/1697184)). The format is stickers. If the terms run as reported, they end around mid-2026, so a re-tender may be under way; that is unverified.

In Jeddah, **MediaGo, with Jeddah Transport Company, says it is the first licensed operator of screens on moving vehicles**. No regulator has confirmed the claim ([Campaign ME](https://campaignme.com/mediago-mobile-dooh-platform-jeddah/)). No Saudi operator of roaming LED trucks with a public licence trail was found.

### Taxi cabins are open; exteriors need TGA, Traffic and the Amanah

The Transport General Authority (TGA) regulates taxis and ride-hailing. Its taxi and ride-hailing regulation **lets the carrier advertise inside the vehicle, including on an electronic screen**, if the screen does not draw the driver's attention or affect passenger safety, and subject to the municipal ad rules ([TGA regulation 1538](https://www.tga.gov.sa/Regulations/Read/1538); [Umm Al-Qura](https://www.uqn.gov.sa/details?p=23377)). The earlier ride-hailing rules required the **driver's consent** ([Istitlaa](https://istitlaa.ncc.gov.sa/ar/Transportation/tga/TGARIDE/Pages/default.aspx)). Taxis must also carry a TGA-specified external identity of colour and approved stickers. Unapproved stickers draw TGA fines of about SAR 400–500 (amounts unconfirmed for taxis).

Traffic adds its own limits. The Traffic Law treats unauthorised body additions and unauthorised light devices as violations, with **impoundment until removed** ([Traffic Law, BOE](https://laws.boe.gov.sa/BoeLaws/Laws/LawDetails/85364e57-c01e-41ba-8def-a9a700f183e9/1?csrt=10181980062366402065)). An exterior LED screen without Traffic approval is therefore exposed on two fronts. In 2017 Traffic threatened **SAR 3,000 fines** on a Jeddah firm that paid car owners to carry rear-window stickers ([Al-Mowaten](https://www.almowaten.net/2017/11/3-%D8%A2%D9%84%D8%A7%D9%81-%D8%B1%D9%8A%D8%A7%D9%84-%D8%B9%D9%82%D9%88%D8%A8%D8%A9-%D9%88%D8%B6%D8%B9-%D9%85%D9%84%D8%B5%D9%82%D8%A7%D8%AA-%D9%85%D8%AE%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%81%D8%A9-%D8%B9%D9%84%D9%89/)). That is exactly the "pay drivers to carry ads" model, applied to private cars.

### Delivery vehicles must show their own brand

Saudi delivery rules point the same way as the sign rules:

- **TGA restricts freelance delivery to Saudis.** Non-Saudi couriers must work through licensed light-transport companies, in a TGA-approved uniform ([Saudi Gazette](https://saudigazette.com.sa/article/641803)).
- **TGA stopped issuing new motorcycle delivery licences for app orders in October 2024** ([Argaam](https://www.argaam.com/en/article/articledetail/id/1760983)).
- **Since 1 July 2025, the Balady home-delivery permit requires the establishment's name or trademark to be clearly displayed on the vehicle** ([Sabq](https://sabq.org/article/TdI2UJ6); [Okaz](https://www.okaz.com.sa/news/local/2202338)).

Together with the owner-only rule, the delivery box is effectively **reserved for the fleet's or platform's own brand**. A screen that sells that brand, or its co-marketing partners, is arguable. A rotating third-party ad network is not. Saudi freelancers with their own TGA-approved vehicles are the only riders who could contract directly, and their boxes face the same advertising rules.

### Company set-up runs through MISA unless the owners are all GCC

A wholly GCC-owned company may register a CR without a licence from the Ministry of Investment (MISA). Any non-GCC shareholder, as is common in VC-backed startups, means a MISA licence first. Formation vendors report that 100% foreign ownership is possible for most advertising services, with MISA fees of SAR 2,000–10,000 and 2–4 weeks' processing ([NobleCore](https://noblecoreksa.com/marketing-advertising-agency-saudi-arabia/); [AstroLabs](https://insight.astrolabs.com/how-to-get-a-misa-license-in-saudi-arabia-2025-guide-for-foreign-businesses)). These are vendor figures, not checked against MISA's negative list. The sequence is MISA (if needed), then a CR with the advertising activity, then the GAMR agency licence, then municipal and traffic permits for each vehicle or concession. Nitaqat Saudization quotas apply to staff, and no advertising-specific quota was found.

## Recommended route: Kuwait taxis and vans first, Saudi through partners

**Kuwait, phase 0 (now to Q1 2027): papers before hardware.**

- Incorporate a WLL or apply to KDIPA, with a MOCI advertising activity.
- Register with the Municipality as an advertising company.
- Send three written questions:
  1. To the Municipality's Advertisement Licensing Section: which category and fee apply to (a) third-party static ads on delivery-bike boxes, (b) screens on a light van, and (c) a roof unit that cycles still images.
  2. To the GTD and Technical Inspection: an approval for one prototype of each hardware type, and a written vehicle-class ruling so the van falls outside the truck ban.
  3. To the Ministry of Information's General Administration for Media Regulation: whether a DOOH operator needs an Art. 34 licence, raised while the Executive Regulations are still being drafted.

**Kuwait, phase 1: taxis.**

- Sign investor–owner contracts with taxi companies that hold ad licences.
- File TR133 licences and post the KD 1,000 guarantee.
- Fit 25×40 cm rear-cabin video screens and static 40×120 cm roof units.

This is the only format where the text already allows third-party moving content.

**Kuwait, phase 1b: light vans.**

- Run static frames or blank screens while driving.
- Play full video only while parked on mall or event land, under landowner contracts and occasion licences.
- Stay out of residential areas and off bridges.
- Use battery power and no external audio.

**Kuwait, phase 2: delivery fleets.**

- Sign B2B media leases with unbranded third-party logistics fleets.
- Start with the fleet's own or co-branded static content under the KD 40 line.
- Move to third-party static ads only after a written Municipality ruling.
- Keep the mandatory rear light and reflectors visible.

**Kuwait, April–October 2027:** obtain the Media Law licence and add ad labels and records of each advertiser's licence.

**Saudi Arabia: partner rather than build.**

- Set up MISA (if needed), a CR and the GAMR agency licence.
- Lead with **in-cabin taxi and ride-hailing screens**, which TGA expressly allows, through taxi operators with driver consent.
- For exteriors, partner with or bid against the concession holders: Fadn/Al Arabia through Remat and Furas in Riyadh, and JTC/MediaGo in Jeddah.
- Do not deploy third-party LED trucks or delivery boxes until MOMAH, TGA and Traffic confirm a route in writing. Without that, the owner-only rule makes every unit a per-vehicle violation.

## Conclusion

The two countries look alike from a distance: municipal licence, traffic approval, media regulator. Up close they are opposites. Kuwait regulates **formats**: it says how big and how static each screen may be, and what remains is a narrow permitted space that a disciplined product can fit. Saudi Arabia regulates **who may carry an ad**: owner-only rules for commercial vehicles, branded delivery vehicles and concessioned taxi inventory. No hardware design solves that; only a partner or a concession does. The practical consequence is that Kuwait favours building the operating company and the hardware, while Saudi Arabia favours building relationships with whoever already holds the concessions.

Two points cut against the original idea of a rider-paid video network. First, the most valuable surface, a video screen visible to street traffic, is the one both countries restrict most. The most permissive surface is inside the taxi cabin, where the audience is passengers, not the street. Second, the rider is never the right counterparty. Residence law in Kuwait and TGA rules in Saudi Arabia both route the money through licensed fleets. The business is best framed as a licensed media owner leasing space from fleets and concession holders. In Kuwait, licences issued now may need re-papering under the Media Law within a year. That argues for a small, well-documented first fleet rather than a fast, large one.
