# Fleet deals, not rider payments, unlock LED ads

Launching LED ad screens on delivery boxes and shop windows is legally workable in Kuwait and Egypt only if the platform contracts with **fleet operators and licensed advertising entities, not with individual riders**, and gets each screen licensed by both the advertising regulator and the traffic authority. In Kuwait, three rules shape the model. Delivery-motorcycle ads are reportedly limited to **the operator's own "identifying" ad, as static content, with General Traffic Department approval**. Commercial ads without a licence draw fines of **KD 3,000–5,000**. And **Article 19 of Residence Decree-Law 114/2024** makes paying another company's sponsored expatriate rider a ground for deportation. In Egypt, Traffic Law Art. 69 bis lets only **Interior-Ministry-approved joint-stock advertising companies with at least EGP 30 million issued capital** apply for third-party vehicle ads. Street-facing shop screens need a Law 208/2020 licence and must follow the **April 2026 lighting curfew**. The commercial terrain matters as much as the law. Flyby already runs this exact product with **Talabat in Dubai, Deliveroo in Abu Dhabi and Noon across the UAE**, and its chairman is Talabat's former CEO. Talabat now earns **3.4% of GMV from advertising**, so a rider-direct model would collide with platform kit rules and a well-connected incumbent. Much of the evidence comes from search-engine extracts, not full reads of the gazettes, and several sources conflict on fees and scope. Each section below rates its confidence and names what local counsel must verify before money is committed.

## Kuwait: three approvals stand between a box screen and the street

Kuwait is the hardest of the two priority markets for the rider-box product and a manageable one for shop windows. The decisive constraints are municipal, traffic and residency rules, and each points toward a B2B fleet structure.

### The Municipality licenses every screen, and the motorcycle rules are restrictive

**Kuwait Municipality (Baladia) licenses all outdoor advertising** through its Advertisement Licensing Section. The rules are in the Advertising Regulation: **Ministerial Resolution 599/2023, amended by Resolution 223/2024** ([lawskw.com](https://lawskw.com/section/%D8%A8%D9%84%D8%AF%D9%8A%D8%A9-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%83%D9%88%D9%8A%D8%AA-%D9%82%D8%B1%D8%A7%D8%B1-%D8%B1%D9%82%D9%85-599-%D9%84%D8%B3%D9%86%D8%A9-2023-%D8%A8%D8%B4%D8%A7%D9%86-%D9%84%D8%A7%D8%A6%D8%AD%D8%A9-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A7%D8%B9%D9%84%D8%A7%D9%86%D8%A7%D8%AA%D9%88%D8%B2%D8%A7%D8%B1%D8%A9-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AF%D9%88%D9%84%D8%A9-%D9%84%D8%B4%D8%A6%D9%88%D9%86-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A8%D9%84%D8%AF%D9%8A%D8%A9-%D9%82%D8%B1%D8%A7%D8%B1-%D8%B1%D9%82%D9%85-223-%D9%84%D8%B3%D9%86%D8%A9-2024-%D8%A8%D8%B4%D8%A7%D9%86-%D8%AA%D8%B9%D8%AF%D9%8A%D9%84-%D9%84%D8%A7%D8%A6%D8%AD%D8%A9-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A7%D8%B9%D9%84%D8%A7%D9%86%D8%A7%D8%AA); [Al-Rai](https://www.alraimedia.com/article/1670948/)). The Municipal Council then approved **"sweeping amendments" in late December 2025** ([Times Kuwait](https://timeskuwait.com/municipal-council-approves-sweeping-amendments-to-advertising-regulations/)). These define an advertising medium as any **fixed or mobile, traditional or electronic means, expressly including vehicles and electronic screens**. No gazette citation was found for the ministerial resolution that gives these amendments legal effect. The 2026 enforcement reporting suggests they are being applied anyway, but counsel must confirm the in-force text (confidence: high on scope, medium on in-force status).

For delivery motorcycles, the amended text as reported in October 2025 sets a fee of **KD 40/year "for the identifying advertisement of the activity only"**. The ad "may be a **fixed electronic panel with non-moving content**, provided approval of the Ministry of Interior – General Traffic Department is submitted", and lighting must be "self-contained and fixed" ([Al-Anba](https://www.alanba.com.kw/1329133); [Al-Rai](https://www.alraimedia.com/article/1751030/%D9%85%D8%AD%D9%84%D9%8A%D8%A7%D8%AA/%D8%A3%D8%AE%D8%A8%D8%A7%D8%B1-%D9%85%D8%AD%D9%84%D9%8A%D8%A9/%D9%84%D8%A7%D8%A6%D8%AD%D8%A9-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A5%D8%B9%D9%84%D8%A7%D9%86%D8%A7%D8%AA-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AC%D8%AF%D9%8A%D8%AF%D8%A9-%D8%BA%D8%B1%D8%A7%D9%85%D8%A7%D8%AA-%D8%AD%D8%AA%D9%89-5000-%D8%AF%D9%8A%D9%86%D8%A7%D8%B1-%D9%84%D9%84%D9%85%D8%AE%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%81%D9%8A%D9%86)). The same draft has a separate **KD 100/year category for commercial ads placed through advertising companies**, which must be registered or classified with the Municipality. The extract does not say whether that category covers motorcycles. Read literally, the motorcycle rule allows the fleet's own branding on a static screen, not a rotating third-party video network. That is the single biggest legal obstacle to the product in Kuwait (confidence: medium; the final adopted wording is unconfirmed).

The closest workable template is the **taxi "investment advertisement" licence (transaction TR133)**. It already permits third-party ads with moving TV screens on taxis at **KD 30/m² for moving-screen ads and KD 20/m² per face for variable ads, plus a KD 1,000 guarantee**. It requires General Traffic Department approval and a copy of the vehicle registration ([Kuwait Municipality TR133](https://www.baladia.gov.kw/sites/ar/Pages/transactionsRequirements/TR133.aspx)). The same structure applies to public-transport vehicles ([e.gov.kw](https://e.gov.kw/sites/kgoarabic/Pages/Services/KM/ApplyingforIssuingAdvertisementLicensesonPublicTransportVehicles.aspx)). Kuwait therefore accepts moving third-party screens on licensed commercial vehicles in principle. The task is to get the Municipality to apply that logic to delivery cars, or to confirm in writing that the KD 100 "advertising company" route covers delivery motorcycles. Delivery cars are the more promising first target: the draft rules only require ads on **taxis and delivery vehicles to be confined to the vehicle's sides without protruding**, with designs approved by an accredited engineering office ([Arab Times](https://www.arabtimesonline.com/news/new-advertising-rules-introduced-in-kuwait-fines-fees-and-standards-updated/)). A box sticking out of a bike's rear rack may itself raise the protrusion question.

Penalties are steep and enforcement is active. The amended regulation sets graduated fines of **KD 100–5,000**, including **KD 3,000–5,000 for commercial ads without a licence** and KD 500–3,000 for ads promoting prohibited goods. Sanctions also include removal at the violator's expense, confiscation, and licence withdrawal after two warnings ([Times Kuwait](https://timeskuwait.com/municipality-finalizes-amendments-to-advertising-regulations-with-stricter-fines-and-new-licensing-rules/); [Times Kuwait](https://timeskuwait.com/municipal-council-approves-sweeping-amendments-to-advertising-regulations/)). Inspection campaigns ran through 2026, from **90 violations in Ahmadi in April** to a nationwide sweep in June, with the Municipality reporting a **45% drop in ad and shop violations by August** ([Arab Times](https://www.arabtimesonline.com/news/kuwait-municipality-targets-licensing-breaches-issues-90-violations-in-ahmadi-area/); [ARY News](https://arynews.tv/kuwait-cracks-down-on-shop-and-advertising-violations)). Whether a fine applies per screen or per campaign is unresolved. At fleet scale, the downside of launching unlicensed is existential.

### Traffic rules make lit boxes mandatory but video screens risky

The General Traffic Department actually **requires rear lighting and reflective strips on delivery boxes**. An October 2026 campaign cited riders for having *no* lights on their boxes ([Kuwait Times](https://kuwaittimes.com/article/51768/kuwait/traffic-department-lists-rules-for-delivery-motorcycle-riders/); [Kuwait Times](https://kuwaittimes.com/article/52132/kuwait/traffic-campaign-targets-delivery-motorcycle-violations/)). Illumination in itself is therefore not the problem. The risk lies in the *kind* of light and content. **Decree-Law 5/2025, in force since 22 April 2025**, amended the Traffic Law to penalise "illegal lights and other banned devices" and unauthorised modifications, with fines up to KD 200 for changes to external features ([Arab Times](https://www.arabtimesonline.com/news/kuwait-revamps-traffic-law-new-penalties-for-violations-and-changes-to-legal-procedures); [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/world/gulf/kuwait/kuwait-enacts-tougher-traffic-laws-and-harsher-penalties-for-violations-1.500022722)). In December 2023 the Traffic Department classed **unapproved electronic screens on vehicle windows as a "serious" violation, leading to traffic detention and impoundment** ([Al-Jarida](https://www.aljarida.com/article/47209)).

Enforcement increasingly targets the *company*. Riders face **impoundment of the motorcycle for up to 60 days**, and the MOI warned in October 2026 of administrative action against operating companies whose riders repeatedly offend ([Arab Times](https://www.arabtimesonline.com/news/kuwait-warns-delivery-riders-stay-off-sidewalks-or-lose-your-bike-for-60-days/); [Times Kuwait](https://timeskuwait.com/kuwait-tightens-traffic-controls-on-delivery-drivers/)). Fleet operators will therefore refuse any device that might trigger a stop unless it carries written GTD approval. No published GTD standard on screen brightness, size or box dimensions was found, so the approval criteria must be negotiated (confidence: high that GTD approval is required; low on what it will demand).

### The delivery sector runs on licensed fleets that sponsor their riders

Kuwaiti delivery is a fleet-licence business. Under MOI Ministerial Resolution 271/2020 as amended, **motorbikes are barred from ring roads and expressways**, and companies may add bikes only after owning a minimum number of cars ([Arab Times](https://www.arabtimesonline.com/news/new-regulations-for-delivery-vehicles-issued-by-kuwait-ministry/)). Reports conflict on the per-licence vehicle cap (30, 45 or 90) ([Times Kuwait](https://timeskuwait.com/kuwaits-delivery-sector-thrives-with-vehicle-limit-raised-to-90/)). Since 1 October 2022, the Traffic Department has required **company stickers on vehicles, uniforms, and riders on the employer company's residency** ([Al-Rai](https://www.alraimedia.com/article/1604983/محليات/أخبار-محلية/المرور-4-اشتراطات-لشركات-توصيل-الطلبات)). Delivery bikes were also banned from roads **11:00–16:00 from 1 June to 31 August 2026** ([Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/world/gulf/kuwait/kuwait-bans-delivery-bikes-during-peak-summer-heat-hours-1.500559094)), which removes the midday hours from summer inventory.

Platforms such as Talabat, Keeta and Jahez "rely on 3PL providers, which typically supply drivers and vehicles and are paid by platforms on a per-order basis" ([Kuwait Times](https://kuwaittimes.com/article/50539/kuwait/other-news/court-strikes-down-delivery-penalties-raising-fears-of-weak-enforcement/)). Kuwait's 2026 platform rules add commercial pressure on these fleets. Decision 109/2026 reportedly **caps consumer delivery fees at KD 1 and platform commissions at 10–17%**, and required contracts to be aligned by 1 September 2026. The Administrative Court annulled its Article 15 on a Talabat lawsuit ([Kuwait Times](https://kuwaittimes.com/article/46493/kuwait/other-news/new-rules-cap-delivery-fees-at-kd-1-limit-app-commissions/); [Kuwait Times](https://kuwaittimes.com/article/48886/kuwait/other-news/new-delivery-rules-put-platforms-delivery-companies-to-the-test/)). A related MOCI decision under the new **Digital Commerce Decree-Law 10/2026** reportedly caps everything a platform charges a restaurant, **including advertising fees and featured placement, at 17% of order value** ([Madhyamam](https://madhyamamonline.com/middle-east/kuwait/kuwait-issues-binding-rules-for-restaurant-food-delivery-platforms-1491805)). Sources disagree on whether 109/2026 is an MOI or MOCI instrument and on how it relates to "10/2026", so verify the text. The cap targets platform-to-restaurant fees and does not appear to reach a third-party OOH network. But squeezed per-order economics give 3PL fleets a real reason to welcome **new per-box revenue**, which is the commercial opening for this business.

### Paying riders directly breaches the residence law

The rider-payment idea fails in Kuwait as conceived. **Article 19 of Decree-Law 114/2024** on Foreigners' Residence bars foreign workers from working for anyone other than their sponsor ([Arab Times](https://www.arabtimesonline.com/news/expats-face-hefty-fines-under-kuwaits-new-residency-law)). Doing so is listed among the grounds for **deportation even with a valid residence permit** ([Arab Times](https://www.arabtimesonline.com/news/valid-residency-permit-wont-prevent-deportation-of-expats-in-these-3-cases)). The payer faces separate exposure for letting workers "work for others without a licence", which can stack with Labour Law penalties ([Haqq.ai](https://www.haqq.ai/blog/kuwait-recruiting-workers-without-need-crime)). Article 18's residency-trading offence carries **3–5 years' imprisonment and/or KD 5,000–10,000**, though one outlet says "up to 3 years" ([ARALF](https://www.arazzaqlaw.com/new-foreign-residency-law-issued-under-amiri-decree-no-114-2024/)).

The only worker-level legal route is the **PAM part-time permit**. It allows up to 4 hours a day for a second employer, requires the primary employer's NOC, is applied for by the second employer through Ashal, and costs KD 5–30 for 1–12 months ([Fragomen](https://www.fragomen.com/insights/kuwait-launch-of-part-time-work-permits.html); [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/world/gulf/kuwait/kuwait-approves-part-time-work-all-you-need-to-know-1.1703846396025)). It fits badly here. The "work" happens during the rider's main shift, every rider would need a sponsor NOC, and the payer would have to be a licensed employer. The defensible structure is a **B2B media-space lease with the fleet licence holder**. The ad company pays a per-box monthly fee or a revenue share to the 3PL, which owns the bikes, bears MOI compliance, and may pass bonuses to riders through its own payroll. Kuwaiti nationals riding as independents would be outside Article 19, but the 2022 company-residency rule shows the sector is overwhelmingly expatriate and sponsored (confidence: high).

### Shop windows are licensable but need a third-party-ad answer

Every facade sign or screen needs a Municipality licence through the e-service **"Issuing Licenses for Advertisements on Store Facades and Light Boxes"**. Fees are charged per m² per side, with a guarantee equal to half the fees ([e.gov.kw](https://e.gov.kw/sites/kgoenglish/Pages/Services/KM/IssuingLicensesForAdvertisementsOnStoreFacadesAndLightBoxes.aspx)). Under the amended rules:

- Electronic screens are classed as "moving" or "fixed", at **KD 75 or KD 100 per m² per side per year**, depending on the source.
- Facade ads go at the top of the facade, **at most 3 m high**.
- **Illuminated light boxes may not show moving or animated content.**
- Facade licences share the validity of the shop's commercial licence ([Arab Times](https://www.arabtimesonline.com/news/new-advertising-rules-introduced-in-kuwait-fines-fees-and-standards-updated/); [Times Kuwait](https://timeskuwait.com/municipality-finalizes-amendments-to-advertising-regulations-with-stricter-fines-and-new-licensing-rules/)).
- **Electronic billboards are banned in residential areas** ([Times Kuwait](https://timeskuwait.com/kuwait-tightens-street-advertising-rules-to-boost-road-safety-and-urban-aesthetics/)).
- The Municipality has enforced Ministry of Interior brightness limits since January 2026 ([Lexis ME](https://www.lexismiddleeast.com/news/2026-01-26_17/en)).
- September 2025 inspections focused **"particularly on rooftop and screen displays"** ([Arab Times](https://www.arabtimesonline.com/news/nine-shops-penalized-for-advertising-violations-in-the-latest-inspections/)).

Two questions remain open. Because the facade licence is tied to the shop's own commercial licence, it may not cover third-party "investment" advertising. And no rule was found that exempts screens mounted inside the glass and facing the street. Assume these need licensing and ask the Municipality for a written classification (confidence: high that a licence is needed; low on the third-party category and on screens behind glass). The payment to the shop owner is an ordinary private lease of window space, and **Kuwait has no VAT** ([TaxinME](https://www.taxinme.com/articles/kuwait-vat-status-2026.php)).

### Content passes through four regulators, and a new media law arrives in 2027

Content is regulated separately from the sign licence. The **Media Regulation Decree-Law 102/2026** was published in Kuwait Al-Youm on 4 October 2026 and takes effect about April 2027. It requires licensing and **explicit labelling of advertising and promotional content**, bans misleading content, and sets standards on religion, public order and children ([Al Tamimi](https://www.tamimi.com/law-update/the-technology-issue-2026/articles/kuwaits-new-media-law-a-comprehensive-overhaul-of-the-media-regulatory-framework/); [GLA & Co](https://www.glaco.com/blog/kuwaits-new-media-law-one-framework-for-print-broadcast-digital-media-and-influencers/)). Unlicensed advertising reportedly carries **KD 1,000–50,000**, with doubling for companies reported only by secondary sources ([Al-Dostour](https://al-dostour.com/kuwait-media-law-2026/)). Whether its executive regulations will require a Ministry of Information licence for DOOH operators is unknown, so treat April–October 2027 as a re-licensing checkpoint.

Sector rules already apply now:

- **Health:** Ministerial Decision 9/2025 requires prior MOH licensing for all ads for pharmacies, medicines, supplements and cosmetic preparations ([Al Tamimi](https://www.tamimi.com/our-knowledge/publications/eyes-on-2026/articles/kuwait-tightens-public-health-and-digital-advertising-oversight-what-beauty-operators-brands-and-influencers-need-to-know-through-2026/)). MOH has closed 33 cosmetic clinics over ad violations ([Arab Times](https://www.arabtimesonline.com/news/33-plastic-surgery-clinics-closed-by-ministry-of-health-for-violating-advertising-regulation)).
- **Promotions:** MOCI sales-promotion ads must display the licence number and run for at most one month ([e.gov.kw MOCI](https://e.gov.kw/sites/kgoenglish/Pages/Services/MOCI/LicenseApplicationPublishingAnnouncement.aspx)).
- **Real estate:** promotions need MOCI approval ([Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/world/gulf/kuwait/kuwait-to-require-licences-for-influencer-ads-under-new-media-law-1.500260303)).
- **Language:** a pre-2026 legal overview says ads must be in Arabic and must not promote prohibited products ([Practical Law](https://uk.practicallaw.thomsonreuters.com/w-012-8167)).

### Company, tax, data and product rules are routine but have traps

**Company and tax.**

- **Ownership:** foreigners may hold **at most 49% of a WLL without KDIPA approval**. KDIPA can license up to **100% foreign ownership** under Law 116/2013 ([PwC](https://www.pwc.com/m1/en/tax/documents/doing-business-guides/dbiku.pdf); [KDIPA](https://kdipa.gov.kw/)). Whether advertising is on KDIPA's negative list was not confirmed. An "ad-tech platform" framing may fit KDIPA's priorities better than "publicity agency", but that is an inference.
- **Corporate tax:** foreign corporate shareholders pay **15% CIT** on their share of Kuwait-source profit. GCC-owned shares face **1% Zakat** ([Commenda](https://www.commenda.io/kuwait/corporate-tax-rates); [Al Tamimi](https://www.tamimi.com/our-knowledge/publications/through-the-looking-glass/kuwait-2026-the-strategic-shifts-investors-need-to-watch-now/)).
- **DMTT:** the 15% minimum top-up tax applies only to groups with revenue of €750m or more ([KPMG](https://kpmg.com/kw/en/insights/2025/01/domestic-minimum-top-up-tax.html)).
- **VAT:** none.

**Data privacy.** Kuwait has no general data-protection statute. Sources conflict on whether CITRA's Data Privacy Protection Regulation 26/2024 reaches a non-telecom ad company. Design to its standards regardless: explicit consent, deletion when the purpose is fulfilled, **24-hour breach notification to CITRA**, and transfers only to adequate jurisdictions ([ConsentStack](https://www.consentstack.io/regulations/kw-dppr)).

**Product conformity.** LED boxes and window units will likely need **PAI's KUCAS route**: a Technical Evaluation Report and a Technical Inspection Report from an approved certification body before shipment ([Intertek](https://www.intertek.com.hk/government/coc-kuwait)). Low-voltage products on GSO "List 2" need a **Gulf Type Examination Certificate (G-Mark)** ([Intertek](https://www.intertek.com/government/export-import/regulatory-news-updates/news-kuwait/regulatory-updates-gulf-tr-electrical/)). Whether LED display panels are on the regulated list was not confirmed. CITRA type approval for 4G modules was not researched and should be checked.

## Egypt: an EGP 30 million gatekeeper controls vehicle ads

Egypt has clearer statutory text than Kuwait and an existing local market of mobile screen operators. For a foreign startup it also has two hard structural barriers: who may apply for vehicle-ad licences, and who may own an advertising agency.

### Traffic Law Art. 69 bis licenses every vehicle ad through approved companies

Since Law 161/2021, **Art. 69 bis of Traffic Law 66/1973 requires a licence from the competent traffic department for any ad on a vehicle**. The fee is capped at **EGP 10,000**, with bands set by the executive regulations, and the licence lasts up to **3 years** ([Ahram Gate](https://gate.ahram.org.eg/News/3082998.aspx); [Dostor](https://www.dostor.org/3615067)). The decisive clause is Art. 69 bis 2: **only Interior-Ministry-approved advertising companies may apply, on behalf of vehicle owners on their registers, and each must be a joint-stock company with at least EGP 30 million in issued capital** ([Youm7](https://www.youm7.com/story/2022/1/14/ما-هى-الشركات-التى-يحق-لها-الإعلان-على-المركبات-قانون/5615118); [El Balad](https://www.elbalad.news/6208306)). Companies and shops advertising their **own name or activity on their own vehicles are exempt**. That is why platform-branded boxes are lawful and third-party ads are not. Violations cost **EGP 1,000–5,000 per violation, multiplied by the number of violations** ([El Balad](https://www.elbalad.news/5399695)).

In 2025 the Interior Ministry addressed delivery and ride-hailing drivers directly after complaints about fines over logos. It confirmed that placing an ad on a vehicle without a licence is banned and can lead to **cancellation of the vehicle's operating licence**, and that owners may regularise by obtaining **"licences to advertise for others"** ([El Watan](https://www.elwatannews.com/news/details/8184906); [Youm7](https://www.youm7.com/7226268)). A Ministerial Decree 889/2024 amending Art. 225 of the executive regulations is reported but unverified ([Egypt Telegraph](https://www.egypttelegraph.com/article/201488/بعد-شكوى-سائقي-التوصيل،-الداخلية-وضع-الإعلانات)). No source addresses motorcycles specifically or illuminated screens on vehicles. A box fixed to a bike is very likely "on the vehicle" (confidence: high on the licence requirement, medium on the EGP 30m threshold, low on motorcycle and screen specifics).

For a startup, the practical choice is between **capitalising a JSC at EGP 30m+ and seeking Interior approval, or partnering with an already-approved advertising company** that files licences on its behalf. Unlicensed operation is not a realistic option. The fines fall on riders, and cancellation of a bike's licence ends a rider's income.

### Delivery-sector rules are in flux

Reports conflict on the Traffic Law overhaul. A February 2026 item said it was withdrawn; a July 2026 item said it was still being debated, with motorcycle-licensing fines of up to EGP 100,000 ([Parlmany](https://www.parlmany.com/News/7/231301/النواب-يناقشون-قانون-المرور-الجديد)). Motorcycle licence fees were cut to **EGP 500 from 1 October 2026** ([Youm7](https://www.youm7.com/story/2026/9/30/تخفيض-رسوم-إصدار-وتجديد-رخص-القيادة-والسيارات-والدراجات-النارية-بالمرور/7562546)), so more delivery bikes are being formally registered. That helps, because Art. 69 bis licences attach to registered vehicles.

### Shop windows fall under Law 208/2020 and its lighting curfew

**Law 208/2020 on Advertisements on Public Roads** defines an "advertisement or sign" as any visual product carrying an advertising, promotional or informational message *to road users*. Art. 3 bars placing one without a licence from the "competent body", meaning the local unit (hay), the NUCA city authority in new cities, or the Roads & Bridges Authority ([cc.gov.eg](https://www.cc.gov.eg/legislation_single?id=406929); [Youm7](https://www.youm7.com/story/2026/2/15/مش-كل-إعلان-محتاج-ترخيص-اعرف-الحالات-المستثناة-بالقانون/7303342)). The scheme works as follows:

- The fee is capped at **EGP 10,000**, and licences last up to 3 years ([Masrawy](https://www.masrawy.com/news/news_egypt/details/2020/12/10/1928342/رسوم-10-آلاف-جنيه-10-معلومات-عن-قانون-تنظيم-الإعلانات-على-الطرق-العامة-ضوابط-وعقوبات-)).
- Executive Decree 990/2022 gives the authority **60 days to decide, and silence counts as conditional deemed approval** ([Decree 990/2022 text](https://ahmedazimelgamel.blogspot.com/2024/01/990-2022-208-2020.html)).
- Third-party commercial ads fall under none of the Art. 7 exemptions.
- Violators pay a fine of **2–3 times the cost of the works, double fees**, and removal costs ([Youm7](https://www.youm7.com/story/2024/10/29/الغرامة-والإزاله-عقوبة-وضع-الإعلانات-المخالفة-بالطرق-العامة/6755370)).

No source found Law 208 applied to moving vehicles, so the bike product sits under the Traffic Law, while the shop product sits under Law 208.

The operational shock is the **April 2026 NAPRA lighting rules**:

- Illuminated billboards, digital screens and shopfront lighting must be **off from midnight to 5 a.m.** on main and internal roads outside tourist zones.
- Ahram Online reports that all illuminated signage, **including digital screens and shopfront displays, must also be off 7 a.m.–5 p.m.** Signs with sensors that auto-dim to **37.5% by day and 4% at night** are exempt ([Ahram Online](https://english.ahram.org.eg/NewsContentP/1/567028/Egypt/Egypt-introduces-new-billboard-lighting-regulation.aspx)).
- Lexis ME describes the daytime ban as covering only spotlights, backlit panels and MUPI units ([Lexis ME](https://www.lexismiddleeast.com/news/2026-05-01_55/en)).

If Ahram's reading is correct, a shop screen without auto-dimming would be legal only from 5 p.m. to midnight. Specifying ambient-light sensors with 37.5%/4% dimming profiles is therefore a design requirement, not a nice-to-have. The March 2026 emergency shutdown of illuminated roadside ads during the energy crisis shows the state can switch inventory off at short notice ([EgyptToday](https://www.egypttoday.com/Article/1/146802/Egypt-sets-new-controls-on-roadside-advertising-lighting-to-balance)). Whether screens behind the glass on private property are "on public roads" is the central unresolved question for the shop product (confidence: medium).

### Content, company structure and tax

**Content.** Consumer Protection Law 181/2018 bans misleading ads and requires **Arabic, or Arabic plus another language** ([Chambers](https://practiceguides.chambers.com/practice-guides/advertising-marketing-2025/egypt); [Lexology](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=0dea9950-f50e-4666-952b-d490ab77432f)). Health products need a licence under **Law 206/2017**, and advertising without one carries **at least one month in prison and EGP 50,000–100,000** ([Youm7](https://www.youm7.com/story/2026/6/22/ضوابط-بالقانون-للإعلان-عن-المنتجات-الصحية-والحبس-للمخالفين/7455306)). SCMR Decision 9/2025 requires EDA registration and approval numbers to be shown on medical-product ads, and also covers food ads ([Masrawy](https://www.masrawy.com/news/news_egypt/details/2025/2/4/2721608/-الأعلى-للإعلام-يصدر-ضوابط-إعلانات-المستحضرات-الطبية-والمنتجات-الغذائية-والمسابقات)). The National Food Safety Authority has its own food-ad controls ([Al Mal](https://almalnews.com/2100348/)), which matters because F&B brands are the natural buyers of delivery-box inventory.

**Company structure.** The structural trap is **Ministerial Decree 962/2012**, which reportedly requires advertising and marketing agencies to register in the **Commercial Agents' Registry** ([Mondaq](https://webiis08.mondaq.com/advertising-marketing-branding/1426214/advertising-agencies-in-egypt-governing-laws-and-restrictions)). **Commercial agency is reserved for companies wholly owned by Egyptians** ([DLA Piper](https://intelligence.dlapiper.com/global-expansion-corporate/?c=EG)). If both points hold, a foreign-owned LLC set up through GAFI cannot itself run the advertising activity. Combined with the EGP 30m JSC requirement, the likely structure is a foreign-owned tech/hardware LLC plus an Egyptian-owned or partner advertising entity (confidence: medium; this is counsel's first question).

**Tax.**

- **CIT** is 22.5% ([KPMG](https://kpmg.com/eg/en/services/tax/investment-in-egypt/business-taxation.html)).
- **Advertising moved from 20% stamp tax to 14% VAT under Law 3/2022** ([PwC](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/egypt/corporate/other-taxes)).
- **Withholding** under Art. 59 of Law 91/2005 applies to payments above EGP 300: 1% for supply, 3% for services, 5% for commissions ([Andersen](https://eg.andersen.com/withholding-taxes-in-egypt)). How payments to shops and riders are classified (rent, service or commission) sets the rate.
- **Simplified regime:** a startup below EGP 20m turnover can elect **Law 6/2025** and pay **0.4%–1.5% of turnover** instead of CIT ([Andersen translation](https://eg.andersen.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/Law-No.-6-of-2025-edited.pdf)).
- **E-documents:** B2B e-invoicing and B2C e-receipts are mandatory ([VATit](https://vatit.com/?p=90013)).
- **Deductibility:** a 2026 guide says companies can deduct freelancer fees only when the payee's tax registration number appears on the invoice ([Enterprise](https://enterpriseam.com/egypt/2026/10/11/your-guide-to-registering-as-a-freelancer-in-egypt/)). Paying thousands of unregistered riders directly may therefore produce non-deductible costs.

### Riders can be paid, but through fleets is safer

Unlike Kuwait, Egypt has no sponsorship bar on paying Egyptian riders. Most Talabat riders are treated as independent contractors managed through external contractors. An ARIJ survey found **80% had no signed contract and 97% were not registered with social insurance** ([ARIJ](https://arij.net/investigations/delivery-workers-rights-egypt/en/); [Al-Monitor](https://www.al-monitor.com/originals/2022/04/egypt-gig-economy-workers-face-rough-ride)). **Labour Law 14/2025, in force 1 September 2025**, recognises platform work for the first time but leaves the detail to executive regulations. Its Art. 99 allows an employment relationship to be proved **"by any means"** ([Masaar](https://masaar.net/en/masaar-and-defense-release-research-paper-on-platform-work-in-egypts-new-labor-law)). An ad-hosting fee stays a rental or service arrangement as long as the startup does not dictate routes, hours or discipline. If it does, reclassification risk brings social-insurance contributions of **18.75% employer and 11% employee** on EGP 2,700–16,700 a month ([Business Today Egypt](https://www.businesstodayegypt.com/Article/1/6950/Egypt-s-Social-Insurance-Authority-announces-increase-in-wages-starting)). Payout rails are not a constraint: InstaPay allows **EGP 70,000 per transaction and 400,000 a month** ([Egypt Independent](https://www.egyptindependent.com/central-bank-of-egypt-modifies-transaction-limits-on-instapay-application/)). Paying the Art. 69 bis licence-holding company or the 3PL, which issues e-invoices, solves the deductibility and WHT problems together.

### Privacy and product deadlines have already passed

**Privacy.** Egypt's PDPL Executive Regulations (Decree 816/2025) were published on 2 November 2025 and opened a **one-year compliance window that closed about 1–2 November 2026**. Most controllers now need a **PDPC licence**, and cross-border transfers need a separate one ([CMS](https://cms.law/en/are/legal-updates/egypt-s-pdpl-executive-regulations-issued-one-year-compliance-countdown-begins); [Al Tamimi](https://www.tamimi.com/news/from-policy-to-practice-egypt-issues-executive-regulations-of-the-personal-data-protection-law)). A launch now has to be compliant from day one. Rider GPS traces are personal data, and camera analytics in public would add sensitive-data issues.

**Product conformity.** Any box with a 4G, Wi-Fi or Bluetooth module needs **NTRA type approval and pre-import IMEI registration** ([NTRA Intermediate TA Procedure 2026](https://www.tra.gov.eg/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/Doc-03-Intermediate-Type-Approval-Scheme-Procedure-Version1-2026.pdf)). Choosing an already-approved module such as Quectel's EG915U simplifies this ([Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20240131682406/en/)). Imports may also fall under **GOEIC factory/brand registration (Decree 43/2016)**, which lists "LED lamps, except for vehicles" ([GTAI](https://www.gtai.de/gtai-de/trade/zoll/aegyptische-standards-und-registrierungspflichten-fuer-hersteller-546126.pdf)).

### Local competitors already sell bike screens in Cairo

**Street Level** markets digital screens on trucks and delivery bikes, sold per play by area and time slot ([streetleveleg.com](https://streetleveleg.com/)). **Routen Media** runs a route-based DOOH network on vehicles across Cairo ([routenmedia.com](https://routenmedia.com/)). Their licensing basis is not public. Either they work through an approved EGP 30m company or they operate in a grey zone. Asking them, or their advertisers, is the fastest way to learn actual enforcement practice, and either could be a partner or an acquisition target.

## Platforms and Flyby set the commercial terms, not just the law

The decisive private-law fact is that **Flyby is already the platform-endorsed operator of this exact product in the Gulf**. Talabat announced Flyby "Smart Delivery Boxes" with geo-targeted LED screens over 4G/5G for its Dubai fleet on 20 June 2025 ([Zawya](https://www.zawya.com/en/press-release/companies-news/talabat-partners-with-flyby-to-introduce-smart-delivery-boxes-in-dubai-yaxrd856)). Deliveroo piloted them in Abu Dhabi with **"still images with creative guidelines to prioritise road safety"** ([Zawya](https://www.zawya.com/en/press-release/companies-news/deliveroo-transforms-bikes-into-digital-billboards-in-abu-dhabi-pilot-with-flyby-506339)). Noon put them on hundreds of riders, **billing advertisers per minute of screen time** ([Menabytes](https://www.menabytes.com/noon-digital-ads-fleet/)). In November 2026, Yango Ads added the inventory to its UAE offering ([invidis](https://invidis.com/news/2026/11/mobile-dooh-yango-ads-and-flyby-bring-mobile-dooh-advertising-to-uae-streets/)). Flyby's chairman is **former Talabat CEO Tomaso Rodriguez**, appointed ahead of a Series A and "global expansion" ([Zawya](https://www.zawya.com/en/press-release/people-in-the-news/flyby-appoints-former-talabat-ceo-tomaso-rodriguez-as-chairman-ahead-of-series-a-and-global-expansion-353819)). No Flyby deployment was found in Kuwait or Egypt. Kuwait is Talabat's home market and an obvious next step for Flyby, so the founder's window there is real but probably short.

Platforms have both the motive and the means to block a rider-direct entrant. Talabat's advertising revenue reached **3.4% of GMV in Q2 2026**, and its digital ad vertical drove H1 revenue up 19% to US$2.2bn ([AGBI](https://www.agbi.com/food-drink/2026/08/talabat-raises-guidance-as-revenue-rises/)). Talabat's Kuwait job postings refer to **rider kit procurement and "compliance with rider equipment"** ([Delivery Hero careers](https://careers.deliveryhero.com/job/supervisor-riders-in-salmiya-kuwait-jid-2357)). It enforces conduct rules through its "logistics partners" ([Khaleej Times](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/world/gulf/viral-video-talabat-rider-seen-eating-food-from-delivery-box-company-responds)). Deliveroo bars merchants from putting other channels' branding in order bags ([Deliveroo](https://merchants.deliveroo.com/legal/policies?)). Talabat has also used exclusivity aggressively before ([Hotelier ME](https://hoteliermiddleeast.com/operations/front-of-house/food-beverage/116722-coronavirus-talabat-only-accepting-new-restaurants-that-join-exclusively)).

No rider-terms text banning third-party box ads was found. Even so, a rider who swaps a Talabat-standard box for a third-party screen box would very likely breach the 3PL's kit obligations and risk deactivation, and the startup could face inducement-of-breach claims. Riders who work for several apps on one box face conflicting branding rules. The realistic channels are:

- platform deals that complement or displace Flyby where it is absent;
- **3PL fleets**, which own the vehicles and need revenue under Kuwait's commission caps;
- independent restaurant, grocery, pharmacy and courier fleets with unbranded boxes.

**Product and insurance.** Hardware is not a moat. Three-sided LED delivery boxes cost about **US$1,500 per unit** from Chinese vendors ([Leeman LED](https://www.leemanled.com/takeaway-outdoor-food-delivery-box-led-display-screens-with-motorcycles)). The compliance stack and the heat engineering are the real work. Lithium packs fall under **GSO IEC 62133-2:2021** ([GSO](https://www.gso.org.sa/store/standards/GSO:789526/GSO%20IEC%2062133-2:2021?lang=en)) and need transport compliance under IATA guidance ([IATA](https://www.iata.org/contentassets/05e6d8742b0047259bf3a700bc9d42b9/lithium-battery-guidance-document.pdf)). Suppliers themselves warn that a screen box **affects vehicle-modification disclosure, goods-in-transit, theft and public-liability cover** ([Dynamo LED](https://dynamo-led-displays.co.uk/delivery-box-outdoor-led-takeaway-box-display/)). An undisclosed modification could let a motor insurer dispute an accident claim. Fleet contracts allow motor policies to be endorsed centrally; a rider-direct model leaves each rider's cover uncertain. Abroad, regulators have targeted novel mobile digital ads before: the NYC TLC fought Vugo's taxi screens ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vugo_(company))), New York banned digital billboards on vessels ([NY Senate](https://nysenate.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2019/brad-hoylman-sigal/governor-signs-hoylman-gottfried-legislation-ban)), and London roadside guidance bans moving images ([Camden](https://camdocs.camden.gov.uk/CMWebDrawer/Record/6523993/file/document)). Still images, no animation while moving, and auto-dimming should be the baseline everywhere.

Dubai proves that regulators can accept moving LED box ads when a major app holds the permit. It is also where competition is fiercest. Bahrain's simple per-m² permit makes it the easiest small GCC test market.

## A compliant structure: lease media space from fleets, license through approved entities

Across every market, the evidence points to one structure. The company **owns the hardware and the advertiser relationship, leases display rights from licensed fleets and shops, and holds or partners for the advertising licences**. It never directly pays sponsored expatriate riders. In Kuwait that means a WLL or KDIPA entity registered as an advertising company with the Municipality. It signs per-box lease or revenue-share agreements with 3PL licence holders, and those agreements allocate Municipality licensing, GTD approval, insurance endorsement and removal-on-demand duties. In Egypt it means pairing a tech/hardware company with an Egyptian-owned advertising entity and either capitalising to EGP 30m for Interior approval or contracting an already-approved vehicle-ad company. Shop owners are paid under simple space-licence agreements, with WHT in Egypt.

Product rules should be set to the strictest regulator rather than the most permissive:

- **Still images only**, no animation or video while the vehicle moves (matching Kuwait's "non-moving content" and Deliveroo's pilot).
- Mandatory **ambient-light auto-dimming** (Egypt's 37.5%/4% profile) and Kuwait's existing rear lights and reflective strips.
- **No cameras or device sniffing.** Audience estimates come from consented GPS traces joined to third-party traffic data.
- An **Arabic-first creative pre-clearance desk** that checks health, food, promotions and real-estate permits by sector.

Before any capital is committed, local counsel should resolve six questions, in this order:

1. Kuwait's in-force motorcycle rule ("identifying ad only / non-moving") and whether the KD 100 advertising-company category or a TR133-style licence covers third-party box screens.
2. The GTD's technical conditions for box screens.
3. Kuwait's licence category for third-party shop-window ads, and the treatment of screens behind glass.
4. Egypt's Decree 962/2012 ownership reservation.
5. Egypt's Art. 69 bis approved-company route and the motorcycle fee band.
6. Whether NAPRA's daytime ban covers in-window screens.

## Conclusion

The regulatory map changes what kind of company this has to be. Its regulated core is **licences and fleet contracts, not a rider marketplace with screens attached**. The founder's instinct to pay riders directly is the one element that is unlawful in Kuwait and the GCC, and tax-inefficient in Egypt. The same regulatory friction is also the moat: Kuwait's two-regulator approval for each vehicle, Egypt's EGP 30m approved-company gate, and the Municipality's active 2026 enforcement keep out casual entrants just as they keep out the founder. Whoever first secures written Municipality and GTD positions in Kuwait, and an Art. 69 bis licensing route in Egypt, will hold an asset that commodity hardware cannot copy.

Two timing facts sharpen the decision. Flyby has platform deals and a Talabat-connected chairman but no visible presence in Kuwait or Egypt, so the opening exists and is likely to close. Kuwait's Media Law 102/2026 will bring a new licensing layer around April–October 2027, so licences obtained now may need re-papering within a year. A sensible sequence is: shop windows plus delivery cars in Kuwait first, where the licence routes are clearest; motorcycles only after written GTD and Municipality sign-off; and Egypt entry through a partnership with an approved vehicle-ad company or an existing operator such as Street Level or Routen Media, not as a greenfield build.
